## What this is about

On Windows, files are stored on drives with letters, C: for the hard disk, D: or E: for a USB stick, and you look for them with the mouse in File Explorer. In a Linux terminal, there are neither letters nor a mouse. Still, thousands of files are stored there: programs, configuration settings, logs and your own texts and photos.

This chapter pursues one question: How does Linux organize its files, and how do you work with them when all you can do is type text? It shows how to find your way around with a few commands, how to view, create, copy, rename and delete files and where a Linux system keeps its programs and configuration. Then it looks underneath: What actually is a file name, why can a file have two names, and why is there no trash in the terminal? Finally, you find files again whose location you have forgotten and measure how much space they take up. Afterward, you can move around confidently in any Linux system.
