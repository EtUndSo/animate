The discipline of __electrical engineering__ is the engineering science that moves electric charges in a controlled way in order to transmit energy and process information. A power plant, a motor, a wireless network and a processor all rest on the same process: forces set charges in motion. Physics describes nature with measurable quantities and looks for the laws connecting them. The engineering sciences apply these laws, for example in components, circuits (connections of components) and power plants.

All materials consist of atoms. An __atom__ has a tiny nucleus of __protons__ and __neutrons__, around which __electrons__ move (Fig. [1](#abb-1)). Protons carry a positive charge, electrons a negative charge of exactly the same size, neutrons none. An atom contains as many protons as electrons; seen from outside, their charges cancel, and it is __neutral__. If it loses an electron, a positive charge is left over. An atom with a surplus charge is called an __ion__. Protons sit firmly in the nucleus and are 1836 times heavier than electrons, whereas outer electrons can be detached. That is why electrons are what moves in nearly all processes of electrical engineering.
