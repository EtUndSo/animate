## What this is about

A small rectangular battery is labeled 9 V. This figure is a __voltage__. It does not say how much energy the battery contains, but how much energy each unit of __charge__ gives up as it passes from one terminal to the other: 9 joules per coulomb. Charge is the property of certain particles of exerting and feeling electric forces. The joule is the unit of energy, the coulomb the unit of charge. The question of this chapter is: *Why is voltage always a difference between two points, and what does it do to a charge?*

The answer leads through two concepts. An __electric field__ determines, for every location, what force acts on a charge there. The __potential__ determines, for every location, how much energy per coulomb a charge has there relative to a freely chosen zero point, the __reference point__.
