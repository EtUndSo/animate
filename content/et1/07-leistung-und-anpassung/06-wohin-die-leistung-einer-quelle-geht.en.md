:::zweck
The source, too, has a resistance inside it. This stage sets up the balance and defines the efficiency.
:::

**The idea.** In the source, each coulomb receives the energy $U_\mathrm{q}$ in joules, because the chemistry pushes it against the field to the higher potential. It loses a part $U_\mathrm{Ri}$ in $R_\mathrm{i}$ as heat and gives off the rest $U_\mathrm{L}$ in the load (Fig. [6](#abb-6)). Then it is back at the starting point. All coulombs make the same round trip, so the ratio of the voltages determines which fraction of the power arrives.

:::abbildung et1/k7kreis 6
Left, the source at the load. Right, the potential of a charge on the round trip for $R_\mathrm{L}=16\,\Omega$, with B as the reference point. The source lifts it by 9 V, $R_\mathrm{i}$ takes 1 V, the load 8 V.
:::

:::definition Definition 5.1 (Power balance and efficiency)
<span class="anker" id="satz-5-1"></span>

In the circuit with $I=U_\mathrm{q}/(R_\mathrm{i}+R_\mathrm{L})$,

<span class="anker" id="gl-5-1"></span>
$$P_\mathrm{q}=U_\mathrm{q}\,I,\qquad P_\mathrm{v}=U_\mathrm{Ri}\,I=I^2R_\mathrm{i},\qquad P_\mathrm{L}=U_\mathrm{L}\,I=I^2R_\mathrm{L},\qquad P_\mathrm{q}=P_\mathrm{v}+P_\mathrm{L}. \tag{5.1}$$

The efficiency is the power used relative to the total power expended by the source:

<span class="anker" id="gl-5-2"></span>
$$\eta=\frac{P_\mathrm{L}}{P_\mathrm{q}}=\frac{I\,U_\mathrm{L}}{I\,U_\mathrm{q}}=\frac{U_\mathrm{L}}{U_\mathrm{q}}=\frac{R_\mathrm{L}}{R_\mathrm{L}+R_\mathrm{i}}=\frac{R_\mathrm{L}/R_\mathrm{i}}{1+R_\mathrm{L}/R_\mathrm{i}}. \tag{5.2}$$
:::

⁠**How to read it:** $P_\mathrm{q}$ is the power generated by the source, $P_\mathrm{v}$ the power loss in its internal resistance, $P_\mathrm{L}$ the useful power. $U_\mathrm{Ri}=R_\mathrm{i}I$ is the voltage across $R_\mathrm{i}$. $\eta$ is a pure number between 0 and 1 and depends only on the ratio $R_\mathrm{L}/R_\mathrm{i}$. **Validity:** This holds for the series connection of ideal source, $R_\mathrm{i}$ and $R_\mathrm{L}$. With an equivalent source, the internal quantities are not correct (Stage [7](warum-50-und-welches-ziel-gilt.html#stufe-7)).

:::herleitung The balance is the mesh rule times the current
The mesh rule reads $U_\mathrm{q}=U_\mathrm{Ri}+U_\mathrm{L}$. Multiplied by $I$, it gives $P_\mathrm{q}=P_\mathrm{v}+P_\mathrm{L}$. In [(5.2)](#gl-5-2), $I$ cancels, because both resistors carry the same current. The voltage divider ([Part 2](/wissen/en/elektrotechnik/1-semester/grundlagen-der-elektrotechnik/strom-widerstand-und-kirchhoff/strom-widerstand-und-kirchhoff.html)) gives $U_\mathrm{L}=U_\mathrm{q}\,R_\mathrm{L}/(R_\mathrm{L}+R_\mathrm{i})$.
:::

::::beispiel
The battery with four loads:

| $R_\mathrm{L}$ in $\Omega$ | $I$ in A | $U_\mathrm{L}$ in V | $P_\mathrm{L}$ in W | $P_\mathrm{v}$ in W | $P_\mathrm{q}$ in W | $\eta$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 3.6 | 1.8 | 6.48 | 25.92 | 32.4 | 20 % |
| 2 | 2.25 | 4.5 | 10.125 | 10.125 | 20.25 | 50 % |
| 8 | 0.9 | 7.2 | 6.48 | 1.62 | 8.1 | 80 % |
| 16 | 0.5 | 8 | 4 | 0.5 | 4.5 | 88.9 % |
::::

⁠**Limiting cases:** In a short circuit, $R_\mathrm{L}=0$, $\eta=0$: the load stays cold, and the entire power $P_\mathrm{q}=40.5$ W heats $R_\mathrm{i}$. In an open circuit, $R_\mathrm{L}\to\infty$, $\eta\to1$, but $I\to0$ and thus $P_\mathrm{L}\to0$.

:::frage The efficiency keeps rising with $R_\mathrm{L}$. Why not make the load arbitrarily large?
Because $\eta$ is a fraction and not an amount. With a large $R_\mathrm{L}$, each coulomb gives off almost everything in the load, but hardly any coulombs arrive. The table shows it: from 8 $\Omega$ to 16 $\Omega$, $\eta$ rises while the power $P_\mathrm{L}$ falls.
:::
