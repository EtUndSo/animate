Chapter 1: Sets, Logic and Real Numbers

  1. Termset, statement
    A set collects objects; a statement is a sentence that is true or false.
    Overview
  2. Termaxiom
    An axiom is a basic assumption that is laid down and not proved.
    Overview
  3. Termfield
    Three groups of axioms suffice: A field is a set in which plus and times work according to the familiar rules.
    Overview
  4. Termorder
    The order fixes which numbers are positive.
    Overview
  5. TermCompleteness
    Completeness says that the number line has no gaps.
    Overview
  6. Termmathematical induction, absolute value
    In addition there are mathematical induction, the proof method for statements about all natural numbers, and the absolute value, the distance of a number from zero.
    Overview
  7. Termnatural numbers, integers, rational numbers
    The symbol $:=$ means “is defined as.” The natural numbers are $\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,3,\dots\}$, the integers $\mathbb{Z}:=\{0,1,-1,2,-2,\dots\}$, the rational numbers (fractions) $\mathbb{Q}:=\{p/q:p,q\in\mathbb{Z},\ q\ne0\}$.
    From the ground up: Number systems and decimal numbers
  8. Termreal number
    A real number is a number with a decimal representation, that is, with a sign, an $m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$ and digits $a_1,a_2,\ldots\in\{0,1,\dots,9\}$, finitely or infinitely many.
    From the ground up: Number systems and decimal numbers
  9. Termirrational
    The numbers in $\mathbb{R}$ that are not fractions are called irrational.
    From the ground up: Number systems and decimal numbers
  10. FormulaDefinition 0.1 (Real number, provisional)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\pm\Bigl(m+\frac{a_1}{10}+\frac{a_2}{100}+\frac{a_3}{1000}+\dots\Bigr).}$$
    From the ground up: Number systems and decimal numbers
  11. TermVenn diagram
    A Venn diagram (sets drawn as regions) only illustrates; it shows one possible arrangement of the sets (Fig. 1).
    Stage 1: Sets and statements
  12. Termelements
    A set is an unordered collection of definite, distinct objects, its elements.
    Stage 1: Sets and statements
  13. Termdisjoint
    $A$ and $B$ are called disjoint if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A\cap B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\emptyset}$.
    Stage 1: Sets and statements
  14. Termtruth table
    From statements $\mathcal A$, $\mathcal B$ new ones arise, defined by this truth table.
    Stage 1: Sets and statements
  15. Termnegation, disjunction, conjunction, implication, equivalence
    The names are negation, disjunction, conjunction, implication and equivalence.
    Stage 1: Sets and statements
  16. Termparameter
    Statements can depend on a parameter: $\mathcal T(n):=(n\text{ divides }12)$ is true for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ and false for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$.
    Stage 1: Sets and statements
  17. Termtautology
    A tautology is a statement that is true for all truth values of its building blocks.
    Stage 1: Sets and statements
  18. Termuniversal quantifier, existential quantifier
    $\forall$ means “for all” (universal quantifier), $\exists$ “there is at least one” (existential quantifier), $\exists_1$ “there is exactly one,” $\nexists$ “there is no.”
    Stage 2: Quantifiers and indirect proof
  19. Termcontradiction hypothesis
    You assume the opposite of the claim (contradiction hypothesis) and reason until something false comes out.
    Stage 2: Quantifiers and indirect proof
  20. TermRussell's paradox
    Let $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{N:N\notin N\}}$ be the set of all sets that do not contain themselves (Russell's paradox, 1903).
    Stage 2: Quantifiers and indirect proof
  21. FormulaTheorem 2.2 (Negation rule)
    $$\neg\bigl(\forall x\in M:\mathcal A(x)\bigr)\Leftrightarrow\bigl(\exists x\in M:\neg\mathcal A(x)\bigr),\quad \neg\bigl(\exists y\in N:\mathcal B(y)\bigr)\Leftrightarrow\bigl(\forall y\in N:\neg\mathcal B(y)\bigr).$$
    Stage 2: Quantifiers and indirect proof
  22. Termoperation
    An operation assigns to any two elements an element of the same set.
    Stage 3: Field axioms
  23. Termdistributive law
    In addition there are (AM) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x\cdot(y+z)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x\cdot y)+(x\cdot z)}$ (distributive law) and (NNE) $0\ne1$.
    Stage 3: Field axioms
  24. Termdifference
    Equations are uniquely solvable: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x+z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$ has exactly the solution $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y-x}$ (difference).
    Stage 3: Field axioms
  25. Termquotient
    $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{xz}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$ has, for $x\ne0$, exactly the solution $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y\cdot x^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac yx}$ (quotient).
    Stage 3: Field axioms
  26. Termtotally ordered
    A field $\mathbb K$ with a relation “$>0$” is called totally ordered if.
    Stage 4: Order axioms
  27. FormulaTheorem 5.1 (Mathematical induction)
    $$\bigl(\mathcal A(n_0)\wedge\mathcal A(n_0+1)\wedge\dots\wedge\mathcal A(n)\bigr)\Rightarrow\mathcal A(n+1),$$
    Stage 5: Mathematical induction, sum and product
  28. FormulaDefinition 5.2 (Sum, product, power: recursive)
    $$\sum_{i=1}^{1}x_i:=x_1,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_i:=\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i\Bigr)+x_{n+1},\qquad \prod_{i=1}^{1}x_i:=x_1,\quad\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}x_i:=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_i\Bigr)\cdot x_{n+1}.$$
    Stage 5: Mathematical induction, sum and product
  29. Termbounded above
    $M$ is called bounded above if there is $S\in\mathbb K$ with $x\le S$ for all $x\in M$.
    Stage 6: The completeness axiom
  30. Termupper bound
    Every such $S$ is called an upper bound.
    Stage 6: The completeness axiom
  31. Termbounded below, lower bound
    Correspondingly bounded below and lower bound with $R\le x$.
    Stage 6: The completeness axiom
  32. Termbounded
    Both together are called bounded.
    Stage 6: The completeness axiom
  33. Termsupremum
    $S$ is called the supremum of $M$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sup M}$, if $S$ is an upper bound and for every upper bound $\tilde S$ we have $S\le\tilde S$ (least upper bound).
    Stage 6: The completeness axiom
  34. Terminfimum
    $R$ is called the infimum of $M$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\inf M}$, if $R$ is a lower bound and for every lower bound $\tilde R$ we have $\tilde R\le R$ (greatest lower bound).
    Stage 6: The completeness axiom
  35. Termmaximum, minimum
    If $S$ lies in $M$, it is called the maximum ($\max M$); if $R$ lies in $M$, it is called the minimum ($\min M$).
    Stage 6: The completeness axiom
  36. Termcomplete
    A totally ordered field $\mathbb K$ is called complete if every nonempty set $M\subset\mathbb K$ that is bounded below has a greatest lower bound $\inf M\in\mathbb K$.
    Stage 6: The completeness axiom
  37. TermTheorem of Archimedes:
    Theorem of Archimedes: For all $x,y>0$ there is an $n\in\mathbb{N}$ with $nx>y$.
    Stage 6: The completeness axiom
  38. Terminterval, nonnegative numbers
    An interval is $[a,b]:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:a\le x\le b\}$; the nonnegative numbers are $\mathbb{R}_0^+:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x\ge0\}$.
    Stage 7: Nested intervals and roots
  39. Termsquare root
    One writes $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt y}$ (“square root of $y$”).
    Stage 7: Nested intervals and roots
  40. FormulaTheorem 7.1 (Nested intervals)
    $$\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists n\in\mathbb{N}:\ 0\le b_n-a_n<\varepsilon.$$
    Stage 7: Nested intervals and roots
  41. Termreverse triangle inequality
    In addition, $|x-y|\le|x|+|y|$, the reverse triangle inequality $\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|\le|x\pm y|$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{x^2}}$ hold.
    Stage 8: Distance and absolute value
  42. FormulaDefinition 8.1 (Distance and absolute value)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d(x,y):=\begin{cases}x-y,&x\ge y\\y-x,&x<y\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|x|:=d(x,0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{cases}x,&x\ge0\\-x,&x<0\end{cases}}$$
    Stage 8: Distance and absolute value
  43. TermBernoulli
    Bernoulli: For $x>-1$, $x\ne0$ and $n\ge2$, $(1+x)^n>1+nx$.
    Stage 9: Elementary inequalities
  44. TermYoung
    Young: For $\varepsilon>0$ and $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$, $|xy|\le\frac\varepsilon2x^2+\frac1{2\varepsilon}y^2$.
    Stage 9: Elementary inequalities
  45. TermMeans
    Means: For $x,y\ge0$, $\sqrt{xy}\le\frac{x+y}2$.
    Stage 9: Elementary inequalities
  46. Termgeometric mean, arithmetic mean
    On the left is the geometric mean, on the right the arithmetic mean.
    Stage 9: Elementary inequalities
  47. Termvector, norm
    $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x_1,\dots,x_n)}$ is a vector, a list of $n$ numbers, and $\|\vec x\|$ its norm.
    Stage 9: Elementary inequalities
  48. FormulaTheorem 9.1 (Five inequalities)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sum_{i=1}^n|x_iy_i|\le\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^nx_i^2}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^ny_i^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\|\vec x\|\cdot\|\vec y\|.}$$
    $$\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i+y_i)^2}\le\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^nx_i^2}+\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^ny_i^2},\quad\text{that is,}\quad\|\vec x+\vec y\|\le\|\vec x\|+\|\vec y\|.$$
    Stage 9: Elementary inequalities

Chapter 2: Complex Numbers

  1. Termreal part, imaginary part
    In $z_1$, 3 is the real part (the portion without $\mathrm{i}$) and 1 is the imaginary part (the real number in front of $\mathrm{i}$).
    Overview
  2. Termstandard form
    Every complex number has exactly one standard form $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x+\mathrm{i}y}$ with $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$.
    Stage 1: Pairs instead of single numbers
  3. FormulaDefinition 1.1 (Complex numbers)
    $$\begin{aligned} (x_1,y_1)\oplus(x_2,y_2)&:=(x_1+x_2,\;y_1+y_2),\\ (x_1,y_1)\odot(x_2,y_2)&:=(x_1x_2-y_1y_2,\;x_1y_2+x_2y_1). \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 1: Pairs instead of single numbers
  4. FormulaTheorem 1.2 (Imaginary unit and standard form)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(x_1+\mathrm{i}y_1)(x_2+\mathrm{i}y_2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x_1x_2-y_1y_2)+\mathrm{i}(x_1y_2+x_2y_1).}$$
    Stage 1: Pairs instead of single numbers
  5. Termreal axis, imaginary axis, purely imaginary
    The horizontal real axis is $\mathbb{R}$; on the vertical imaginary axis lie the purely imaginary numbers $\mathrm{i}y$.
    Stage 2: The complex plane
  6. TermGaussian plane
    The complex plane with the coordinates $\operatorname{Re}(z)$ and $\operatorname{Im}(z)$ is also called the Gaussian plane.
    Stage 2: The complex plane
  7. FormulaTheorem 4.1 (Reciprocal and division)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\overline{z}}{z\overline{z}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{x}{x^2+y^2}-\mathrm{i}\,\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}\qquad\text{with}\qquad z\cdot z^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.}$$
    Stage 4: Division, and what is lost
  8. FormulaDefinition 5.1 (Distance and absolute value)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d(z,w):=\sqrt{(x-u)^2+(y-v)^2},\qquad |z|:=d(z,0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}.}$$
    Stage 5: Absolute value and distance
  9. Termdouble solution
    For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ there is only $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-p/2}$, a double solution.
    Stage 6: Quadratic equations
  10. Termdiscriminant
    $D$ is called the discriminant.
    Stage 6: Quadratic equations
  11. FormulaTheorem 6.1 (Square roots in $\mathbb{C}$)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{|D|+s}2,\qquad v^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{|D|-s}2.}$$
    Stage 6: Quadratic equations
  12. FormulaTheorem 6.2 (Quadratic equation in $\mathbb{C}$)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac p2\pm w\qquad\text{with}\qquad w^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{D.}$$
    Stage 6: Quadratic equations
  13. Termcomplex number
    A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x+\mathrm{i}y}$.
    Key points
  14. Termimaginary unit
    The imaginary unit $\mathrm{i}$ is the pair $(0,1)$ and satisfies $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{i}^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$.
    Key points
  15. Termcomplex plane
    All pairs together form the complex plane, in which $\mathbb{R}$ is the horizontal axis.
    Key points
  16. Termcomplex conjugate
    The complex conjugate $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\overline{z}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x-\mathrm{i}y}$ is the mirror image of $z$ across the real axis, and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z\overline{z}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2+y^2}$ is real.
    Key points
  17. Termtriangle inequality
    From this follow division, the absolute value $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|z|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}$ (distance from the origin) and the triangle inequality $|z+w|\le|z|+|w|$ (a detour is never shorter).
    Key points

Chapter 3: Functions and Polynomials

  1. Termfunction
    A temperature sensor delivers exactly one voltage for each temperature. Such an assignment is called a function: Every input $x$ gets exactly one output $f(x)$.
    Overview
  2. Terminverse function
    The inverse function provides the way back as a function in its own right.
    Overview
  3. Termpolynomial
    A polynomial is a sum of powers of $x$ with fixed numbers in front.
    Overview
  4. Termzero, linear factor
    A zero is an $x_0$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P(x_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$; it comes with a linear factor $x-x_0$, a factor of degree one.
    Overview
  5. TermHorner scheme
    The Horner scheme is the computational procedure that splits it off.
    Overview
  6. TermCartesian product
    The Cartesian product (also cross product) of two sets is the set of all ordered pairs.
    From the ground up: Pairs and intervals
  7. TermIntervals
    Intervals are the connected pieces of the number line.
    From the ground up: Pairs and intervals
  8. Termopen, closed, half-open
    For $a\le b$ the bounded sets $(a,b):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:a<x<b\}$ are called open, $[a,b]:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:a\le x\le b\}$ closed, and $(a,b]$ and $[a,b)$ half-open.
    From the ground up: Pairs and intervals
  9. Termmap
    A function or map $f:A\to B$ is a rule that assigns to every $a\in A$ a unique $b\in B$.
    Stage 1: What a function is
  10. Termimage, preimage
    $b$ is called the image of $a$, $a$ is called a preimage of $b$.
    Stage 1: What a function is
  11. Termreal function
    For $A,B\subset\mathbb{R}$, $f$ is called a real function.
    Stage 1: What a function is
  12. FormulaDefinition 1.1 (Function)
    $$f:\begin{cases}A\longrightarrow B\\ a\longmapsto b=:f(a).\end{cases}$$
    $$\mathcal D(f):=A,\quad\mathcal W(f):=\{b\in B:\text{there is }a\in A\text{ with }f(a)=b\},\quad\mathcal G(f):=\{(a,f(a)):a\in A\}$$
    Stage 1: What a function is
  13. Termeven, odd
    If $\mathcal D(f)$ contains $-x$ along with $x$, then $f$ is called even if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{f(-x)}$ for all $x\in\mathcal D(f)$, and odd if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-f(-x)}$ for all $x\in\mathcal D(f)$.
    Stage 2: Even, monotonic, bounded
  14. Term$n$th root
    The inverse function of $x\mapsto x^n$ (on $\mathbb{R}_0^+$ for even $n$, on $\mathbb{R}$ for odd $n$) is called the $n$th root, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f^{-1}(y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt[n]{y}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y^{1/n}}$.
    Stage 4: Combining and inverting
  15. FormulaDefinition 4.1 (Combinations)
    $$(f\pm g)(x):=f(x)\pm g(x),\qquad(f\cdot g)(x):=f(x)\cdot g(x),\qquad\Bigl(\frac fg\Bigr)(x):=\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}.$$
    $$(g\circ f)(x):=g\bigl(f(x)\bigr)\quad\text{for all }x\in A.$$
    Stage 4: Combining and inverting
  16. FormulaDefinition 4.3 (Inverse function and root)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y\ \Leftrightarrow\ x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{f^{-1}(y),\qquad f^{-1}\bigl(f(x)\bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x,\qquad f\bigl(f^{-1}(y)\bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y.}$$
    Stage 4: Combining and inverting
  17. Termcoefficients
    The numbers $a_0,\dots,a_n$ are called the coefficients of $P_n$.
    Stage 5: Polynomials and coefficients
  18. Termcomparing coefficients
    The theorem is the basis of comparing coefficients: To compare two polynomials as functions, you compare their coefficients.
    Stage 5: Polynomials and coefficients
  19. FormulaDefinition 5.1 (Polynomial)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a_nx^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\dots+a_1x+a_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{i=0}^na_ix^i.}$$
    Stage 5: Polynomials and coefficients
  20. FormulaTheorem 5.2 (Identity theorem for polynomials)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_n(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a_k}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{b_k\text{ for }k}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0,\dots,n.}$$
    Stage 5: Polynomials and coefficients
  21. FormulaDefinition 6.1 (Rational function)
    $$R(x):=\frac{P_n(x)}{Q_m(x)},\qquad\mathcal D(R):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:Q_m(x)\neq0\}$$
    Stage 6: Division with remainder
  22. FormulaTheorem 6.2 (Division with remainder)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_{n-m}(x)+\frac{T_k(x)}{Q_m(x)},}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_{n-m}(x)\,Q_m(x)+T_k(x).}$$
    Stage 6: Division with remainder
  23. FormulaDefinition 7.1 (Horner form)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Bigl\{\Bigl[\cdots\bigl(\underbrace{\{a_nx+a_{n-1}\}}_{=\,c_{n-1}(x)}\cdot x+a_{n-2}\bigr)\cdot x+\dots+a_2\Bigr]\cdot x+a_1\Bigr\}\cdot x+a_0.}$$
    Stage 7: The Horner scheme
  24. FormulaTheorem 7.2 (Horner at $x_0$)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_n:=a_n,\qquad c_i:=c_{i+1}\,x_0+a_i\quad\text{for }i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{n-1,n-2,\dots,0.}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_n(x_0),\qquad P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-x_0)\,Q_{n-1}(x)+c_0}$$
    Stage 7: The Horner scheme
  25. Termmultiplicity
    $m$ is called the multiplicity of the zero $x_0$.
    Stage 8: Every zero splits off a linear factor
  26. FormulaDefinition 8.1 (Zero and multiplicity)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-x_0)^m\,Q_{n-m}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{n-m}(x_0)\neq0.}$$
    Stage 8: Every zero splits off a linear factor
  27. FormulaTheorem 8.2 (Linear factor)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-x_0)\,Q_{n-1}(x)\text{ with a polynomial }Q_{n-1}.}$$
    Stage 8: Every zero splits off a linear factor
  28. Termasymptote
    Because $\deg T<\deg Q$, $T/Q$ becomes arbitrarily small for large $|x|$, since the highest power of the denominator grows faster. So $R$ approaches the polynomial $S$, the asymptote (for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n-m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ a straight line).
    Stage 9: Rational functions and poles
