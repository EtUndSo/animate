What this is about#

School teaches rules like $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(-1)\cdot(-1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ or “you must not divide by zero.” Why do they hold? Habit as a justification does not get you far: For complex numbers (Chapter 2) the rule “squares are not negative” fails, and all limits (Chapter 4) depend on a property of the numbers that school never mentions. You can see it in a single number: The diagonal of a square with side length 1 has length $\sqrt2$, and no fraction has this value. The question of this chapter is: What exactly must be assumed about numbers so that all the rules of arithmetic become provable, and what distinguishes the real numbers from the fractions?

The answer needs two tools and a short list of assumptions. A set collects objects; a statement is a sentence that is true or false. An axiom is a basic assumption that is laid down and not proved. Three groups of axioms suffice: A field is a set in which plus and times work according to the familiar rules. The order fixes which numbers are positive. Completeness says that the number line has no gaps. In addition there are mathematical induction, the proof method for statements about all natural numbers, and the absolute value, the distance of a number from zero.

The reference model for this chapter

  • ⁠Sets: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{1,2,\dots,12\}}$, within it $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{1,2,3,4,6,12\}}$ (divisors of 12) and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{2,3,5,7,11\}}$ (primes).

  • ⁠Statement: $\mathcal E(n):=(n\ge7\Rightarrow n>5)$ for natural numbers $n$.

  • ⁠Gap set: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{x\in\mathbb{Q}:x>0,\ x^2<2\}}$, the positive fractions with square below 2.

  • ⁠Numbers for inequalities: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-3}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ for the absolute value; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8}$ for the means; the lists $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(3,4)}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1,2)}$ for Cauchy-Schwarz.
