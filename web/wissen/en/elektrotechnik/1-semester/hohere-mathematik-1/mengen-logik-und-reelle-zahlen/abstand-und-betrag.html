Stage 8: Distance and absolute value

What this stage is about:

Limits measure how close two numbers lie to each other. For that you need distance and a rule for detours.

The idea. The distance between two numbers is the length of the segment between them on the line. The length does not depend on direction, and a detour via a third point is never shorter (Fig. 8). The absolute value is the distance from zero.

Definition 8.1 (Distance and absolute value)

For $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$,

$$d(x,y):=\begin{cases}x-y,&x\ge y\\y-x,&x<y\end{cases}$$
$$\text{and}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x|:=d(x,0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{cases}x,&x\ge0\\-x,&x<0\end{cases}}$$
(8.1)

are called the distance of $x$ and $y$ and the absolute value of $x$.

⁠How to read it: $d$ as in distance. From the definition it follows that

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d(x,y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{d(x-y,0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|x-y|,\ \ -|x|\le x\le|x|,\ \ |x|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\max\{x,-x\},\ \ -|x|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\min\{x,-x\}.}$$
(8.2)

In the reference model, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|y|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d(x,y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|{-3}-5|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8}$. The minus sign in (8.1) is deceptive: For $x<0$, $-x$ is positive; the absolute value is never negative. Values with a tolerance are written like this: $|U-5\,\mathrm V|\le0.1\,\mathrm V$ means that $U$ lies between 4.9 V and 5.1 V.

Theorem 8.2 (Properties of distance and absolute value)

For all $x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}$:

DistanceAbsolute value
positive$d(x,y)\ge0$$\vert x\vert \ge0$
definite$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d(x,y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0\Leftrightarrow x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vert x\vert }\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0\Leftrightarrow x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$
symmetric, product$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d(x,y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{d(y,x)}$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vert xy\vert }\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\vert x\vert \vert y\vert }$; for $z\ne0$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vert x/z\vert }\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\vert x\vert /\vert z\vert }$
triangle inequality$d(x,y)\le d(x,z)+d(z,y)$$\vert x+y\vert \le\vert x\vert +\vert y\vert $

In addition, $|x-y|\le|x|+|y|$, the reverse triangle inequality $\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|\le|x\pm y|$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{x^2}}$ hold.

⁠How to read it: The triangle inequality estimates a sum from above, the reverse one from below.

QuestionWhy doesn't $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x+y|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|x|+|y|}$ hold?

It holds only with equal signs or if one summand is zero. With different signs the summands partly cancel: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|-3+5|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2<8}$. In the worst case two errors add up; that is what the inequality says.

Proof: Triangle inequality and consequences

Definiteness and symmetry are in (8.1). Triangle inequality for $d$. Without loss of generality (WLOG for short: the remaining cases turn into this one by renaming) let $x<y$; for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$ the left side is 0. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x}$ or $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$, one summand is 0 and the inequality is an equality. Three cases remain. $x<y<z$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d(x,z)+d(z,y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2z-x-y>2y-x-y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y-x}$. $x<z<y$: The sum is exactly $y-x$. $z<x<y$: The sum is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x+y-2z>x+y-2x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y-x}$.

⁠Product. If $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{xy}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, both sides are 0 by Theorem 3.2 (v). For $xy>0$, $x$, $y$ have equal signs by Theorem 4.2 (c), and $|x||y|$ is $xy$ or $(-x)(-y)$, both equal to $xy$. $xy<0$ works the same way. For the quotient: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z\cdot\frac xz}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x}$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|z|\,|\frac xz|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|x|}$.

⁠Absolute value. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x+y|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|x-(-y)|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{d(x,-y)\le d(x,0)+d(0,-y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|x|+|y|}$, because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|-y|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|y|}$. With $-y$ in place of $y$ it follows that $|x-y|\le|x|+|y|$.

⁠Reverse triangle inequality. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|(x-y)+y|\le|x-y|+|y|}$, so $|x|-|y|\le|x-y|$. Swapping $x$ and $y$ also gives $|y|-|x|\le|x-y|$. By (8.2), the maximum of the two is $\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|$. With $-y$ in place of $y$ the variant with $+$ follows.

⁠Root. $|x|\ge0$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x|^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2}$, so $|x|$ is the unique nonnegative solution of $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2}$.

Example

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-3}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x+y|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\le8}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|x|+|y|}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\le|x+y|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$.

⁠Limiting case $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: All inequalities become $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|x|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|x|}$.

Exercise 9 ★★ Inequalities with absolute values

  1. Describe $\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|x-2|\le3\}$ as an interval.

  2. Solve $|x-2|+|x+1|\le5$ by case analysis at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$.

  3. Two voltages satisfy $|U_1-5\,\mathrm V|\le0.1\,\mathrm V$, $|U_2-3\,\mathrm V|\le0.2\,\mathrm V$. How far can $U_1+U_2$ deviate from 8 V at most? Give a pair that reaches this value.

Solution

(a) $-3\le x-2\le3$, so $[-1,5]$. (b) For $x\ge2$: $2x-1\le5$, so $2\le x\le3$. For $-1\le x<2$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(2-x)+(x+1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3\le5}$, always true. For $x<-1$: $1-2x\le5$, so $-2\le x<-1$. Together: $[-2,3]$. (c) $|(U_1+U_2)-8\,\mathrm V|\le|U_1-5\,\mathrm V|+|U_2-3\,\mathrm V|\le0.3\,\mathrm V$. Reached at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.1\,\mathrm V}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.2\,\mathrm V}$.
