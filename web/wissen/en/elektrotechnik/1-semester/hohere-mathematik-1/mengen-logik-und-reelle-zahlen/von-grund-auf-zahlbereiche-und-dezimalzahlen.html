From the ground up: Number systems and decimal numbers

The symbol $:=$ means “is defined as.” The natural numbers are $\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,3,\dots\}$, the integers $\mathbb{Z}:=\{0,1,-1,2,-2,\dots\}$, the rational numbers (fractions) $\mathbb{Q}:=\{p/q:p,q\in\mathbb{Z},\ q\ne0\}$. The colon means “for which.”

Definition 0.1 (Real number, provisional)

A real number is a number with a decimal representation, that is, with a sign, an $m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$ and digits $a_1,a_2,\ldots\in\{0,1,\dots,9\}$, finitely or infinitely many:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\pm\Bigl(m+\frac{a_1}{10}+\frac{a_2}{100}+\frac{a_3}{1000}+\dots\Bigr).}$$
(0.1)

$\mathbb{R}$ is the set of real numbers. The numbers in $\mathbb{R}$ that are not fractions are called irrational.

⁠How to read it: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{3/8}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.375}$ terminates, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{5/11}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.4545\ldots}$ is periodic, and every fraction has one of these two forms. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sqrt2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.41421356\ldots}$ never becomes periodic. We have $\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}$. This definition is a stopgap: It does not explain what an infinite sum is (Chapter 4), and it is not unique: $1.000\ldots$ and $0.999\ldots$ are the same number. Stage 6 replaces it with axioms.
