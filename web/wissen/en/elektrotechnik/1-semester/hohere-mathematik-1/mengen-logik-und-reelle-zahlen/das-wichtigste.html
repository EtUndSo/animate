The answer in three sentences

  1. All rules of arithmetic follow by logical reasoning from a few axioms: the field axioms for plus and times and the order axioms for “positive.”

  2. The fractions satisfy these axioms too, but they have gaps: No fraction $r$ solves $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$.

  3. The completeness axiom closes the gaps: Every nonempty set that has a lower bound (a number less than or equal to all elements) also has a greatest one. With it the real numbers are uniquely determined, and $\sqrt2$ exists.

Key points

  • Sets and statements obey the same rules, proved by table. $\mathcal A\Rightarrow\mathcal B$ is false only for $t\Rightarrow f$.

  • Negating swaps $\forall$ and $\exists$. Different quantifiers must not be swapped.

  • Field: 10 axioms, link $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x(y+z)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{xy+xz}$. Order: $z<0$ flips the inequality, $x\ne0\Rightarrow x^2>0$.

  • Induction: base case plus step.

  • $\sqrt2\notin\mathbb{Q}$. Completeness: Every nonempty set that is bounded below has an infimum in the field. That separates $\mathbb{R}$ from $\mathbb{Q}$. Nested intervals yield $\sqrt y$.

  • $|x+y|\le|x|+|y|$, $(1+x)^n>1+nx$, $\sqrt{xy}\le\frac{x+y}2$, $\sum|x_iy_i|\le\|\vec x\|\|\vec y\|$, $\|\vec x+\vec y\|\le\|\vec x\|+\|\vec y\|$.

One principle behind all the results. Nothing is taken on faith: Every rule stands at the end of a chain of inferences that begins with an axiom.
