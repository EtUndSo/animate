Stage 1: What a function is

What this stage is about:

School presents functions as formulas. That is not enough: A measured relationship has no formula, and the same formula can belong to different functions. This stage says what a function is.

The idea. A function fixes exactly one output for each admissible input (Fig. 1). It is the list of these pairs of input and output; the formula is only a short way of giving the list. Two inputs may share one output; in the reference model $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{h(4)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$. But one input may not have two outputs, and it may not be left without an output. Otherwise $f(x)$ would not be a definite number, and you could not calculate with it.

Definition 1.1 (Function)

Let $A$, $B$ be sets ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{B}$ is possible). A function or map $f:A\to B$ is a rule that assigns to every $a\in A$ a unique $b\in B$:

$$f:\begin{cases}A\longrightarrow B\\ a\longmapsto b=:f(a).\end{cases}$$
(1.1)

$b$ is called the image of $a$, $a$ is called a preimage of $b$. Further,

$$\mathcal D(f):=A,\quad\mathcal W(f):=\{b\in B:\text{there is }a\in A\text{ with }f(a)=b\},\quad\mathcal G(f):=\{(a,f(a)):a\in A\}$$
(1.2)

are called the domain, the range and the graph of $f$; here $\mathcal G(f)\subset A\times B$. For $A,B\subset\mathbb{R}$, $f$ is called a real function. Two functions are equal if their graphs are equal: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g:\Leftrightarrow\mathcal G(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathcal G(g)}$.

⁠How to read it: The arrow $\to$ connects sets, $\mapsto$ connects elements, and $=:$ means “is called this.” $B$ is only a reservoir; $\mathcal W(f)\subset B$ holds. Since $\mathcal W(f)$ is often unknown, for real functions one usually writes $f:\mathcal D(f)\to\mathbb{R}$. Equal graphs also mean equal domains: $\sqrt x$ on $[0,10]$ and $\sqrt x$ on $\mathbb{R}_0^+$ have the same formula and are different functions.

Examples

The parabola reaches only $y\ge-1$: A square is never negative, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-2)^2-1\ge-1}$, and every $y\ge-1$ is hit by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2+\sqrt{y+1}}$. For the bell, $1+x^2\ge1$, so $0<b(x)\le1$.

Not every set of pairs is a graph. The unit circle $\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2:x^2+y^2=1\}$ contains $(0,1)$ and $(0,-1)$: The input $0$ would have two outputs. In the picture this means: A vertical line meets a graph at most once (Fig. 2).

QuestionAren't $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2/x}$ the same function?

For every $x\neq0$ they give the same value, that is true. But $g(0)$ would be $0/0$ and is not defined, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal D(g)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}$, and the graphs differ by the point $(0,0)$. Stage 9 calls this a gap.

Exercise 1 ★ Domain, range, graph

(a) Determine the largest possible domain in $\mathbb{R}$ and the range of $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{9-x^2}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|x-1|+2}$. (b) Is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2:x=y^2\}}$ the graph of a function of $x$? And $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2:x=y^3\}}$?

Solution

(a) We need $9-x^2\ge0$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal D(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{[-3,3]}$. Largest value 3 at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, smallest 0 at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\pm3}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal W(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{[0,3]}$. For $g$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal D(g)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbb{R}}$, and because $|x-1|\ge0$, then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal W(g)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{[2,\infty)}$. (b) $M_1$ is not: $(4,2)$ and $(4,-2)$ lie in it, and for $x<0$ there is no $y$. $M_2$ is: For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$ there is exactly one $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt[3]{x}}$.
