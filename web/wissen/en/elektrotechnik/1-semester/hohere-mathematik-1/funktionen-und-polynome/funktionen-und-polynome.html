What this is about#

A temperature sensor delivers exactly one voltage for each temperature. Such an assignment is called a function: Every input $x$ gets exactly one output $f(x)$. Going forward, substituting is enough. What is usually needed is the way back: For the measured voltage $y$ we seek the temperature $x$, that is, the solution of the equation $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$. The question of this chapter is: When does $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$ have exactly one solution, and how do you find the solutions of $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ for the most important functions, the polynomials?

The answer leads through seven concepts. The inverse function provides the way back as a function in its own right. A polynomial is a sum of powers of $x$ with fixed numbers in front. A zero is an $x_0$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P(x_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$; it comes with a linear factor $x-x_0$, a factor of degree one. The Horner scheme is the computational procedure that splits it off. Then there are the rational function, a quotient of two polynomials, and the pole, where its denominator becomes zero and its absolute value grows beyond every bound.

  • ⁠Bell $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{b(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/(1+x^2)}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{b(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{b(1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{b(3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.1}$.

  • ⁠Polynomial $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2x^3-3x^2-11x+6}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-3)(2x-1)(x+2)}$ with the zeros $3$, $\tfrac12$ and $-2$.

  • ⁠Denominator $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2-x-2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-2)(x+1)}$, together the rational function $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P(x)/Q(x)}$.

  • ⁠Polynomial of degree four $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^4-3x^3+x^2+4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-2)^2(x^2+x+1)}$ with a double zero.
