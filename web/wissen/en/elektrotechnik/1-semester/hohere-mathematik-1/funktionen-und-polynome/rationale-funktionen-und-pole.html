What this stage is about:

The remainder $T/Q$ from Stage 6 has points where its denominator becomes zero. The function is not defined there. What happens nearby, and what happens for large $|x|$?

The idea. Dividing a fixed number by something very small makes the result very large. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, but the numerator stays at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-12}$. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.01}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.0301}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-398}$; at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.99}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.0299}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{+402}$. The mechanism is the small factor $x-2$ in the denominator: It changes sign at 2, and $R$ changes with it. If the numerator is also zero there, on the other hand, the factor can cancel.

Definition 9.1 (Pole and gap)

Let $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_n/Q_m}$. Cancel all common linear factors of numerator and denominator. A point $x_0$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_m(x_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ is called

  • a pole of order $m_0$ if after canceling it is an $m_0$-fold zero of the denominator and not a zero of the numerator;

  • a gap if after canceling it is no longer a zero of the denominator.

⁠How to read it: Near a pole, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{A(x)/(x-x_0)^{m_0}}$ with $A(x_0)\neq0$: The numerator stays near $A(x_0)$, the denominator becomes arbitrarily small, and $|R|$ grows beyond every bound. (Chapter 4 makes this precise with limits.) Odd order means a sign change, even order none, for the same reason as in Stage 8: $1/(x-2)^2$ equals $10\,000$ at $2.01$ and at $1.99$. At a gap $R$ stays finite, but there too $x_0\notin\mathcal D(R)$. Example of a gap: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x^2-4)/(x-2)}$ agrees with $x+2$ for $x\neq2$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L(2.01)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.01}$; at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ only one point of the graph is removed.

Large $|x|$. By (6.2), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S+T/Q}$. Because $\deg T<\deg Q$, $T/Q$ becomes arbitrarily small for large $|x|$, since the highest power of the denominator grows faster. So $R$ approaches the polynomial $S$, the asymptote (for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n-m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ a straight line). In the reference model $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R(100)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{198.92}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\cdot100-1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{199}$; the remainder accounts for only $-0.08$. Limiting cases: For $n<m$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, and $R$ approaches 0. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{m}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a_n/b_m}$ is a constant.

Going deeper, not needed for the rest: Partial fraction decomposition

The remainder can be decomposed further so that each pole gets its own term:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2x-1+\frac{A}{x-2}+\frac{B}{x+1}.}$$

Multiplying $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{-8x+4}{(x-2)(x+1)}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{A}{x-2}+\frac{B}{x+1}}$ by $x-2$ and setting $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ leaves $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-12/3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-4}$, likewise $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12/(-3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-4}$. For poles of order 1 that is enough. In electrical engineering this is how transfer functions, which describe how a circuit changes a signal, are decomposed into contributions per pole.

QuestionIsn't a gap the same as a pole, since the denominator is zero in both cases?

The denominator is zero in both cases, that is true. The difference lies in the numerator. At a pole it stays nonzero, and a finite value divided by almost zero becomes large. At a gap it is zero as well, the common factor drops out, and the quotient stays finite.

Exercise 10 ★★ Pole or gap

Let $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_2(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2-3x+2}}$. (a) Determine $\mathcal D(L_2)$, poles and gaps. (b) Compute $L_2(2.01)$ and $L_2(1.99)$ and explain the sign. (c) Which value does $L_2$ approach for large $|x|$? Check with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1000}$.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_2(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x-1)(x-2)}}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal D(L_2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1,2\}}$. Canceled: $\frac{x+1}{x-2}$. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ a gap (value of the canceled form: $-2$), at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ a pole of order 1. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_2(2.01)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.01/0.01}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{301}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_2(1.99)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.99/(-0.01)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-299}$. The numerator is positive, near 3; the denominator $x-2$ changes sign. (c) We have $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_2\to1/1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_2(1000)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1001/998}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.003}$.

Exercise 11 ★★★ Asymptote with Horner

Let $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{x^2+x+1}{x-1}}$. (a) Divide with the Horner scheme and give the polynomial part and the remainder. (b) Where is the pole, and what order does it have? (c) How far does $W$ lie above the asymptote at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$?

Solution

(a) Scheme at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ with $1,1,1$: $1,2,3$. So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x^2+x+1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-1)(x+2)+3}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x+2+\frac{3}{x-1}}$. (b) At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, because the numerator is 3 there. The denominator factor occurs once: order 1. (c) Distance $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{3/99}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.0303}$, above the asymptote, because $x-1>0$.

Summary

Terms
  • pole, order – … a pole of order $m_0$ if after canceling it is an $m_0$-fold zero of the denominator and not a zero of the numerator; …
  • gap – … a gap if after canceling it is no longer a zero of the denominator.
  • asymptote – Because $\deg T<\deg Q$, $T/Q$ becomes arbitrarily small for large $|x|$, since the highest power of the denominator grows faster. So $R$ approaches the polynomial $S$, the asymptote (for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n-m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ a straight line).
