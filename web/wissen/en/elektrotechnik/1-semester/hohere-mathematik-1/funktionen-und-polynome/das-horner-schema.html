Stage 7: The Horner scheme

What this stage is about:

For the divisor $x-x_0$ the division is so regular that a scheme with three rows is enough. At the same time it delivers the value $P(x_0)$ with only $n$ multiplications. That is why computers usually evaluate polynomials this way.

The idea. If you compute $P_n(x_0)$ as written, you need $n-1$ multiplications for $x_0^2,\dots,x_0^n$, $n$ more for the coefficients, and $n$ additions, in total $2n-1$ multiplications. If you repeatedly factor out $x$, each power comes out by itself, and $n$ multiplications are enough. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ that is 10 instead of 19.

Definition 7.1 (Horner form)

Repeatedly factoring out $x$ turns (5.1) into the Horner form

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Bigl\{\Bigl[\cdots\bigl(\underbrace{\{a_nx+a_{n-1}\}}_{=\,c_{n-1}(x)}\cdot x+a_{n-2}\bigr)\cdot x+\dots+a_2\Bigr]\cdot x+a_1\Bigr\}\cdot x+a_0.}$$
(7.1)

Read from the inside out, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a_n}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_i(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{c_{i+1}(x)\,x+a_i}$ and at the end $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_0(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_n(x)}$.

Theorem 7.2 (Horner at $x_0$)

Let $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{k=0}^na_kx^k}$ be a polynomial of degree $n\ge1$ and $x_0\in\mathbb{R}$ fixed. Set

$$c_n:=a_n,$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_i:=c_{i+1}\,x_0+a_i\quad\text{for }i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{n-1,n-2,\dots,0.}$$
(7.2)

Then

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_n(x_0),}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-x_0)\,Q_{n-1}(x)+c_0}$$
(7.3)

with $Q_{n-1}(x):=c_nx^{n-1}+c_{n-1}x^{n-2}+\dots+c_2x+c_1$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\deg Q_{n-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{n-1}$.

Proof: Horner

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_n(x_0)}$: The recursion (7.2) computes (7.1) at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x_0}$ from the inside out. For the second equation, multiply out and sort by powers:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(x-x_0)\bigl[c_nx^{n-1}+\dots+c_1\bigr]+c_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{c_nx^n+(c_{n-1}-x_0c_n)x^{n-1}+\dots+(c_1-x_0c_2)x+(c_0-x_0c_1).}$$

By (7.2), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a_n}$ and every parenthesis $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_i-x_0c_{i+1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a_i}$, so the right side is $P_n(x)$. Because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a_n\neq0}$, the degree of $Q_{n-1}$ is exactly $n-1$.

⁠How to read it: The $c_i$ are intermediate results of the computation and at the same time the coefficients of the quotient. That is why one computation delivers both. The scheme arranges them like this:

$a_n$$a_{n-1}$$a_{n-2}$$\cdots$$a_1$$a_0$
$x_0$$c_nx_0$$c_{n-1}x_0$$\cdots$$c_2x_0$$c_1x_0$
$c_n$$c_{n-1}$$c_{n-2}$$\cdots$$c_1$$c_0$

The first row contains all coefficients, including the zeros. You write $a_n$ down, multiply by $x_0$, write the result in the next column, add, and so on.

Example

Fig. 8 computes $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-12}$ with 3 multiplications; directly: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\cdot8-3\cdot4-11\cdot2+6}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-12}$. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ the bottom row is $2,\ 3,\ -2,\ 0$: So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P(3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-3)(2x^2+3x-2)}$.

⁠Limiting cases: For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ the bottom row is the top row, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P(0)}$. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ the scheme adds all coefficients, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2-3-11+6}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-6}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P(1)}$. Repeating the scheme with the quotient produces the representation in powers of $x-x_0$ (Exercise 8).

QuestionWhy must the zero for a missing power go into the scheme?

Each column belongs to a power; the scheme counts the powers through the columns. If you omit the zero for $x$ in $S$, it computes with $1,-3,1,4$, that is, with $x^3-3x^2+x+4$. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ it then gives 2 instead of $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

Exercise 8 ★ Computing with Horner

(a) For $3x^4-2x^3+5x-7$, compute the value at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2}$ and the quotient. (b) How many multiplications does the scheme save for degree 20? (c) Write $P$ in the form $d_3(x-1)^3+d_2(x-1)^2+d_1(x-1)+d_0$. Hint: Apply the scheme repeatedly at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$; $d_0$ is the first remainder, $d_1$ the second.

Solution

(a) Top row $3,-2,0,5,-7$ with the zero for $x^2$, bottom row $3,-8,16,-27,47$. So the value is 47 (directly: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{48+16-10-7}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{47}$), the quotient $3x^3-8x^2+16x-27$. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\cdot20-1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{39}$ against 20: 19 multiplications fewer, almost half. (c) At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$: $2,-1,-12,-6$; then for the quotient: $2,1,-11$; then $2,3$. So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-6}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-11}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$. Check at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-2+3+11-6}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P(0)}$.
