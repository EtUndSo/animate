From the ground up: Pairs and intervals

Sets, the symbols $\in$, $\subset$ and the number systems are known from Chapter 1. Two building blocks are added. The Cartesian product (also cross product) of two sets is the set of all ordered pairs:

$$A\times B:=\bigl\{(a,b):a\in A\ \wedge\ b\in B\bigr\}.$$

The order counts, $(1,2)\neq(2,1)$. For $n$ sets likewise $A_1\times\dots\times A_n:=\{(a_1,\dots,a_n):a_i\in A_i\text{ for all }i\}$, and $A^n:=A\times\dots\times A$ with $n$ factors. $\mathbb{R}^2$ is the number plane.

Intervals are the connected pieces of the number line. For $a\le b$ the bounded sets $(a,b):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:a<x<b\}$ are called open, $[a,b]:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:a\le x\le b\}$ closed, and $(a,b]$ and $[a,b)$ half-open. The square bracket means: The endpoint belongs to the interval; the round one means: It does not. Unbounded are $(a,\infty):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:a<x\}$, $[a,\infty):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:a\le x\}$, $(-\infty,b):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x<b\}$, $(-\infty,b]:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x\le b\}$ and $(-\infty,\infty):=\mathbb{R}$. The symbol $\infty$ is not a number, which is why the round bracket always stands there. In addition, $\mathbb{R}_0^+:=[0,\infty)$.

Summary

Terms
  • Cartesian product – The Cartesian product (also cross product) of two sets is the set of all ordered pairs.
  • Intervals – Intervals are the connected pieces of the number line.
  • open, closed, half-open – For $a\le b$ the bounded sets $(a,b):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:a<x<b\}$ are called open, $[a,b]:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:a\le x\le b\}$ closed, and $(a,b]$ and $[a,b)$ half-open.
