The answer in three sentences

  1. A function is an assignment that gives each element of its domain (the set of admissible inputs) exactly one image. A formula is only one way of specifying it.

  2. The equation $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$ has exactly one solution for every $y$ if $f$ is bijective, that is, if no $y$ is hit twice and none is missed. Then the inverse function $f^{-1}$ exists, and its graph (the picture of the assignment in the plane) is the graph of $f$ reflected in $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x}$.

  3. A polynomial is determined by its coefficients (the fixed numbers in front of the powers). Dividing it by $x-x_0$ leaves the remainder $P(x_0)$. Therefore every zero splits off a linear factor, and a polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ zeros.

Key points

  • Function $f:A\to B$: exactly one image for each $a\in A$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal D(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{A}$, $\mathcal W(f)\subset B$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal G(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{(a,f(a))\}}$.

  • Even: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{f(-x)}$, odd: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-f(-x)}$. Strictly increasing: $x<y\Rightarrow f(x)<f(y)$. Bounded: $|f|\le K$.

  • Injective: at most, surjective: at least, bijective: exactly one solution of $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$. Strictly monotonic implies bijective onto $\mathcal W(f)$.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(g\circ f)(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g(f(x))}$. Injective: There is $g$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(f(x))}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x}$. Surjective: There is $g$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(g(y))}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y}$. Bijective: Exactly one $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{f^{-1}}$ does both.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{y\Leftrightarrow x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{f^{-1}(y)}$. Graph: reflection in $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x}$. $f^{-1}(y)\neq1/f(y)$. Monotonicity is inherited.

  • Polynomial $\sum_{i=0}^na_ix^i$, $a_n\neq0$. Equal for all $x$ $\Leftrightarrow$ equal coefficients.

  • Division: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_{n-m}Q_m+T_k}$ with $k<m$ or $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T_k}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

  • Horner: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a_n}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{c_{i+1}x_0+a_i}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_n(x_0)}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x-x_0)Q_{n-1}(x)+c_0}$.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_n(x_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0\Leftrightarrow}$ factor $x-x_0$; at most $n$ zeros. $m$-fold: $(x-x_0)^mQ_{n-m}$, $Q_{n-m}(x_0)\neq0$; odd $m$ crosses, even $m$ touches.

  • Pole: denominator zero, numerator not after canceling. Gap: The factor cancels. For large $|x|$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{ S}$.

Looking ahead to Chapter 4. “Growing beyond every bound” and “approaching” have remained descriptions here. Chapter 4 turns them into the limit, first for sequences.

One principle behind all the results. A function gives exactly one $y$ for each $x$. Almost everything in this chapter asks the reverse question: How many $x$ belong to a given $y$?
