What this stage is about:

Stage 3 provides a real product. This stage turns it into division, shows that $\mathbb{C}$ is a field, and explains why the order is missing.

The idea. Dividing by a real number is possible: Real part and imaginary part are divided individually. So you multiply the top and bottom of the fraction by the conjugate of the denominator. The value stays the same, the denominator becomes real. In the plane, $1/z$ arises in two steps: reflect across the real axis, then divide by $x^2+y^2$ (Fig. 4). In exactly this way AC analysis splits fractions of complex impedances into real and imaginary parts.

Theorem 4.1 (Reciprocal and division)

For every $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x+\mathrm{i}y\ne0}$ there is the number

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\overline{z}}{z\overline{z}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{x}{x^2+y^2}-\mathrm{i}\,\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}}$$
$$\text{with}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z\cdot z^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.}$$
(4.1)

For arbitrary $w$, this gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\dfrac wz}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{w\cdot z^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{w\,\overline{z}}{x^2+y^2}}$.

⁠How to read it: The denominator $x^2+y^2$ is real. Validity: Because $z\ne0$, either $x\ne0$ or $y\ne0$, so $x^2+y^2>0$. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ there is no reciprocal, because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0\cdot w}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0\ne1}$ for every $w$. Limiting case: For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, (4.1) becomes the real reciprocal $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x/x^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/x}$.

Proof: Product with the reciprocal

Let $N:=x^2+y^2>0$. The reciprocal is the pair $(x/N,\,-y/N)$. By (1.2),

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(x,y)\odot\Bigl(\frac xN,\frac{-y}N\Bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Bigl(\frac{x\cdot x}N-\frac{y\cdot(-y)}N,\;\frac{x\cdot(-y)}N+\frac{y\cdot x}N\Bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Bigl(\frac{x^2+y^2}N,\;0\Bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1,0).}$$

Example

With the reference model, denominator $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z_2\overline{z_2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{z_1}{z_2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{(3+\mathrm{i})(1-2\mathrm{i})}{(1+2\mathrm{i})(1-2\mathrm{i})}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{3-6\mathrm{i}+\mathrm{i}-2\mathrm{i}^2}{5}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{5-5\mathrm{i}}{5}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-\mathrm{i}.}$$

Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(1-\mathrm{i})(1+2\mathrm{i})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+2\mathrm{i}-\mathrm{i}-2\mathrm{i}^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3+\mathrm{i}}$. Moreover, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1/z_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1-2\mathrm{i})/5}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2-0.4\mathrm{i}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1/\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\mathrm{i}}$.

Theorem 4.2 ($\mathbb{C}$ is a field)

$\mathbb{C}$ with $+$ and $\cdot$ satisfies all field axioms from Chapter 1: commutative, associative and distributive laws, neutral elements $0\ne1$, the negative $-z$ for every $z$ and the reciprocal $1/z$ for every $z\ne0$.

⁠How to read it: What follows in Chapter 1 from the field axioms alone also holds in $\mathbb{C}$: arithmetic with fractions, the binomial formulas and the inference from $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{zw}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ or $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{w}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

Proof: Idea

Addition works separately and inherits all laws from $\mathbb{R}$, with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-x-\mathrm{i}y}$. (1.2) does not change when the indices 1 and 2 are swapped: Multiplication is commutative. The associative and distributive laws are shown by computing both sides according to (1.2). $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(1,0)\odot(x,y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x,y)}$ shows the unit element, (4.1) the axiom (M4), the reciprocal.

Theorem 4.3 ($\mathbb{C}$ is not ordered)

There is no relation “$>0$” on $\mathbb{C}$ with the order axioms from Chapter 1: (T1) For every $x$, exactly one of $x>0$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $-x>0$ holds. (T2) From $x>0$ and $y>0$ follows $x+y>0$. (T3) From $x>0$ and $y>0$ follows $x\cdot y>0$.

⁠How to read it: For non-real numbers, $z<w$ and $z>0$ have no meaning. Only real quantities such as $\operatorname{Re}(z)$ or $|z|$ may be compared.

Proof: Contradiction

Suppose there were such a relation. Then $x\cdot x>0$ would hold for every $x\ne0$: For $x>0$ this follows from (T3), for $-x>0$ from (T3) and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(-x)(-x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x\cdot x}$. In particular, $1\cdot1>0$ and $\mathrm{i}\cdot\mathrm{i}>0$. With (T2) it would follow that $1\cdot1+\mathrm{i}\cdot\mathrm{i}>0$. That is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1+(-1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0>0}$, a contradiction to (T1).

QuestionCouldn't one fix a ranking, say first by the real part, then by the imaginary part?

You can sort that way. It just does not get along with multiplication: It would give $\mathrm{i}>0$, but $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{i}\cdot\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1<0}$, against (T3). The proof shows that this hits every possible choice.

Exercise 4 ★ Dividing

(a) Bring $\dfrac{7+\mathrm{i}}{2-\mathrm{i}}$ to standard form. (b) Compute $\dfrac{10}{1+3\mathrm{i}}+\dfrac{3-\mathrm{i}}{1+\mathrm{i}}$. (c) Solve $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(2+\mathrm{i})z-3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2+3\mathrm{i}}$ and check the result.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\dfrac{(7+\mathrm{i})(2+\mathrm{i})}{(2-\mathrm{i})(2+\mathrm{i})}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{14+7\mathrm{i}+2\mathrm{i}+\mathrm{i}^2}{5}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{13+9\mathrm{i}}5}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.6+1.8\mathrm{i}}$.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\dfrac{10(1-3\mathrm{i})}{10}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-3\mathrm{i}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\dfrac{(3-\mathrm{i})(1-\mathrm{i})}{2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{3-3\mathrm{i}-\mathrm{i}+\mathrm{i}^2}2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{2-4\mathrm{i}}2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-2\mathrm{i}}$. The sum is $2-5\mathrm{i}$.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(2+\mathrm{i})z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+3\mathrm{i}}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{(1+3\mathrm{i})(2-\mathrm{i})}{5}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{2-\mathrm{i}+6\mathrm{i}-3\mathrm{i}^2}5}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{5+5\mathrm{i}}5}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+\mathrm{i}}$. Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(2+\mathrm{i})(1+\mathrm{i})-3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+3\mathrm{i}-3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2+3\mathrm{i}}$.

Exercise 5 ★★ An attempt to order $\mathbb{C}$

Someone calls $z$ positive if $\operatorname{Re}(z)>0$. Check (T1), (T2) and (T3) and give a counterexample for each violated axiom.

Solution

(T1) is violated: For $\mathrm{i}$ we have $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Re}(\mathrm{i})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Re}(-\mathrm{i})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, so neither $\mathrm{i}$ nor $-\mathrm{i}$ is positive, although $\mathrm{i}\ne0$. (T2) holds, because by Stage 3, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Re}(z+w)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}(z)+\operatorname{Re}(w)>0}$. (T3) is violated: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+2\mathrm{i}}$ is positive, but $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z\cdot z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-3+4\mathrm{i}}$ has the real part $-3$. The reason is the term $-y_1y_2$ in the real part of the product.
