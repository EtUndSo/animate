The answer in three sentences

  1. A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x+\mathrm{i}y}$. The imaginary unit $\mathrm{i}$ is the pair $(0,1)$ and satisfies $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{i}^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$. All pairs together form the complex plane, in which $\mathbb{R}$ is the horizontal axis.

  2. You calculate as in $\mathbb{R}$ and replace $\mathrm{i}^2$ by $-1$. That is why $\mathbb{C}$ is a field (a set in which the four basic operations follow the familiar laws). What is lost is the order: There is no “greater than” between complex numbers.

  3. The complex conjugate $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\overline{z}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x-\mathrm{i}y}$ is the mirror image of $z$ across the real axis, and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z\overline{z}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2+y^2}$ is real. From this follow division, the absolute value $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|z|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}$ (distance from the origin) and the triangle inequality $|z+w|\le|z|+|w|$ (a detour is never shorter).

Key points

  • A complex number is a pair: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x+\mathrm{i}y}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{i}^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$. You calculate as in $\mathbb{R}$.

  • Addition is the parallelogram, multiplication by $\mathrm{i}$ rotates by $90^\circ$.

  • Conjugating reflects: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\overline{z}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x-\mathrm{i}y}$, and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z\overline{z}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2+y^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|z|^2}$.

  • Division: Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{w/z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{w\overline{z}/|z|^2}$.

  • $\mathbb{C}$ is a field, but not ordered. Inequalities stand only between real quantities.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|z|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d(z,w)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|z-w|}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|zw|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|z||w|}$, $|z+w|\le|z|+|w|$.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z^2+pz+q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ always has solutions in $\mathbb{C}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-p/2\pm w}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{w^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{p^2/4-q}$.

One principle behind all the results. Everything in this chapter follows from one rule: Calculate with $\mathrm{i}$ as with a letter and replace $\mathrm{i}^2$ by $-1$.
