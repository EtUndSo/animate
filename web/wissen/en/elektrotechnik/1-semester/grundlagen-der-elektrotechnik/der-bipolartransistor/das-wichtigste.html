The answer in three sentences

  1. The emitter gives off electrons, the thin base lets them through, the collector collects them. The voltage $U_\mathrm{BE}$ decides how many electrons set off: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{S}\,\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm{BE}/U_T}}$. The field of the blocking collector diode sweeps them out, and only a small remainder flows through the base, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{C}/B}$. The factor $B$ is called the current gain.

  2. Three terminals mean two independent voltages. That is why $I_\mathrm{C}$ is a surface over $U_\mathrm{BE}$ and $U_\mathrm{CE}$, and each characteristic curve is a slice of it. On this surface the external circuit selects the operating point, the DC state of the transistor. An emitter resistor holds it in place through negative feedback: the effect weakens its own cause.

  3. For small signals, the tangent replaces the curve. Its slope, the transconductance $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{C}/U_T}$, turns the transistor into a controlled current source. The voltage gain of the common-emitter circuit, the standard circuit with the emitter at ground, is the DC voltage across the collector resistor divided by $U_T$.

Key points

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{B}+I_\mathrm{C}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CB}+U_\mathrm{BE}}$. Three terminals mean two free voltages, $I_\mathrm{C}$ is a surface, each characteristic curve a slice.

  • Mechanism: $U_\mathrm{BE}$ lowers the barrier, electrons cross the thin base, the field of the blocking CB diode sweeps them out: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{S}\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm{BE}/U_T}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{C}/B}$. The base current only replaces the few losses.

  • Regions: cut-off region (open), active ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{B\,I_\mathrm{B}}$, $U_\mathrm{CE}\ge0.2$ V), saturation ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2}$ V, $I_\mathrm{C}\le B\,I_\mathrm{B}$). Assume, calculate, check. Switch: ends of the load line. Amplifier: active, in the middle.

  • Negative feedback: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{BE})/\bigl(R_\mathrm{E}(1+1/B)+R_\mathrm{i}/B\bigr)}$. More current raises the emitter and takes $U_\mathrm{BE}$ back. Current mirror: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{ref}/(1+2/B)}$. Regulator: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{Z}-U_\mathrm{BE}}$.

  • Small signal: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{C}/U_T}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{B/S}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{ U_\mathrm{Early}/I_\mathrm{C}}$. The tangent holds for $|u_\mathrm{be}|\ll U_T$.

  • Common emitter $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-U_{R_\mathrm{C}}/U_T}$, common collector $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, common base $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{+S\,R_\mathrm{C}}$. With $R_\mathrm{E}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-R_\mathrm{C}/R_\mathrm{E}}$.

One principle behind all the results. The transistor has two boundaries with two tasks. Control happens at the base-emitter boundary: the voltage there sets exponentially how many electrons start. The collector-base boundary only collects: whatever arrives is swept out, and the voltage there hardly matters.
