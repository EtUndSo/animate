What this stage is about:

Which region applies is not known in advance. This stage determines the operating point by assuming and checking, and draws it as an intersection.

The idea. The transistor supplies its characteristic curves, the circuit supplies mesh equations. Both must hold at once. The result is the operating point (OP), as in Chapter 5 the intersection of a characteristic curve with a straight line. Because each region has a different equivalent circuit, one assumes a region, calculates linearly and checks. A contradiction with the conditions (4.1) to (4.3) reveals the wrong assumption.

Theorem 5.1 (Operating point of the circuit with base and collector resistor)

The input mesh is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{B}I_\mathrm{B}+U_\mathrm{BE}}$, the output mesh is the load line $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CC}-R_\mathrm{C}I_\mathrm{C}}$. For $U_\mathrm{in}\le U_\mathrm{BE,on}$ the transistor blocks and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{in}}$. For $U_\mathrm{in}>U_\mathrm{BE,on}$, in both conducting regions

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{in}-U_\mathrm{BE,on}}{R_\mathrm{B}}.}$$
(5.1)

Assuming active operation gives

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{B\,I_\mathrm{B},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CC}-B\,I_\mathrm{B}R_\mathrm{C}.}$$
(5.2)

If the result is $U_\mathrm{CE}\ge U_\mathrm{CE,sat}$, the assumption is correct. Otherwise saturation applies, with

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CE,sat},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{CC}-U_\mathrm{CE,sat}}{R_\mathrm{C}}<B\,I_\mathrm{B}.}$$
(5.3)

⁠How to read it: $U_\mathrm{CC}$ is the supply voltage, $U_\mathrm{in}$ the input voltage against ground. $R_\mathrm{B}$ sets the base current, $R_\mathrm{C}$ converts the collector current into a voltage. Validity: emitter at ground, the linear model from Stage 4. Equation (5.1) holds in both conducting regions because the same 0.6 V source lies between B and E there.

Example

Reference model: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(5\,\mathrm{V}-0.6\,\mathrm{V})/440\,\text{k}\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ µ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12\,\mathrm{V}-6\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V. That is more than 0.2 V, so active (point A). A wrong first assumption does no harm: saturation would give $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.93}$ mA, but $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B\,I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ mA is smaller, in contradiction to (4.3). For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{25.9}$ µ A. Assuming active operation would yield $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-3.5}$ V, a contradiction. In saturation, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.93}$ mA and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B\,I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.18}$ mA (point S).

⁠Limiting cases: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CC}}$, and the output is rigid. $U_\mathrm{in}<0.6$ V gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ V (point Z).

The middle of the active region. In the active region, $U_\mathrm{CE}$ lies between 0.2 V and $U_\mathrm{CC}$. So that a signal has equal room on both sides, the point is placed in the middle. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Delta U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CC}-U_\mathrm{CE,sat}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{11.8}$ V,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE,OP}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CE,sat}+\frac{\Delta U}{2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6.1\,\mathrm{V},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{CC}-U_\mathrm{CE,OP}}{R_\mathrm{C}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.97\,\mathrm{mA}.}$$
(5.4)

From this follows $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(U_\mathrm{in}-U_\mathrm{BE})\,B/I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{447}$ k$\Omega$. The 440 k$\Omega$ of the reference model lie almost there.

With an emitter resistor. If the base lies directly at $U_\mathrm{in}$ and the emitter goes to ground via $R_\mathrm{E}$, the input mesh reads $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{BE,on}+R_\mathrm{E}I_\mathrm{E}}$. For $U_\mathrm{in}>0.6$ V the transistor does not block, and

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{in}-U_\mathrm{BE,on}}{R_\mathrm{E}},\quad I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{I_\mathrm{E}}{1+B},\quad I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{B\,I_\mathrm{B},\quad U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CC}-R_\mathrm{C}I_\mathrm{C}-R_\mathrm{E}I_\mathrm{E}.}$$
(5.5)

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.6}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.99}$ mA and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.03}$ V. Here $B$ came into play only in the last step and changes almost nothing. Stage 7 makes use of this.

Exercise 5 ★★ Calculating the operating point

Given are T, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{220}$ k$\Omega$. (a) Determine the operating point for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V. (b) Determine it for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8}$ V. (c) Which $R_\mathrm{B}$ places the point in the middle of the active region at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.4\,\mathrm{V}/220\,\text{k}\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10.9}$ µ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.18}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\mathrm{V}-4.36\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.64}$ V: active.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{33.6}$ µ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B\,I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6.73}$ mA. Assuming active operation would give $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-3.45}$ V, a contradiction. So saturation: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9.8\,\mathrm{V}/2\,\text{k}\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.9}$ mA $<6.73$ mA.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE,OP}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2\,\mathrm{V}+4.9\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.1}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.9\,\mathrm{V}/2\,\text{k}\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.45}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12.25}$ µ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.4\,\mathrm{V}/12.25\,\mu\text{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{196}$ k$\Omega$.

Exercise 6 ★★ Emitter resistor

The base of T lies directly at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.6}$ V, with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{500\,\Omega}$. (a) Determine $I_\mathrm{E}$, $I_\mathrm{B}$, $I_\mathrm{C}$ and $U_\mathrm{CE}$. (b) What changes if $B$ falls to 100?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\,\mathrm{V}/500\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\mathrm{mA}/201}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9.95}$ µ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.99}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12\,\mathrm{V}-3.98\,\mathrm{V}-1\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{7.02}$ V.

(b) $I_\mathrm{E}$ stays at 2 mA, because it depends only on $U_\mathrm{in}$ and $R_\mathrm{E}$. $I_\mathrm{B}$ becomes 19.8 µ A, $I_\mathrm{C}$ becomes 1.98 mA, $U_\mathrm{CE}$ becomes 7.04 V. Half of $B$, but only 0.5 % less collector current.
