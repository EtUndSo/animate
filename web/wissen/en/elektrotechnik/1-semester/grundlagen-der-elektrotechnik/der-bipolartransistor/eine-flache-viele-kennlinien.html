Stage 3: One surface, many characteristic curves

DEENES中文
What this stage is about:

Data sheets show several characteristic curves with different axes. This stage traces them back to one object.

The idea. $I_\mathrm{C}$ depends on two voltages. A function of two variables is a surface, like a terrain over a map: the map has the directions $U_\mathrm{BE}$ and $U_\mathrm{CE}$, the height of the terrain is $I_\mathrm{C}$ (Fig. 3). A characteristic curve (relationship between two quantities, Part 5) arises when one voltage is held fixed: a slice through the terrain. Several parallel slices give a family of curves. So there are not many dependencies, but one terrain with several views. In the direction of $U_\mathrm{BE}$ it is a steep slope (red), in the direction of $U_\mathrm{CE}$ an edge and then an almost horizontal plane (green).

Theorem 3.1 (The model surface)

For $U_\mathrm{BE}\gg U_T$ and $U_\mathrm{CE}\ge0$, the reference model has

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\underbrace{I_\mathrm{S}\,\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm{BE}/U_T}}_{\text{injection}}\cdot\underbrace{\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-U_\mathrm{CE}/U_T}\bigr)}_{\text{collection}}\cdot\underbrace{\Bigl(1+\frac{U_\mathrm{CE}-6\,\mathrm{V}}{80\,\mathrm{V}}\Bigr)}_{\text{small slope}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{I_\mathrm{S}}{B}\,\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm{BE}/U_T}.}$$
(3.1)

⁠How to read it: The first factor is (2.1). The second tends to 1 as soon as the collector lies a few $U_T$ above the emitter: it counts how many electrons the collector sends back, which is 15 % at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.05}$ V, still 2 % at 0.1 V and only 0.05 % at 0.2 V. The third factor describes the slight increase with $U_\mathrm{CE}$ (Stage 9). $I_\mathrm{S}$ and $B$ apply at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V. Validity: a model for small and medium currents.

Derivation: The factor $1-\mathrm{e}^{-U_\mathrm{CE}/U_T}$

The electron current through the base is proportional to the density gradient between its two edges. Each edge follows the voltage at its boundary: $\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm{BE}/U_T}$ at the emitter, $\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm{BC}/U_T}$ at the collector, with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{BC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-U_\mathrm{CB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{BE}-U_\mathrm{CE}}$ from (1.1). So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}\sim\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm{BE}/U_T}-\mathrm{e}^{(U_\mathrm{BE}-U_\mathrm{CE})/U_T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm{BE}/U_T}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-U_\mathrm{CE}/U_T})}$. The exponential function in $U_\mathrm{BE}$ and the edge in $U_\mathrm{CE}$ are two sides of one difference.

Characteristicheld fixedaxesshape and reason
Input characteristic$U_\mathrm{CE}$$I_\mathrm{B}$ over $U_\mathrm{BE}$Diode, knee around 0.6 V: the BE boundary is a diode
Control characteristic$U_\mathrm{CE}$$I_\mathrm{C}$ over $U_\mathrm{BE}$Same shape, $B$ times higher: injection
Current control characteristic$U_\mathrm{CE}$$I_\mathrm{C}$ over $I_\mathrm{B}$Straight line with slope $B$: the exponential function cancels out
Output characteristic$U_\mathrm{BE}$ or $I_\mathrm{B}$$I_\mathrm{C}$ over $U_\mathrm{CE}$Steep, then almost flat like a current source: collection

The current control characteristic is also called the transfer characteristic. At very large currents it flattens and $B$ falls: the base is then so full of electrons that the simple calculation no longer holds. The boundary $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{BE}}$ in Fig. 4 is the locus $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. To the left of it the CB diode already conducts weakly, but the curve only bends down below around 0.2 V.

Example. Slice at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V: 0.582 V, 0.600 V and 0.618 V give 1.0 mA, 2.0 mA and 4.0 mA. Slice at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6}$ V: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.05}$ V; 0.2 V; 6 V; 14 V give 1.58 mA; 1.85 mA; 2.00 mA; 2.20 mA. Limiting case: for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: both edges of the base lie at the same height, and there is no gradient.

QuestionSo does the current depend on $U_\mathrm{CE}$ or not?

Yes, but to very different degrees. Strongly up to 0.2 V, after that by around 1 % per volt. For comparison: 1 mV at $U_\mathrm{BE}$ has an effect of 3.9 %. “Depends” does not mean “depends equally strongly”. The terrain has a slope in both directions; they are just very different.

Exercise 3 ★ Reading slices

(a) From (3.1), determine $I_\mathrm{C}$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6}$ V for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.1}$ V and 12 V. Which characteristic are you calculating? (b) Determine $I_\mathrm{C}$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.56}$ V and 0.62 V. Which characteristic is this? (c) Predict: What does the curve $I_\mathrm{C}(I_\mathrm{B})$ look like at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.05}$ V, compared with the one at 6 V?

Solution

(a) $1.81$ mA and $2.15$ mA, a slice at fixed $U_\mathrm{BE}$: output characteristic. (b) 0.43 mA and 4.32 mA, a slice at fixed $U_\mathrm{CE}$: control characteristic. 60 mV make the factor of 10. (c) Again a straight line through the origin, only flatter. $I_\mathrm{B}$ in (3.1) does not depend on $U_\mathrm{CE}$, and the other two factors are constants at fixed $U_\mathrm{CE}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0.854\cdot0.926}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.79}$, so the slope is 158 instead of 200.
