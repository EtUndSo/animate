In n-doped silicon, electrons are free to move; in p-doped silicon, holes are: missing electrons that move like positive charges. At the boundary between the two regions, the pn junction, the space-charge region forms: a strip with almost no free carriers, in which fixed ions produce an electric field from the n region to the p region. This field acts as a barrier for the majority carriers of each side. Plus at the p region lowers it, and the diode conducts in the forward direction. Plus at the n region raises it, and the diode blocks. Diffusion is the motion of particles from a dense place to a sparse one; recombination is the encounter of an electron with a hole, in which both disappear as free carriers. For $U\gg U_T$ the diode follows the equation $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{S}\,\mathrm{e}^{U/U_T}}$, and at the operating point one calculates with the tangent to its characteristic curve.
