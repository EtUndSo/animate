Stage 1: Three layers, two voltages

What this stage is about:

A diode has two terminals and one characteristic curve. The transistor has three terminals. This stage explains its structure and counts how many quantities are free.

The idea. An npn transistor consists of three regions of the same semiconductor: n-doped emitter, p-doped base, n-doped collector (Fig. 1). The emitter is the most heavily doped, so that it can give off many electrons. The collector is the most lightly doped, so that its space-charge region becomes wide and withstands high voltages. The base lies between them and is very narrow, around 1 µ m. This creates two pn junctions that share the base: the BE diode and the CB diode. In normal operation, the BE diode conducts and the CB diode blocks. Swapping n and p gives the pnp transistor (Stage 4). In the circuit symbol, the symbol in the schematic, the emitter is the terminal with the arrow. The arrow shows the direction of the conventional current.

Definition 1.1 (Currents and voltages at the npn transistor)

$I_\mathrm{B}$ and $I_\mathrm{C}$ are counted into the component, $I_\mathrm{E}$ out of it. Voltages are potential differences, and the first index is the plus pole: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_\mathrm{B}-\varphi_\mathrm{E}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_\mathrm{C}-\varphi_\mathrm{E}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_\mathrm{C}-\varphi_\mathrm{B}}$. It always holds that

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{B}+I_\mathrm{C},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CB}+U_\mathrm{BE}.}$$
(1.1)

⁠How to read it: $I_\mathrm{C}$ is the collector current, from microamperes to amperes depending on the type. $I_\mathrm{B}$ is the much smaller base current, $I_\mathrm{E}$ the emitter current. $U_\mathrm{BE}$ is across the BE diode, around 0.6 V. $U_\mathrm{CE}$ ranges from around 0.2 V up to several tens of volts. $U_\mathrm{CB}$ is the reverse voltage of the CB diode. Validity: the node and mesh rules hold in every operating state.

Derivation: How many quantities are free

A component with $n$ terminals has $n$ currents. It stores no charge, so the node rule (Chapter 2) removes one current. It has $n$ potentials, but only differences count, so $n-1$ voltages remain. The diode ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$) has one voltage, one current and one curve. The transistor ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$) has two voltages and two currents. Its interior links them through two equations,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{f(U_\mathrm{BE},U_\mathrm{CE}),}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g(U_\mathrm{BE},U_\mathrm{CE}).}$$

So it is a two-port (Chapter 6) whose two ports share the emitter.

Example

In the reference model, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V. This gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.4}$ V. The BE diode conducts because its p side is higher. The CB diode blocks because its n side is 5.4 V higher. From $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ mA and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ µ A follows $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.01}$ mA.

⁠Limiting case: for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{BE}}$. This is where normal operation ends.

QuestionCan I solder a transistor together from two diodes?

No. The equivalent picture of two diodes only shows the polarity of the boundaries. In two separate diodes, every electron recombines long before it reaches the second depletion layer, and the blocking diode stays blocked. The transistor effect arises only because the two boundaries lie closer together than the distance an electron survives in the p region.

Exercise 1 ★ From potential to voltage

Measured against ground are $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.2}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.85}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{7}$ V, as well as $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{15}$ µ A and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ mA. (a) Determine $U_\mathrm{BE}$, $U_\mathrm{CE}$, $U_\mathrm{CB}$ and check (1.1). (b) How large is $I_\mathrm{E}$? (c) To what value must $\varphi_\mathrm{C}$ fall so that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.65}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.8}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.15}$ V. Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{5.15\,\mathrm{V}+0.65\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.8}$ V. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.015}$ mA. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ means $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.85}$ V.
