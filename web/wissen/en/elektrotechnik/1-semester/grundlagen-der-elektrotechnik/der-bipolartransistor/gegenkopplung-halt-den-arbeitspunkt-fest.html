What this stage is about:

The operating point from Stage 5 depends on $B$ and on $U_\mathrm{BE}$, and both are unreliable. This stage shows how the circuit holds the current in place by itself.

The idea. In the reference model, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{B\cdot10}$ µ A. A specimen with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$ carries 1 mA ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9}$ V), one with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{400}$ reaches saturation. A fixed voltage $U_\mathrm{BE}$ would be worse: at fixed current, $U_\mathrm{BE}$ falls by around 2 mV per kelvin, and 18 mV double the current, so as little as 9 K of warming already doubles $I_\mathrm{C}$.

The remedy is a resistor in the emitter. A divider holds the base potential almost fixed. The current now runs in a loop against itself:

$I_\mathrm{C}$ rises $\to$ voltage across $R_\mathrm{E}$ rises $\to$ $\varphi_\mathrm{E}$ rises $\to$ $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_\mathrm{B}-\varphi_\mathrm{E}}$ falls $\to$ less injection $\to$ $I_\mathrm{C}$ falls.

The effect weakens its cause: this is negative feedback, here current feedback, because the current through $R_\mathrm{E}$ produces the opposing voltage. In the end $U_\mathrm{BE}$ changes only by millivolts, and the current follows from Ohm's law at $R_\mathrm{E}$ instead of from the exponential function. The second form connects $R_\mathrm{B}$ to the collector (Fig. 8, center). If $I_\mathrm{C}$ rises, $U_\mathrm{CE}$ falls, and with it $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(U_\mathrm{CE}-U_\mathrm{BE})/R_\mathrm{B}}$ falls. This is voltage feedback.

Theorem 7.1 (Operating point with negative feedback)

Current feedback with the divider $R_1$, $R_2$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_1\parallel R_2}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{CC}\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2},\quad I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{BE}}{(1+B)R_\mathrm{E}+R_\mathrm{i}},\quad I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{BE}}{R_\mathrm{E}\bigl(1+\frac1B\bigr)+\frac{R_\mathrm{i}}{B}}.}$$
(7.1)

For $R_\mathrm{E}\gg R_\mathrm{i}/B$ and $B\gg1$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{BE})/R_\mathrm{E}}$; if in addition $U_\mathrm{q}\gg U_\mathrm{BE}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{ U_\mathrm{CC}R_2/\bigl(R_\mathrm{E}(R_1+R_2)\bigr)}$.

Voltage feedback with $R_\mathrm{B}$ from the collector to the base:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{CC}-U_\mathrm{BE}}{R_\mathrm{C}\bigl(1+\frac1B\bigr)+\frac{R_\mathrm{B}}{B}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{CC}-U_\mathrm{BE}}{R_\mathrm{C}+\frac{R_\mathrm{B}}{B}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\text{for } R_\mathrm{C}\gg\frac{R_\mathrm{B}}{B}:\ I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{CC}-U_\mathrm{BE}}{R_\mathrm{C}}.}$$
(7.2)

⁠How to read it: $U_\mathrm{q}$ and $R_\mathrm{i}$ describe the divider as an equivalent voltage source (Chapter 5): $U_\mathrm{q}$ is its open-circuit voltage, $R_\mathrm{i}$ its internal resistance. In the denominator, $R_\mathrm{E}$ acts almost fully, $R_\mathrm{i}$ only divided by $B$. Validity: active region. This must be checked at the end. $U_\mathrm{BE}$ is a fixed value here; the last approximation in (7.1) needs a large $U_\mathrm{q}$.

Derivation: The formulas from the meshes

The divider becomes the source $U_\mathrm{q}$ with $R_\mathrm{i}$ in series. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1+B)I_\mathrm{B}}$, the mesh through base and emitter reads

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{B}R_\mathrm{i}+U_\mathrm{BE}+I_\mathrm{B}(1+B)R_\mathrm{E}.}$$

Solving for $I_\mathrm{B}$ and multiplying by $B$ gives (7.1). With voltage feedback, the current $I_\mathrm{C}+I_\mathrm{B}$ flows through $R_\mathrm{C}$, and only $I_\mathrm{B}$ through $R_\mathrm{B}$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{C}/B}$, the mesh reads

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CC}-U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Bigl(\frac{I_\mathrm{C}}{B}+I_\mathrm{C}\Bigr)R_\mathrm{C}+\frac{I_\mathrm{C}}{B}R_\mathrm{B}.}$$

Solving for $I_\mathrm{C}$ gives (7.2).

Example

Reference model: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.7}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6.98}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}/B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{35\,\Omega\ll1}$ k$\Omega$. This gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.1\,\mathrm{V}/1040\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.02}$ mA and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12\,\mathrm{V}-6.06\,\mathrm{V}-2.03\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.91}$ V, so active. The base potential is 2.63 V, and the divider carries 300 µ A, 30 times $I_\mathrm{B}$.

Temperature, too, has hardly any effect now: 50 K of warming lowers $U_\mathrm{BE}$ by 0.1 V, and with (7.1) $I_\mathrm{C}$ rises by only 4.8 %. At a fixed voltage $U_\mathrm{BE}$ it would be 47 times the value. Voltage feedback achieves less, because here $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{B}/B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.7}$ k$\Omega$ is not small compared with 3 k$\Omega$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$ k$\Omega$, $I_\mathrm{C}$ varies only between 2.83 mA and 3.50 mA, but then only $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.2}$ V remains.

⁠Limiting cases: $R_\mathrm{E}\to0$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{B(U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{BE})/R_\mathrm{i}}$, the formula with base resistor from Stage 5. $B\to\infty$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{BE})/R_\mathrm{E}}$.

QuestionWhat does the emitter resistor cost?

Two things. The voltage across $R_\mathrm{E}$, here 2 V, is missing at the collector for the signal swing. And the negative feedback also acts on the signal: the voltage gain falls sharply (Stage 10). A capacitor in parallel with $R_\mathrm{E}$ acts like a wire for AC signals and removes the second drawback.

Exercise 8 ★★★ Designing the operating point

Using T, design a current feedback circuit for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{15}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ mA, 2 V across $R_\mathrm{E}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V. (a) Determine $R_\mathrm{E}$ and $R_\mathrm{C}$ as round values. (b) $10\,I_\mathrm{B}$ are to flow through $R_2$. Determine $R_2$ and $R_1$ (round to 52 k$\Omega$ and 225 k$\Omega$). (c) Calculate $I_\mathrm{C}$ for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$, 200, 400 and compare with the circuit from the table. Why is the spread larger? Predict the result before you calculate.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\mathrm{V}/1\,\mathrm{mA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(15-6-2)\,\mathrm{V}/1\,\mathrm{mA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{7}$ k$\Omega$.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ µ A, base potential 2.6 V. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.6\,\mathrm{V}/50\,\mu\text{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{52}$ k$\Omega$. Through $R_1$ flow 55 µ A: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12.4\,\mathrm{V}/55\,\mu\text{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{225}$ k$\Omega$.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.82}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{42.2}$ k$\Omega$. With (7.1), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.91}$ mA; 1.00 mA; 1.05 mA. Prediction: the spread is larger because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}/B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{211\,\Omega}$ is no longer small compared with $R_\mathrm{E}$. Here the divider carries only ten times $I_\mathrm{B}$ instead of thirty times. LTspice: 0.92 mA; 1.01 mA; 1.06 mA.

Exercise 9 ★★ Voltage feedback

Take the voltage feedback from the example. (a) Check $I_\mathrm{C}$ and $U_\mathrm{CE}$ for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{200}$. (b) Limiting case $R_\mathrm{B}\to0$: What values do $U_\mathrm{CE}$ and $I_\mathrm{C}$ have? Is the transistor then active? Answer with (4.3) and the meaning of $U_\mathrm{CB}$.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{11.4\,\mathrm{V}/(3015\,\Omega+2700\,\Omega)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.99}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12\,\mathrm{V}-3\,\text{k}\Omega\cdot2.005\,\mathrm{mA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.99}$ V.

(b) Base and collector are connected: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{BE}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{11.4\,\mathrm{V}/3015\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.78}$ mA. Because $U_\mathrm{CE}>0.2$ V the transistor is active, exactly on the line $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{CB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. This is T1 in the current mirror.

Summary

Terms
  • current feedback – The effect weakens its cause: this is negative feedback, here current feedback, because the current through $R_\mathrm{E}$ produces the opposing voltage.
  • voltage feedback – If $I_\mathrm{C}$ rises, $U_\mathrm{CE}$ falls, and with it $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(U_\mathrm{CE}-U_\mathrm{BE})/R_\mathrm{B}}$ falls. This is voltage feedback.
