What this stage is about:

An unbalanced bridge has neither groups nor symmetry. The star-delta transformation exchanges a piece of the network so that groups arise.

The idea. A delta is three resistors between three terminals. A star is three resistors from three terminals to a common inner node, the star point. Both have three terminals and three numbers. With suitable values they cannot be told apart from outside. The exchange helps because the star point is a new node at which each star resistor lies in series with the neighbor behind it.

Theorem 3.1 (Star-delta transformation)

Terminals 1, 2, 3, star point 0. From delta to star:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{10}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{R_{12}R_{31}}{R_{12}+R_{23}+R_{31}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{20}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{R_{12}R_{23}}{R_{12}+R_{23}+R_{31}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{30}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{R_{23}R_{31}}{R_{12}+R_{23}+R_{31}}.}$$
(3.1)

From star to delta, with the conductances $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_{ij}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/R_{ij}}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{G_{10}G_{20}}{G_{10}+G_{20}+G_{30}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_{23}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{G_{20}G_{30}}{G_{10}+G_{20}+G_{30}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_{31}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{G_{30}G_{10}}{G_{10}+G_{20}+G_{30}}.}$$
(3.2)

⁠How to read it: $R_{10}$ leads from terminal 1 to the star point, $R_{12}$ from terminal 1 to terminal 2. The adjacent elements are the two elements that adjoin: for $R_{10}$ the delta sides at terminal 1, for $G_{12}$ the star branches at terminals 1 and 2. As a rule to remember: star resistance = product of the adjacent resistances divided by the perimeter resistance of the delta. Delta conductance = product of the adjacent conductances divided by the sum of the star conductances. Equivalent to (3.2) is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_{10}+R_{20}+R_{10}R_{20}/R_{30}}$ with cyclically permuted indices. Validity: The equality holds for any external circuit, but only at the three terminals. The star point is not a node in the original, and the currents in the resistors themselves differ.

Derivation: Star to delta, then invert

In the star, the node rule at the star point reads $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_{10}(\varphi_1-\varphi_0)+G_{20}(\varphi_2-\varphi_0)+G_{30}(\varphi_3-\varphi_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{G_{10}+G_{20}+G_{30}}$ it follows that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(G_{10}\varphi_1+G_{20}\varphi_2+G_{30}\varphi_3)/S}$. The current into terminal 1 is thus

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{G_{10}(\varphi_1-\varphi_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{G_{10}G_{20}}{S}(\varphi_1-\varphi_2)+\frac{G_{10}G_{30}}{S}(\varphi_1-\varphi_3).}$$

In the delta, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{G_{12}(\varphi_1-\varphi_2)+G_{31}(\varphi_1-\varphi_3)}$. Comparing the two gives (3.2), for arbitrary potentials. Terminal 2 gives $G_{23}$ in the same way.

For the inversion, let $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_{10}R_{20}+R_{20}R_{30}+R_{30}R_{10}}$. Then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H/(R_{10}R_{20}R_{30})}$, and (3.2) becomes $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H/R_{30}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{23}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H/R_{10}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{31}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H/R_{20}}$. From these follow $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{12}R_{31}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H^2/(R_{20}R_{30})}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{12}+R_{23}+R_{31}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H^2/(R_{10}R_{20}R_{30})}$. The quotient is $R_{10}$, which is (3.1).

Example

In Fig. 4, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{23}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_{31}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\Omega}$, and the perimeter is 50 $\Omega$. From (3.1) follow $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{10}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{20}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{30}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8\,\Omega}$. The bridge breaks down into groups:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\,\Omega+(4\,\Omega+20\,\Omega)\parallel(8\,\Omega+16\,\Omega)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\,\Omega+12\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{16\,\Omega.}$$

⁠Check at terminals 1, 2 with terminal 3 open: the star has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{4+4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8\,\Omega}$, the delta $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{10\,\Omega\parallel40\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8\,\Omega}$. Limiting cases: Three equal delta resistors give a star with one third of the value. For $R_{12}\to0$, $R_{10}$ and $R_{20}$ go to zero, and $R_{30}$ becomes $R_{23}\parallel R_{31}$, which is exactly what the short-circuited delta is.

$\pi$ and T. For two-ports (Chapter 6), delta and star are drawn with an input on the left and an output on the right. The delta is then called a $\pi$ network, the star a T network. The formulas and rules stay the same. In communications engineering this is how attenuators are built, which weaken a signal by a fixed factor and present the same input resistance at both terminal pairs (power matching, Chapter 7). Filters made of coils and capacitors can also be built in either form.

QuestionWhich delta should I convert?

Any one whose conversion creates groups. In a bridge the lower delta works too, or even a star converted into a delta. The result is always the same.

Exercise 4 ★★ There and back

(a) Three resistors of 12 $\Omega$ each form a delta. How large are the star resistors? (b) In a bridge, let the three upper resistors (delta A, C, D) be 6 $\Omega$ each; from C, 1 $\Omega$ and from D, 4 $\Omega$ lead to B. Calculate $R_\mathrm{AB}$. (c) Convert the star $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{10}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{20}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_{30}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$ into a delta, once with (3.2) and once with the resistance form.

Solution

(a) Perimeter 36 $\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{star}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{12\cdot12}{36}\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\,\Omega}$, one third of the delta value.

(b) The delta becomes three star resistors of 2 $\Omega$ each. Then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\Omega+(2\,\Omega+1\,\Omega)\parallel(2\,\Omega+4\,\Omega)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\Omega+3\,\Omega\parallel6\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\,\Omega}$.

(c) Conductances 0.2 S, 0.1 S, 0.1 S, sum 0.4 S. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{0.2\cdot0.1}{0.4}}$ S $=0.05$ S, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_{23}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.025}$ S, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_{31}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.05}$ S. These are $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{23}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{40\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{31}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\Omega}$. Resistance form: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5+10+\frac{5\cdot10}{10}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{23}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10+10+\frac{10\cdot10}{5}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{40\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{31}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10+5+\frac{10\cdot5}{10}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\Omega}$.
