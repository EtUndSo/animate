Stage 1: Combining resistors

What this stage is about:

A network of resistors without a source has, at two terminals, a characteristic curve through the origin, so one number is enough. This stage finds it by combining step by step.

The idea. A series-parallel circuit consists of series and parallel groups nested inside one another. Two resistors are in series if nothing else is connected to their common node. Then the same current flows through both, because the charge has nowhere to turn off. They are in parallel if they hang on the same two nodes. Then the same voltage lies across both. Each such group is itself a small one-port with a resistance as its characteristic curve. You replace it by this resistance, and the rest of the circuit notices nothing. You repeat this from the inside out.

Definition 1.1 (Total resistance)

The total resistance $R_\mathrm{AB}$ is the ratio $U_\mathrm{AB}/I_\mathrm{A}$ of a one-port without a source. For the network in Fig. 1,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{a}+R_\mathrm{b}\parallel\bigl(R_\mathrm{c}+R_\mathrm{d}\parallel R_\mathrm{e}\parallel R_\mathrm{f}\bigr) }\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{a}+\left[G_\mathrm{b}+\frac{1}{R_\mathrm{c}+\dfrac{1}{G_\mathrm{d}+G_\mathrm{e}+G_\mathrm{f}}}\right]^{-1}.}$$
(1.1)

⁠How to read it: $R$ is a resistance in $\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/R}$ its conductance in siemens (S). In series you add resistances, in parallel you add conductances. That is why each parallel group appears as a sum of conductances in the second form. The square bracket with the exponent $-1$ inverts the result again. Validity: The method needs genuine groups. If a third branch hangs on an intermediate node, the two neighbors are no longer in series (Stages 2 and 3).

Example

With the values from Fig. 1: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_\mathrm{d}+G_\mathrm{e}+G_\mathrm{f}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50+33.3+16.7}$ mS $=100$ mS, which is 10 $\Omega$. Adding $R_\mathrm{c}$ in series: 15 $\Omega$. In parallel with $R_\mathrm{b}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{30\,\Omega\parallel15\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$. Adding $R_\mathrm{a}$ in series: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{16\,\Omega}$. Working backward gives the currents: with 8 V across A and B, 0.5 A flows. Across $R_\mathrm{a}$ the voltage drops by 3 V, leaving 5 V for the rest. Through $R_\mathrm{b}$ flows $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{5\,\mathrm{V}/30\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{166.7}$ mA, through $R_\mathrm{c}$ flows $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{5\,\mathrm{V}/15\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{333.3}$ mA. The sum is 500 mA.

⁠Limiting case: For $R_\mathrm{b}\to0$, (1.1) becomes $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{a}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6\,\Omega}$. The short circuit bypasses everything behind it; the charge takes the wire.

QuestionMay I do the steps in any order?

No. A group often comes into being only once the inner group has been combined: in the initial diagram $R_\mathrm{c}$ and $R_\mathrm{b}$ are not in parallel, because the three stacked resistors hang between them. That is why you work from the group farthest from the terminals toward the terminals.

Exercise 1 ★ Changing the load network

Start from the network in Fig. 1. What $R_\mathrm{AB}$ results if (a) $R_\mathrm{b}$ drops out through a broken wire, (b) $R_\mathrm{c}$ is short-circuited, (c) $R_\mathrm{d}$ is short-circuited, (d) $R_\mathrm{f}$ drops out? Before calculating, predict which of the four changes has the least effect.

Solution

Prediction: (d) has the least effect, because $R_\mathrm{f}$ is the largest resistor in its parallel group and carries the smallest current.

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6\,\Omega+15\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{21\,\Omega}$. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6\,\Omega+30\,\Omega\parallel10\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{13.5\,\Omega}$. (c) The entire parallel group is bypassed: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6\,\Omega+30\,\Omega\parallel5\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10.29\,\Omega}$. (d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{20\,\Omega\parallel30\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12\,\Omega}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6\,\Omega+30\,\Omega\parallel17\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{16.85\,\Omega}$.
