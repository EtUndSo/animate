Stage 9: Thevenin and Norton

DEENES中文
What this stage is about:

N is to feed many different loads. So far you would have to recalculate the entire network for each load. This stage replaces the network by a straight line, once and for all.

The idea. Imagine the load replaced by a current source that draws exactly the current $I_\mathrm{A}$ from the network. The network notices no difference, since at the terminals it senses only this current. Now it contains only resistors and sources, so superposition is allowed. $U_\mathrm{AB}$ therefore consists of two parts: the share of the internal sources, which does not depend on the current, and the share of $I_\mathrm{A}$, which is proportional to it. A constant minus a factor times the current is a straight line. That is the entire reason why a linear network looks like a straight line from outside.

Theorem 9.1 (Equivalent sources: Thevenin and Norton)

Every linear one-port behaves at its terminals like an equivalent voltage source and equally like an equivalent current source:

$$\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{i}-R_\mathrm{i}\,I_\mathrm{A},}$$
$$\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{i}-G_\mathrm{i}\,U_\mathrm{AB},}$$
(9.1)

with

$$\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{OC},}$$
$$\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{SC},}$$
$$\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{G_\mathrm{i}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{OC}}{I_\mathrm{SC}}.}$$
(9.2)

⁠How to read it: The first form is named after Thevenin, the second after Norton. $U_\mathrm{OC}$ is the voltage at open terminals, $I_\mathrm{SC}$ the current through the short-circuited terminals. $R_\mathrm{i}$ is the same in both forms. The straight line is also called the load characteristic. For small $R_\mathrm{i}$ you usually choose the Thevenin form, for large $R_\mathrm{i}$ the Norton form (Stage 6). Validity: The one-port must be linear, the load need not be. The statement holds only for the terminals; it says nothing about powers inside (Stage 6).

Derivation: The straight line from superposition

With the imagined current source $I_\mathrm{A}$ at the terminals, (8.1) gives

$$\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\underbrace{\sum_k a_kU_{\mathrm{q}k}+\sum_k Z_kI_{\mathrm{q}k}}_{\text{internal sources}}\;\underbrace{-\,R_\mathrm{i}I_\mathrm{A}}_{\text{terminal current}}.}$$

For $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ the first part remains, so it is $U_\mathrm{OC}$. With all internal sources turned off, the second part remains: there $R_\mathrm{i}$ is the total resistance of the turned-off network between A and B. The minus comes from the reference arrow: $I_\mathrm{A}$ flows out of A, so inside from B to A, with A lower. With $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{SC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{OC}/R_\mathrm{i}}$. Rearranging gives the second form.

Three ways to the two numbers.

  1. ⁠Open circuit and short circuit: Calculate or measure $U_\mathrm{OC}$ and $I_\mathrm{SC}$; $R_\mathrm{i}$ follows from (9.2).

  2. ⁠Turning off the sources: Voltage sources as wires, current sources as gaps, then determine the total resistance between A and B. That is $R_\mathrm{i}$. In addition, one of the two quantities $U_\mathrm{OC}$ or $I_\mathrm{SC}$.

  3. ⁠Two load cases: If you must not short-circuit, measure the points $(I_\mathrm{x},U_\mathrm{x})$ and $(I_\mathrm{y},U_\mathrm{y})$ with two loads. Both lie on the line, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{x}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{i}-R_\mathrm{i}I_\mathrm{x}}$ and $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{y}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{i}-R_\mathrm{i}I_\mathrm{y}}$. Subtracting and substituting gives

    $$\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{U_\mathrm{x}-U_\mathrm{y}}{I_\mathrm{x}-I_\mathrm{y}},}$$
    $$\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{x}+R_\mathrm{i}I_\mathrm{x}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{x}I_\mathrm{y}-U_\mathrm{y}I_\mathrm{x}}{I_\mathrm{y}-I_\mathrm{x}}.}$$
    (9.3)

Example. For N, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{OC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9}$ V is known from Stage 8. In the short circuit, $R_2$ lies between two connected terminals and carries no current. The voltage source drives $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{q1}/R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ A through the terminals, the current source 1.5 A: $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{SC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{q1}/R_1+I_\mathrm{q2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.5}$ A. From this follows $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9\,\mathrm{V}/4.5\,\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\Omega}$. The second way confirms it: turned off, $R_1$ and $R_2$ are in parallel, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{6\,\Omega\parallel3\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\Omega}$. The Norton form has $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.5}$ A and $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{G_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$ S (Fig. 10).

These are exactly the numbers of the battery from Stage 5. Without looking at the network again, for 16 $\Omega$ it follows that $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9\,\mathrm{V}/18\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$ A and $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8}$ V. Check in the original: through $R_1$ come $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{(18-8)\,\mathrm{V}/6\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.667}$ A, plus 1.5 A, which makes 3.167 A. Of this, $R_2$ takes the largest part with $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{8\,\mathrm{V}/3\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.667}$ A, and 0.5 A goes to the load. If $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{q2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, the voltage divider from partial circuit 1 would remain, with $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}U_\mathrm{q1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{q1}/R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ A and unchanged $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_1\parallel R_2}$.

Second example: a real current source. An ideal current source $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ A with $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\Omega}$ in parallel feeds terminal A through $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{v}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$. Its other pole is B, and $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{p}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{30\,\Omega}$ lies between A and B. In the short circuit $R_\mathrm{p}$ is bypassed, and the current divider gives $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{SC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{q}\frac{R_\mathrm{q}}{R_\mathrm{q}+R_\mathrm{v}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ A. In the open circuit $R_\mathrm{v}$ and $R_\mathrm{p}$ are in series. They carry $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{q}\frac{R_\mathrm{q}}{R_\mathrm{q}+R_\mathrm{v}+R_\mathrm{p}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ A, so $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{OC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{p}\cdot1\,\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{30}$ V. The quotient is $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{15\,\Omega}$. Turned off, the current source becomes a gap and its parallel resistor stays: $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{p}\parallel(R_\mathrm{q}+R_\mathrm{v})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{15\,\Omega}$, or as a conductance $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{G_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{G_\mathrm{p}+\frac{1}{R_\mathrm{q}+R_\mathrm{v}}}$.

⁠Limiting cases: If an ideal voltage source lies directly across the terminals, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and the Norton form does not exist. If an ideal current source lies in series with a terminal, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\infty}$ and the Thevenin form does not exist. If the sources exactly cancel, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{OC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{SC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. Then the quotient fails, but Way 2 still gives $R_\mathrm{i}$.

QuestionIs N now really the same as the battery?

At the terminals yes. Every load shows the same current and the same voltage on both. Inside no: in the open circuit, N converts 27 W in $R_2$ and 13.5 W in $R_1$, while the battery rests. Equivalent sources answer questions at the terminals, and no others.

Exercise 10 ★★ Two load cases

On an unknown linear device you measure 10 V and 1 A with 10 $\Omega$ at the terminals, then 8 V and 2 A with 4 $\Omega$. (a) Determine $R_\mathrm{i}$, $U_\mathrm{i}$ and $I_\mathrm{SC}$ with (9.3). (b) Predict current and voltage for 1 $\Omega$.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{10-8}{1-2}\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\mathrm{V}+2\,\Omega\cdot1\,\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ V. Check with the fraction: $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{\frac{10\cdot2-8\cdot1}{2-1}\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ V. $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{SC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ A.

(b) $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12\,\mathrm{V}/3\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ A, $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ V.

Exercise 11 ★★★ Two real sources

Between B and a node C lie 2 $\Omega$ and, in parallel with it, an ideal current source of 5 A driving from B to C. From C, 3 $\Omega$ leads to a node D. From D, an ideal voltage source of 4 V leads to terminal A, with its plus terminal at D. Between A and B lie 5 $\Omega$. (a) Calculate $U_\mathrm{OC}$ by superposition. (b) Calculate $R_\mathrm{i}$ by turning off the sources, and from it $I_\mathrm{SC}$. (c) Limiting case: The voltage source has 10 V. What do open circuit and short circuit show, and what is $R_\mathrm{i}$ now?

Solution

(a) Current source only (voltage source as a wire, terminals open): the 5 A feed 2 $\Omega$ in parallel with $3\,\Omega+5\,\Omega$, so $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{2\,\Omega\parallel8\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.6\,\Omega}$, and C is at 8 V against B. The divider of 3 $\Omega$ and 5 $\Omega$ gives $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8\,\mathrm{V}\cdot\frac58}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ V.

Voltage source only (current source open): it drives $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{4\,\mathrm{V}/10\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.4}$ A from the plus terminal through 3 $\Omega$ and 2 $\Omega$ to B and through the 5 $\Omega$ from B to A. So A lies 2 V lower than B: $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U''}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2}$ V. Together: $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{OC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V. In general $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{OC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{I_\mathrm{q2}R_\mathrm{q2}-U_\mathrm{q1}}{R_\mathrm{q1}+R_\mathrm{q2}+R_3}\,R_3}$, if $R_\mathrm{q1}$ is the 3 $\Omega$, $R_\mathrm{q2}$ the 2 $\Omega$ and $R_3$ the 5 $\Omega$.

(b) Turned off, 3 $\Omega$ and 2 $\Omega$ are in series and in parallel with 5 $\Omega$: $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5\,\Omega\parallel5\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.5\,\Omega}$. Thus $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{SC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3\,\mathrm{V}/2.5\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.2}$ A. Directly: the current source with 2 $\Omega$ acts like 10 V with 2 $\Omega$ (Stage 6). The 5 $\Omega$ are short-circuited, so $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{SC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(10\,\mathrm{V}-4\,\mathrm{V})/5\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.2}$ A.

(c) With 10 V the two shares cancel: $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{U_\mathrm{OC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#3f1a78}{I_\mathrm{SC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. The quotient is indeterminate, but $R_\mathrm{i}$ stays 2.5 $\Omega$, because it does not depend on the sources. From outside, the network is a pure resistor.
