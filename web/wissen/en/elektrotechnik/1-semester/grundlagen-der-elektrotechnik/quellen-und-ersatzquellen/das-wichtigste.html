The answer in three sentences

  1. In a linear network the effects of the sources add up, because all the equations are linear. This is superposition, the overlaying of effects.

  2. Therefore the voltage at two terminals A and B is a straight line in the terminal current: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{OC}-R_\mathrm{i}\,I_\mathrm{A}}$. The sources set the open-circuit voltage $U_\mathrm{OC}$ (the voltage with the terminals open), the resistors set the internal resistance $R_\mathrm{i}$ (the slope).

  3. A straight line has two numbers. So the network behaves like an equivalent source, a source with an internal resistance. The open circuit and the short circuit (terminals connected) supply the numbers: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{OC}/I_\mathrm{SC}}$.

Key points

  • Series: add resistances, same current. Parallel: add conductances, same voltage. Combine from the inside out.

  • Nodes of equal potential may be connected, current-free branches may be cut. Symmetry shows where this is possible.

  • Star and delta are interchangeable at the terminals: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_{10}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_{12}R_{31}/(R_{12}+R_{23}+R_{31})}$.

  • Ideal sources hold one quantity fixed. Real sources: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{AB}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{q}-R_\mathrm{q}I_\mathrm{A}}$. Converting: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{q}/R_\mathrm{q}}$, same resistance.

  • Superposition: voltage sources become wires, current sources become gaps, partial effects add. Powers do not add.

  • Every linear one-port is a straight line: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{OC}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{SC}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{OC}/I_\mathrm{SC}}$. The operating point is the intersection with the line of the load.

One principle behind all the results. At two terminals a linear network behaves like a single straight line in the current-voltage diagram, which is why two numbers are enough. From outside, only this characteristic curve counts: two circuits with the same line are interchangeable, no matter what they look like inside. The table assigns the results to this idea.
