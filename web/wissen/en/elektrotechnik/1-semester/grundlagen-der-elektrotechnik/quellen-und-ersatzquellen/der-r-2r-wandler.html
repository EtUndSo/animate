Stage 10: The R-2R converter

What this stage is about:

A processor knows only bits, a loudspeaker needs a voltage. The R-2R converter translates between them. It is superposition and equivalent source in a single circuit.

The idea. A digital-to-analog converter turns a binary number with the bits $d_2d_1d_0$ into a voltage. In Fig. 12 each switch connects its 2$R$ resistor either to $U_\mathrm{ref}$ (bit 1) or to 0 V (bit 0). A switch at 0 V is a turned-off voltage source. So superposition is already built into the structure: every set bit is a source, all the others are wires. The ladder of $R$ and $2R$ is dimensioned so that each rung halves the effect.

Theorem 10.1 (R-2R ladder)

For the converter in Fig. 12 with the output open,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{out}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{ref}\left(\frac{d_2}{2}+\frac{d_1}{4}+\frac{d_0}{8}\right),}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R.}$$
(10.1)

⁠How to read it: $d_k$ is 0 or 1, $U_\mathrm{ref}$ the fixed reference voltage (a few volts), $R$ usually a few k$\Omega$. The most significant bit contributes half the reference voltage, each further one half of the previous one. $R_\mathrm{i}$ does not depend on the switch setting: on turning off, $U_\mathrm{ref}$ becomes 0 V, and then every branch leads to ground, however its switch is set. Validity: ideal switches without resistance of their own, output unloaded.

Derivation: Equivalent source from left to right

At the left node, 2$R$ lies against ground and 2$R$ against $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{d_0U_\mathrm{ref}}$. This is an equivalent source with $\frac12U_0$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2R\parallel2R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R}$. With the series resistor it becomes $\frac12U_0$ and $2R$. At the next node this meets 2$R$ against $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{d_1U_\mathrm{ref}}$: again two equal branches, hence the averaging from (7.2), the equivalent source $\frac12\bigl(\frac12U_0+U_1\bigr)$ and again $R$. The last node repeats this:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{out}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac12\Bigl(\tfrac12\bigl(\tfrac12U_0+U_1\bigr)+U_2\Bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac12U_2+\tfrac14U_1+\tfrac18U_0.}$$

Superposition gives the same number. Take source $U_1$ as an example: at the middle node it sees 2$R$ on the left and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R+2R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3R}$ on the right, together $\frac65R$. The divider gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{6/5}{6/5+2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac38U_1}$ there. The way to the output divides once more by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{2R}{R+2R}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac23}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac38\cdot\frac23}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac14}$.

Example

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{ref}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ k$\Omega$, the bits weigh 4 V, 2 V and 1 V. The setting 101 gives 5 V. A load with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R}$ forms a divider with $R_\mathrm{i}$ and halves this to 2.5 V. That is why real converters are followed by an operational amplifier (Chapter 11) with a very high input resistance: it draws practically no current, and the ladder stays in the open circuit.

⁠Limiting cases: The setting 000 gives 0 V, the setting 111 gives 7 V, one step below $U_\mathrm{ref}$.

⁠Draw: Fig. 12 without switches: 2$R$ from node n0 to ground, then 2$R$ each from n0, n1, n2 to a source voltage against ground, and $R$ between the nodes, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ k$\Omega$. The sources count in binary: PULSE(0 8 1m 1u 1u 1m 2m), PULSE(0 8 2m 1u 1u 2m 4m), PULSE(0 8 4m 1u 1u 4m 8m).

⁠Directive: .tran 8m

⁠What you see: V(n2) is a staircase with steps of 1 V, from 0 V to 7 V. With a load of 1 k$\Omega$ at n2 it rises only to 3.5 V. With 3 k$\Omega$, your result from Exercise 12(c) appears on the top step.

QuestionWhat is the single 2$R$ resistor at the far left for?

It makes the ladder uniform. With it, everything to the left of each node is an equivalent source with $2R$, just like the branch of the node's own switch. Only then do two equal branches and a pure mean value arise. Without it the left branch would be $3R$, and the weights would no longer be binary.

Exercise 12 ★★ Four bits

(a) A converter with four bits runs on $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{ref}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{16}$ V. What are the step, the maximum value and the voltage at 1011? (b) How large is a step for eight bits at 5 V? (c) The three-bit converter from the example is set to 111 and feeds $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3R}$. What voltage arises?

Solution

(a) Step $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{16\,\mathrm{V}/2^4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ V. Maximum value $15$ V. 1011: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{8+0+2+1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{11}$ V. The formula continues with $d_3/2+d_2/4+d_1/8+d_0/16$.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{5\,\mathrm{V}/256}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{19.5}$ mV.

(c) Open circuit 7 V, internal resistance $R$. Divider: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{7\,\mathrm{V}\cdot\frac{3R}{R+3R}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.25}$ V.
