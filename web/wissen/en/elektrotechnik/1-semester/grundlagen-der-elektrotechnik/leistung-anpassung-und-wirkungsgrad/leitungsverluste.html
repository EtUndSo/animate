What this stage is about:

The line lies between source and load, and it too is a resistor. This stage shows why large powers travel over high voltages.

The idea. The line is in series with the load; the entire current must pass through it (Fig. 5). Each coulomb loses $R_\mathrm{cable}I$ joules in it before reaching the load. A fixed power can be transmitted in two ways: with high voltage and low current, or the other way around. At high voltage, each coulomb brings a lot of energy, so few are enough. The low current helps twice: each coulomb loses less, and there are fewer of them.

Theorem 4.1 (Line losses)

If the current $I$ flows to the load over a line with $R_\mathrm{cable}$, then

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{cable}\,I,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{cable}\,I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{cable}\,I^2,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{useful}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{cable})\,I}$$
(4.1)

and therefore

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{P_\mathrm{useful}}{P_\mathrm{q}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{(U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{cable})\,I}{U_\mathrm{q}\,I}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-\frac{U_\mathrm{cable}}{U_\mathrm{q}}.}$$
(4.2)

For two voltages with the same load power $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{L1}I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{L2}I_2}$,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{I_1}{I_2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{L2}}{U_\mathrm{L1}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{P_\mathrm{cable,1}}{P_\mathrm{cable,2}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left(\frac{U_\mathrm{L2}}{U_\mathrm{L1}}\right)^{\!2}.}$$
(4.3)

⁠How to read it: $U_\mathrm{cable}$ is the voltage drop along the line. $P_\mathrm{cable}$ is the power loss, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{useful}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_\mathrm{L}}$ the useful power of the load, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{q}I}$ the power of the source. $U_\mathrm{L}$ is the voltage across the load. Validity: The formulas hold for DC quantities and for the resistance of the line. For an AC voltage and an ohmic load, they hold with RMS values.

Example

The load needs 40 kW, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\,\Omega}$. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{400}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ kW. The source must supply 500 V, and by (4.2), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1-100/500}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{80}$ % arrive. At 20 kV, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ W, and 99.99 % arrive. The current falls by a factor of 50, the loss by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{50^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2500}$.

⁠Limiting case: For $R_\mathrm{cable}\to0$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and (4.2) gives 1. Over longer distances, energy can be transmitted only at high voltage, which is why the grid is built in levels. The nominal voltage is the voltage for which a grid is designed:

LevelNominal voltageRangeTask
Low voltage230/400 Va few 100 mHouseholds and businesses in town
Medium voltage10 kVup to around 10 kmDistribution in large cities
Medium voltage20 kVup to around 25 kmDistribution in rural areas and medium-sized towns
High voltage110 kVup to around 100 kmSupply of the medium-voltage grids
Extra-high voltage220 kV, 380 kVa few 100 to over 1000 kmInterconnection within the country and across Europe

High voltage cannot be brought all the way into the home: air breaks down at around 3 kV/mm (Chapter 1), so overhead lines need clearances of several meters. Transformers step the AC voltage up for transport and back down again ahead of the consumers.

QuestionAccording to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U^2/R}$, power grows with voltage. Shouldn't higher voltages bring more loss?

The formula is correct, but it needs the voltage across the same resistor. In the example, the cable does not have 20 kV across it but $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ V, and $(2\,\mathrm{V})^2/1\,\Omega$ gives the 4 W. The high voltage is across the load. $U_\mathrm{cable}$ falls because the current falls.

Exercise 4 ★★ The cabin at the edge of the forest

A cabin absorbs 480 W at 48 V DC, and the supply line has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2\,\Omega}$. (a) Calculate $I$, $U_\mathrm{cable}$, $P_\mathrm{cable}$, $U_\mathrm{q}$ and the fraction that arrives. (b) The same for 480 W at 12 V. (c) Which voltage $U_\mathrm{L}$ limits the loss to 1 % of the load power?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{480\,\mathrm{W}/48\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20}$ W, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50}$ V. A fraction of $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1-2/50}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{96}$ % arrives. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{40}$ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{320}$ W, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20}$ V. Only 60 % arrives. Four times the current gives sixteen times the loss. (c) $R_\mathrm{cable}\,P_\mathrm{L}^2/U_\mathrm{L}^2\le0.01\,P_\mathrm{L}$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{L}\ge\sqrt{R_\mathrm{cable}\,P_\mathrm{L}/0.01}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{98}$ V.

Theorem 4.1 (Line losses)

If the current $I$ flows to the load over a line with $R_\mathrm{cable}$, then

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{cable}\,I,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{cable}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{cable}\,I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{cable}\,I^2,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{useful}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{cable})\,I}$$
(4.1)

and therefore

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{P_\mathrm{useful}}{P_\mathrm{q}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{(U_\mathrm{q}-U_\mathrm{cable})\,I}{U_\mathrm{q}\,I}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-\frac{U_\mathrm{cable}}{U_\mathrm{q}}.}$$
(4.2)

For two voltages with the same load power $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{L1}I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{L2}I_2}$,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{I_1}{I_2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{L2}}{U_\mathrm{L1}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{P_\mathrm{cable,1}}{P_\mathrm{cable,2}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left(\frac{U_\mathrm{L2}}{U_\mathrm{L1}}\right)^{\!2}.}$$
(4.3)
