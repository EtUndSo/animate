Stage 5: Where the power of a source goes

What this stage is about:

The source, too, has a resistance inside it. This stage sets up the balance and defines the efficiency.

The idea. In the source, each coulomb receives the energy $U_\mathrm{q}$ in joules, because the chemistry pushes it against the field to the higher potential. It loses a part $U_\mathrm{Ri}$ in $R_\mathrm{i}$ as heat and gives off the rest $U_\mathrm{L}$ in the load (Fig. 6). Then it is back at the starting point. All coulombs make the same round trip, so the ratio of the voltages determines which fraction of the power arrives.

Definition 5.1 (Power balance and efficiency)

In the circuit with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{q}/(R_\mathrm{i}+R_\mathrm{L})}$,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{q}\,I,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{v}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{Ri}\,I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I^2R_\mathrm{i},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{L}\,I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I^2R_\mathrm{L},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_\mathrm{v}+P_\mathrm{L}.}$$
(5.1)

The efficiency is the power used relative to the total power expended by the source:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\eta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{P_\mathrm{L}}{P_\mathrm{q}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{I\,U_\mathrm{L}}{I\,U_\mathrm{q}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{L}}{U_\mathrm{q}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{R_\mathrm{L}}{R_\mathrm{L}+R_\mathrm{i}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{R_\mathrm{L}/R_\mathrm{i}}{1+R_\mathrm{L}/R_\mathrm{i}}.}$$
(5.2)

⁠How to read it: $P_\mathrm{q}$ is the power generated by the source, $P_\mathrm{v}$ the power loss in its internal resistance, $P_\mathrm{L}$ the useful power. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{Ri}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{i}I}$ is the voltage across $R_\mathrm{i}$. $\eta$ is a pure number between 0 and 1 and depends only on the ratio $R_\mathrm{L}/R_\mathrm{i}$. Validity: This holds for the series connection of ideal source, $R_\mathrm{i}$ and $R_\mathrm{L}$. With an equivalent source, the internal quantities are not correct (Stage 7).

Derivation: The balance is the mesh rule times the current

The mesh rule reads $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{Ri}+U_\mathrm{L}}$. Multiplied by $I$, it gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_\mathrm{v}+P_\mathrm{L}}$. In (5.2), $I$ cancels, because both resistors carry the same current. The voltage divider (Chapter 2) gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{q}\,R_\mathrm{L}/(R_\mathrm{L}+R_\mathrm{i})}$.

QuestionThe efficiency keeps rising with $R_\mathrm{L}$. Why not make the load arbitrarily large?

Because $\eta$ is a fraction and not an amount. With a large $R_\mathrm{L}$, each coulomb gives off almost everything in the load, but hardly any coulombs arrive. The table shows it: from 8 $\Omega$ to 16 $\Omega$, $\eta$ rises while the power $P_\mathrm{L}$ falls.

Exercise 5 ★ Setting up the balance

A source has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3\,\Omega}$. (a) For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9\,\Omega}$, calculate all the quantities from (5.1) and $\eta$. (b) Which load gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\eta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{90}$ %, and what arrives then? (c) What happens in a short circuit?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12\,\mathrm{V}/12\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{Ri}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ W, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{v}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ W, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9}$ W, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\eta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{75}$ %. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{L}/(R_\mathrm{L}+3\,\Omega)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.9}$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{27\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.4}$ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_\mathrm{L}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.32}$ W. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{SC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ A, all 48 W arise in $R_\mathrm{i}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\eta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.
