What this is about#

An operational amplifier, op amp for short, is an integrated amplifier made of a few dozen transistors. It amplifies the voltage between its two inputs by a factor of about 100000. This factor is called the open-loop gain. It varies by a factor of four from one specimen to the next and changes with temperature. Nevertheless, this component is used to build amplifiers that feel only 0.01 % of this variation, as well as summing amplifiers and integrators. The question is: How does a huge, imprecise factor turn into a component whose behavior is determined by the resistors alone? The answer is negative feedback: part of the output voltage is fed back to the input in such a way that it counteracts its own cause.

The reference model for this chapter

  • ⁠Op amp V: Open-loop gain $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100000}$ (specimens 50000 to 200000), input resistance $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r_\mathrm{e}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ M$\Omega$, output resistance $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r_\mathrm{a}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{75\,\Omega}$. Supply $\pm12$ V; the output reaches $\pm11$ V and at most 20 mA. For Stage 10: transition frequency $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_\mathrm{T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ MHz (there $A$ has fallen to 1), offset $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{off}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ mV (residual difference of the inputs), input bias current $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50}$ nA (base current of the input transistors), common-mode rejection 90 dB (attenuation of common disturbances).

  • ⁠External circuit: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{40}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ V.

  • ⁠Differential stage: transistors T from Chapter 10 ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{200}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{26}$ mV), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ k$\Omega$.

  • ⁠Sensor: 1 mV rms, internal resistance 10 k$\Omega$, bandwidth (the frequency range passed) 20 kHz, temperature 300 K.
