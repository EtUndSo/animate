This chapter calculates with potentials $\varphi$ with respect to ground (Chapter 1) and the node rule: the currents into a node sum to zero (Chapter 2). Two resistors in series divide a voltage in the ratio of their values (voltage divider, Chapter 2). For the capacitor, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{C\,\mathrm{d} u/\mathrm{d} t}$ holds (Chapter 3); for the diode in the forward direction, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{S}\,\mathrm{e}^{U/U_T}}$ (Chapter 9). A bipolar transistor in the active region is a voltage-controlled current source: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{S}\,\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm{BE}/U_T}}$, hardly dependent on $U_\mathrm{CE}$. Around the operating point, the transconductance $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm{C}/U_T}$ is the slope of this curve (Chapter 10). The rms values from Chapter 7 measure the strength of an alternating voltage.
