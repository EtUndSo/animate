Stage 5: The rule and the virtual ground

What this stage is about:

Stage 4 calculated with $A$. But the deviation is tiny. This stage turns this into a rule for all circuits with negative feedback and applies it to the most important case: the input at the minus terminal.

The idea. In every circuit, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|U_\mathrm{D}|\le11\,\mathrm{V}/100000}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{110}$ µ V as long as the output does not hit its limit. Compared with voltages in the volt range, that is nothing. So if you calculate with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{D}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, you make an error of the order of $1/(A\beta)$. With that, $A$ is no longer needed.

Theorem 5.1 (The rule for the op amp with negative feedback)

As long as $U_\mathrm{A,min}<U_\mathrm{A}<U_\mathrm{A,max}$,

  1. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_+}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_-}$: The output adjusts itself so that the difference vanishes.

  2. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_+}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_-}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: No current flows into the inputs.

  3. The output supplies any current needed. No node equation is written for the output node.

Set up the node rule at the minus input, solve for $U_\mathrm{A}$, and check the limits last.

In the inverting amplifier, the plus input is at ground, $U_\mathrm{E}$ arrives at the minus input through $R_1$, and $R_2$ leads back from the output (Fig. 7). From $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_-}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_+}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_-}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ it follows that

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{U_\mathrm{E}}{R_1}+\frac{U_\mathrm{A}}{R_2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0\quad\Rightarrow\quad U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{R_2}{R_1}\,U_\mathrm{E},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_\mathrm{E}}{I_1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_1.}$$
(5.1)

⁠How to read it: $I_1$ is the current through $R_1$. $R_\mathrm{in}$ is the resistance the source sees: only $R_1$, because its right end is firmly at 0 V. With finite $A$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-(R_2/R_1)\big/\bigl(1+1/(A\beta)\bigr)}$ with the same $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\beta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_1/(R_1+R_2)}$. Validity: like the rule, and $A\beta\gg1$.

Derivation: Finite $A$ in the inverting amplifier

Superposition at the divider of $R_1$ and $R_2$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_-}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(R_2\,U_\mathrm{E}+R_1\,U_\mathrm{A})/(R_1+R_2)}$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_+}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{D}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\varphi_-}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-A\,\varphi_-}$. Solving: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}(1+A\beta)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-A\,(1-\beta)\,U_\mathrm{E}}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1-\beta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_2/(R_1+R_2)}$. Because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A(1-\beta)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{A\beta\cdot R_2/R_1}$, the formula follows. The loop sees $U_\mathrm{E}$ as a fixed value, so $\beta$ is the same divider as in Stage 4.

The mechanism of the virtual ground. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ V, the voltage across $R_1$ drives a current of 100 µ A to the minus input. It cannot enter the op amp. So it has to go through $R_2$. For 100 µ A through 40 k$\Omega$, the right end of $R_2$ must be 4 V lower, so the output goes to $-4$ V. The current continues into the output and through the lower output transistor to $U_\mathrm{B-}$ (red line in Fig. 7). At the node only the tiny voltage $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_-}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-U_\mathrm{D}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{A}/(-A)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{+40}$ µ V remains. The node behaves like a ground but is not one:

  • A real ground takes in current. The virtual one passes it on to $R_2$; nothing flows between the node and ground.

  • A real ground always stays at 0 V. The virtual one holds its potential only because the output keeps readjusting. If the output is at its limit, the virtual ground is gone (Fig. 7 bottom).

  • If you connect the node to ground with a wire, the 100 µ A flows there, $R_2$ carries no current, and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

Why no input current flows nevertheless. There is almost no voltage between the inputs, but no conductor either. They have the same potential but are not connected: 40 µ V across $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r_\mathrm{e}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ M$\Omega$ gives 40 pA, 2.5 million times less than $I_1$. Both act together: the differential stage has a high resistance, and the loop leaves it almost no voltage.

QuestionAt 0 V at the node with current arriving, shouldn't the minus input have zero resistance?

That is how it looks to the source: behind $R_1$ it sees a point that holds its potential no matter what current arrives. But that is done by the output via $R_2$. The input itself remains high-resistance at 1 M$\Omega$.

Example

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-4\cdot1\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-4}$ V, more precisely $-3.9998$ V with finite $A$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ k$\Omega$. With a load of 2 k$\Omega$ to ground, 2 mA flows, and the output takes in 2.1 mA: load plus feedback. The source $U_\mathrm{E}$ notices nothing of this.

⁠Limiting cases: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_1}$ only reverses the sign. $R_2\to0$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

The first limit. Rule 1 assumes that the output reaches the demanded voltage. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-4}$ this ends at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|U_\mathrm{E}|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{11\,\mathrm{V}/4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.75}$ V. Above that, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mp11}$ V is fixed, the loop is open, and the divider of $R_1$ and $R_2$ determines the node: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_-}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.8\,U_\mathrm{E}+0.2\,U_\mathrm{A}}$, so $+0.6$ V at 3.5 V.

Exercise 5 ★★ Inverting amplifier

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.7}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{47}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$ V. (a) Determine $U_\mathrm{A}$, $I_1$ and $R_\mathrm{in}$. (b) At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100000}$, how large are $\varphi_-$ and the exact $G$? (c) A load of 1 k$\Omega$ hangs on the output. How large is the output current, and where does it come from? (d) Up to which $U_\mathrm{E}$ does the output reach? How large is $\varphi_-$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ V?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-10\cdot0.5\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-5}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{106.4}$ µ A, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{in}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.7}$ k$\Omega$. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_-}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5\,\mathrm{V}/A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50}$ µ V. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\beta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.7/51.7}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.0909}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-10/(1+1.1\cdot10^{-4})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-9.9989}$. (c) The load carries 5 mA, the feedback 0.106 mA. Into the output flow 5.11 mA, onward to $U_\mathrm{B-}$. (d) $|U_\mathrm{E}|\le1.1$ V. At 2 V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-11}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_-}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(47\cdot2-4.7\cdot11)\,\mathrm{V}/51.7}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.818}$ V.
