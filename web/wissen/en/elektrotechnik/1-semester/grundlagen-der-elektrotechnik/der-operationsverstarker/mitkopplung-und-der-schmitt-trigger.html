Stage 9: Positive feedback and the Schmitt trigger

What this stage is about:

If you swap the inputs of the non-inverting amplifier, the same equations give the same number. But the circuit does something completely different. This stage clarifies why the rule fails here and what happens instead.

The idea. Now $U_\mathrm{E}$ is at the minus input, and the divider leads to the plus input (Fig. 11). The calculation $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{D}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\beta\,U_\mathrm{A}-U_\mathrm{E}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{A\,U_\mathrm{D}}$ has, at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ V, the solution $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.00025}$ V. This is an equilibrium, but an unstable one, like a ball on a hilltop. A disturbance of 1 µ V upward raises the plus input, so $U_\mathrm{D}$ grows, so the output rises further. The effect reinforces its cause. The output runs to its limit and stays there. There the rule does not hold: $U_\mathrm{D}\ne0$.

At the limit the circuit remembers its state. If the output is at $+11$ V, the plus input is at $+2.2$ V. As long as $U_\mathrm{E}$ stays below that, $U_\mathrm{D}>0$ and nothing changes. If $U_\mathrm{E}$ exceeds 2.2 V, the output flips, and the plus input jumps to $-2.2$ V. This reinforces the flipping. To flip back, $U_\mathrm{E}$ has to fall below $-2.2$ V. The output depends on the history; this is called hysteresis. The circuit is called an inverting Schmitt trigger.

Theorem 9.1 (Schmitt trigger)

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\beta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_1/(R_1+R_2)}$ the output flips

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\text{downward at }U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\beta\,U_\mathrm{A,max},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\text{upward at }U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\beta\,U_\mathrm{A,min}.}$$
(9.1)

The width of the hysteresis is $\beta\,(U_\mathrm{A,max}-U_\mathrm{A,min})$. Between the thresholds both states are possible.

⁠How to read it: The output changes as soon as $U_\mathrm{D}$ changes sign. Validity: $A\beta>1$; otherwise the positive feedback is not enough to flip.

Derivation: The same equation, a different sign

With the sluggishness from Stage 4, now $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau\,\mathrm{d} U_\mathrm{A}/\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{A(\beta\,U_\mathrm{A}-U_\mathrm{E})-U_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(A\beta-1)\,U_\mathrm{A}-A\,U_\mathrm{E}}$. The factor in front of $U_\mathrm{A}$ is now positive. A deviation from equilibrium grows like $\mathrm{e}^{+t/\tau_\mathrm{M}}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau_\mathrm{M}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tau/(A\beta-1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.80}$ µ s, the same size as in Stage 4. In this way a disturbance of 1 µ V becomes the 11 V in 13 µ s. An unlimited supply would give an unlimited rise; the circuit would be unstable. Only saturation stops it.

Example

The thresholds are $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\pm0.2\cdot11\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\pm2.2}$ V, and the hysteresis is 4.4 V wide. Shortly after flipping at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.2}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{D}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2.2\,\mathrm{V}-2.2\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-4.4}$ V, far from the window. Benefit: a noisy signal passing slowly through a threshold switches a comparator back and forth many times. With the Schmitt trigger the threshold jumps away by 4.4 V after the first flip, and noise below this width triggers no second flip.

⁠Limiting cases: $\beta\to0$ gives the comparator from Stage 3. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ allows both states, depending on the history.

QuestionWith swapped inputs, LTspice showed 5.00025 V in .op. Is the simulation wrong?

No, it answers a different question. .op solves the equilibrium equation, and this solution exists. Whether an equilibrium holds is decided only by the time behavior. In the component something is always making noise, which is why the output runs away. The LTspice box in Stage 10 shows this in .tran.

Exercise 10 ★★ Schmitt trigger

(a) With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{90}$ k$\Omega$: Where are the thresholds, and how wide is the hysteresis? (b) Choose $R_2$ for the thresholds $\pm1$ V at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ k$\Omega$. (c) With (a), $U_\mathrm{E}$ runs from $-5$ V to $+5$ V and back. Sketch $U_\mathrm{A}(U_\mathrm{E})$. (d) A supply from 0 to 5 V gives an output from 0.5 V to 4.5 V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\beta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2}$. Where are the thresholds?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\beta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.1}$: thresholds $\pm1.1$ V, width 2.2 V. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\beta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/11}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$ k$\Omega$. (c) At $-5$ V the output is at $+11$ V. It stays there until $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{+1.1}$ V, jumps to $-11$ V and stays there up to $5$ V. On the way back it jumps back only at $-1.1$ V. (d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0.2\cdot4.5\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.9}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0.2\cdot0.5\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.1}$ V.
