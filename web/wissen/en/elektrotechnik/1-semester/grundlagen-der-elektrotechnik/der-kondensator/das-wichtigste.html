The answer in three sentences

  1. A capacitor does not let charge through, it displaces it: electrons leave one plate and arrive on the other, and the same current flows at both terminals at the same moment.

  2. The separated charge $Q$ produces the field between the plates. This field determines the voltage $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q/C}$, and it holds the field energy $\frac12CU^2$, stored in the space between the plates.

  3. Charge needs current and time, so the voltage does not jump. At the first moment an empty capacitor acts like a short circuit (current at 0 V), a full one like an open circuit (voltage without current). In between runs an exponential curve with the time constant $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{RC}$, the time that sets the pace of charging.

Key points

  • The capacitor separates charge: $+Q$ and $-Q$, total charge zero. The same current flows at both terminals, no charge through the gap.

  • Capacitance: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q/U}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{C\,\mathrm{d} u/\mathrm{d} t}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u(t_0)+\frac1C\int_{t_0}^ti\,\mathrm{d} t'}$. $u$ does not jump, direct current is blocked.

  • Parallel-plate capacitor: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon A/d}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varepsilon}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_\mathrm{r}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varepsilon_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8.854\cdot10^{-12}}$ A s/(V m).

  • Energy: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12CU^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12QU}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q^2/(2C)}$, in the field with the density $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{w}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\varepsilon E^2}$.

  • Parallel: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum C_i}$. Series: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1/C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum1/C_i}$.

  • Island: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_\mathrm{tot}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum C_iU_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{}$ const. The energy is not conserved in the process.

  • Charging: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_0(1-\mathrm{e}^{-t/\tau})}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_0}{R}\mathrm{e}^{-t/\tau}}$. Discharging: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_0\,\mathrm{e}^{-t/\tau}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{U_0}{R}\mathrm{e}^{-t/\tau}}$. Time constant $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{RC}$.

  • General: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_C(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u_\infty+(u_C(0)-u_\infty)\,\mathrm{e}^{-t/\tau}}$. Empty, it acts at the first moment like a short circuit; full, like an open circuit.

One principle behind all the results. The voltage shows how much charge is separated, and charge changes only through current. From these two facts, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{q/C}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{d} q/\mathrm{d} t}$, all the results of this chapter follow.
