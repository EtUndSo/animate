Stage 6: The stored energy

What this stage is about:

Charging costs energy, because every further charge has to run against the voltage already present. This stage calculates how much it is and where the energy is afterward.

The idea. Voltage is energy per charge (Chapter 1). Bringing the small charge $\Delta q$ from the negative to the positive plate costs $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Delta W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u\,\Delta q}$. The first charges cost almost nothing, because there is no field yet. The last ones cost the full amount $U$ per coulomb. On average each charge pays $U/2$. This is the area under the line $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{q/C}$ (Fig. 6 left).

Theorem 6.1 (Energy in the capacitor)

A capacitor with the charge $Q$ and the voltage $U$ stores

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_0^Q\frac qC\,\mathrm{d} q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{Q^2}{2C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\,Q\,U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\,C\,U^2.}$$
(6.1)

It resides in the field between the plates. For the parallel-plate capacitor, with the volume $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{V}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{A\,d}$,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\,\varepsilon\,E^2\cdot A\,d,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{w}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac WV}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\,\varepsilon\,E^2.}$$
(6.2)

Here $w$ is the energy density, the energy per volume.

⁠How to read it: $W$ in J, $C$ in F, $U$ in V, $w$ in J/m$^3$. The voltage enters squared: twice the voltage means twice the charge, and that runs against twice the voltage. $q$ is the charge during charging, $Q$ its final value. Validity: $C$ constant, no leakage current.

Derivation: Energy from force and distance

The force on $\Delta q$ in the field is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Delta q\,E}$ (Chapter 1). Over the distance $\Delta s$ between the plates, this costs $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Delta W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{F\,\Delta s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Delta q\,E\,\Delta s}$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E\,\Delta s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u}$ it follows that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Delta W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Delta q\,u}$, and with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{q/C}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Delta W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac qC\,\Delta q}$. For small $\Delta q$, the sum of all portions becomes the integral (6.1).

The circuit side gives the same result. The power (energy per time) is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u\,i}$ (Chapter 2). With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{C\,\mathrm{d} u/\mathrm{d} t}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int u\,i\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{C\int_0^Uu\,\mathrm{d} u}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12CU^2}$.

⁠From the component to the field: Substituting $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon A/d}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{E\,d}$ in (6.1) gives (6.2).

Where the energy resides. The metal cannot contain it, because there $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and the electrons are only displaced a tiny bit (Stage 2). Equation (6.2) says: every cubic meter of field contains $\frac12\varepsilon E^2$. That this is more than a way of calculating is shown by an experiment. You disconnect the battery and pull the plates apart. $Q$ stays, and so does $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q/(\varepsilon A)}$. But the field fills more volume, and the field energy grows by $\frac12\varepsilon E^2A\,\Delta d$. The energy comes from the hand pulling against the attraction of the plates. Each plate feels only the field of the other, which is half of $E$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12QE}$. The work is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F\,\Delta d}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12QE\,\Delta d}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\varepsilon E^2A\,\Delta d}$, exactly the increase. That field energy exists independently of charges is shown by light: it carries energy through empty space.

Example

At 9 V the capacitor of the RC circuit stores $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\cdot100\,\mu\text{F}\cdot(9\,\mathrm{V})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.05}$ mJ, the pair of plates only 1.20 nJ. There $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{w}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\varepsilon_0(3\,\mathrm{kV/m})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.98\cdot10^{-5}}$ J/m$^3$, times $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{V}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}}$ gives the same 1.20 nJ. A 100 F supercap stores a respectable 365 J at 2.7 V, but a 9 V rectangular battery with 0.5 Ah stores 16 kJ, four million times as much as the 100 µF capacitor. Capacitors store little energy but release it very quickly, for example in a camera flash.

⁠Limiting case: For $U\to0$, $W\to0$: no field, no energy. Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{F\cdot V^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\mathrm{C}}{\mathrm V}\cdot\mathrm{V^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{C\,V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm J}$.

QuestionWhy not $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q\,U}$? After all, voltage times charge is energy.

$Q\,U$ is right when the whole charge passes through the full voltage. During charging, however, $U$ is the final value; the voltage grows from zero. The battery really does give up $Q\,U$, because every charge runs through it at the full voltage. The capacitor receives only half. Where the rest goes is clarified by Exercise 11.

Exercise 7 ★★★ Pulling the plates apart

  1. A 470 µF capacitor is connected to 12 V, then to 24 V. How large is $W$ in each case?

  2. The pair of plates is charged to 9 V and disconnected. Then $d$ is doubled to 6 mm. How large are $C$, $U$, $E$ and $W$? Check the increase in $W$ with $F\,\Delta d$.

  3. The same, but the battery stays connected. Now $W$ falls, even though the hand does work. Where does the energy go? The mechanical work here is 0.60 nJ.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\cdot470\,\mu\text{F}\cdot(12\,\mathrm{V})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{33.8}$ mJ, at 24 V four times that, 135 mJ.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{266}$ pC stays. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{14.8}$ pF, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q/C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{18}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U/d}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ kV/m as before. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12QU}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.39}$ nJ; the increase is 1.20 nJ. The force is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12QE}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.398\,\mu\text{N}}$, and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F\,\Delta d}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.398\,\mu\text{N}\cdot3\,\mathrm{mm}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.20}$ nJ. The two agree.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9}$ V stays. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{14.8}$ pF, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{133}$ pC, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.5}$ kV/m, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.60}$ nJ. The field loses 0.60 nJ. Added to this are 0.60 nJ from the hand. Both flow into the battery, because 133 pC run back at 9 V: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{133\,\mathrm{pC}\cdot9\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.20}$ nJ. The battery is charged a little.
