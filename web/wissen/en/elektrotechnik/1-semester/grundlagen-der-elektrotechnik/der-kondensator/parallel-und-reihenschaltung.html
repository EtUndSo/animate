Stage 7: Parallel and series connection

What this stage is about:

Several capacitors can be treated like one. The only question is which quantity the circuit makes the same for all of them.

The idea. ⁠Parallel: All upper plates hang on one wire, all lower plates on another. A wire without current has the same potential everywhere, so the same voltage is across all the capacitors. The circuit acts like an enlarged plate, and the charges add. Series: The lower plate of $C_1$ and the upper plate of $C_2$, together with their connecting wire, form an island: a piece of conductor reached only through insulators (Fig. 7). Charge can neither get in nor out there. If the island was uncharged, its sum stays zero: if the upper plate with $+Q$ attracts $-Q$ on the island, the missing counterpart must go as $+Q$ onto the next plate. So all carry the same charge, and the voltages add. The circuit acts like an enlarged separation.

Theorem 7.1 (Total capacitance)

For $n$ capacitors connected in parallel (left) and $n$ initially uncharged capacitors connected in series (right),

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{i=1}^nC_i,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{1}{C_\mathrm{G}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{1}{C_i}.}$$
(7.1)

For two capacitors in series, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{C_1C_2}{C_1+C_2}}$, and the voltage divides according to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{G}\,\dfrac{C_2}{C_1+C_2}}$.

Derivation: Total capacitance

⁠Parallel. Constraint of the circuit: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{G}}$ for all $i$. Individual charge: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{C_iU_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{C_iU_\mathrm{G}}$. Total charge: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum Q_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{G}\sum C_i}$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_\mathrm{G}/U_\mathrm{G}}$ the left formula follows.

⁠Series. Constraint of the circuit: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_\mathrm{G}}$ for all $i$. Total voltage: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum U_i}$. Partial voltage: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_i/C_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_\mathrm{G}/C_i}$. So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_\mathrm{G}\sum 1/C_i}$, and with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_\mathrm{G}/C_\mathrm{G}}$ the right formula follows. For the divider: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_\mathrm{G}/C_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{G}\,C_\mathrm{G}/C_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm{G}\,C_2/(C_1+C_2)}$.

⁠How to read it: $C_\mathrm{G}$ is the capacitance of a single capacitor that shows the same behavior at the two outer terminals. In parallel, $C_\mathrm{G}$ becomes larger than every individual value, in series smaller than the smallest. Validity: The right formula needs uncharged islands at the start. Otherwise the old island charge is conserved, and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_\mathrm{G}}$ holds only for the added charge.

Example

Parallel: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{150\,\mu\text{F}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{900\,\mu\text{C}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{450\,\mu\text{C}}$. In series: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1/C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/(100\,\mu\text{F})+1/(50\,\mu\text{F})}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{33.3\,\mu\text{F}}$. At 9 V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{300\,\mu\text{C}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V.

⁠Limiting cases: For $C_2\to\infty$, in series $C_\mathrm{G}\to C_1$: a huge capacitor behaves like a wire. For $C_2\to0$, in series $C_\mathrm{G}\to0$: the gap interrupts; in parallel, $C_1$ remains.

QuestionWhy are the formulas exactly the reverse of those for resistors?

For resistors, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R\,i}$: in series the voltages add at the same current, so $R$ adds. For the capacitor, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1C\,q}$: in series the voltages add at the same charge, so $1/C$ adds. In short: For the capacitor, $1/C$ plays the role of $R$.

Exercise 8 ★★ A mixed network

  1. Two capacitors of 10 µF and 20 µF are in parallel, with 60 µF in series with them, the whole across 9 V. Calculate $C_\mathrm{G}$, the total charge, all voltages and all individual charges.

  2. Two identical capacitors with 100 µF and 16 V maximum voltage are in series. What capacitance and what maximum voltage does the chain have?

Solution

(a) Parallel: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{10+20}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{30\,\mu\text{F}}$. In series with 60 µF: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{30\cdot60}{30+60}\,\mu\text{F}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\mu\text{F}}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\mu\text{F}\cdot9\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{180\,\mu\text{C}}$. Across the 60 µF capacitor: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{180\,\mu\text{C}/60\,\mu\text{F}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V. Across the parallel block: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{180\,\mu\text{C}/30\,\mu\text{F}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ V, together 9 V. The charge divides: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{10\,\mu\text{F}\cdot6\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{60\,\mu\text{C}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{20\,\mu\text{F}\cdot6\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{120\,\mu\text{C}}$.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C_\mathrm{G}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\mu\text{F}}$. With identical capacitors the voltage divides equally, so 16 V each and 32 V together.
