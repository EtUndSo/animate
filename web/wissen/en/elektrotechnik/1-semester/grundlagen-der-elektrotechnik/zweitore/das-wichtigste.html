The answer in three sentences

  1. At each port, the current that flows into one terminal flows back out of the other (port condition). That leaves two voltages and two currents visible from the outside.

  2. In a linear two-port without sources of its own, two of these four quantities determine the other two. Each dependent quantity is a sum of two parts, so four numbers are enough, the two-port parameters.

  3. The impedance form (currents given), the admittance form (voltages given), the hybrid form (input current and output voltage given) and the chain form (output quantities given) describe the same two-port. Open circuit and short circuit set a given quantity to zero and so isolate the parameters one at a time.

Key points

  • Two-port: four terminals, port condition $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_{1'}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-I_1}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_{2'}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-I_2}$. Port quantities $U_1$, $I_1$, $U_2$, $I_2$; the current arrows point in.

  • Linear, without sources of its own: two quantities fix the others, four parameters.

  • Z: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Z\,\mathbf I}$, open-circuit tests. Y: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Y\,\mathbf U}$, short-circuit tests. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Z^{-1}}$.

  • H: $(U_1,I_2)$ from $(I_1,U_2)$. A: $(U_1,I_1)$ from $(U_2,-I_2)$.

  • A parameter is a quotient of effect and cause, with the other cause set to zero. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ means open circuit, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ means short circuit.

  • The port's own current gives a resistor; the other port's current or voltage gives a controlled source. Voltages add in series, currents in parallel.

  • Load: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{11}-\frac{Z_{12}Z_{21}}{Z_{22}+Z_\mathrm{L}}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Y_{11}-\frac{Y_{12}Y_{21}}{Y_{22}+Y_\mathrm{L}}}$.

  • T: $\mathbf Z$ with $R_\mathrm{a}+R_\mathrm{b}$, $R_\mathrm{b}$, $R_\mathrm{b}+R_\mathrm{c}$. $\pi$: $\mathbf Y$ with $G_1+G_2$, $-G_2$, $G_2+G_3$. Conversion by star-delta.

  • Series, series: Z adds. Parallel, parallel: Y adds. Series, parallel: H adds. Chain: A multiplies. Condition: the port condition remains intact.

  • Reciprocal: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{21}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Y_{21}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-H_{21}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det\mathbf A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. Unilateral: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Y_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det\mathbf A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

  • Power: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_1I_1+U_2I_2}$, active if $P<0$ is possible.

Looking ahead to Chapter 7. The power $P$ from Stage 10 leads to the next question: At which load does a source or a two-port deliver the greatest power, and what does that cost in efficiency?

One principle behind all the results. Of the four port quantities you choose two as causes; the other two are the effects. This choice changes nothing about the two-port, only the calculation, and every result follows from it.
