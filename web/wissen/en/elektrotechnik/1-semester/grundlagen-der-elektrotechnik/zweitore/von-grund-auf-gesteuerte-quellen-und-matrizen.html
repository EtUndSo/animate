From the ground up: Controlled sources and matrices

An ideal source from Chapter 5 supplies a fixed value. A controlled source supplies a value proportional to another voltage or another current elsewhere in the circuit. Its circuit symbol is a diamond. The abbreviations come from the English names (voltage- or current-controlled voltage or current source).

NameAbbreviationLawFactor
voltage-controlled voltage sourceVCVS$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mu\,U_\mathrm{s}}$no unit
current-controlled voltage sourceCCVS$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{r\,I_\mathrm{s}}$in $\Omega$
voltage-controlled current sourceVCCS$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g\,U_\mathrm{s}}$in S
current-controlled current sourceCCCS$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\beta\,I_\mathrm{s}}$no unit

The index s marks the controlling quantity. The best-known example is the bipolar transistor (Chapter 10): A small base current controls a large collector current. The energy for this comes from the supply voltage; the control signal supplies only the information. The controlling quantity and the source sit at different places, so every controlled source is by nature a two-port.

A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers, here almost always with two rows and two columns. Bold letters stand for matrices and for columns of two quantities. In multiplication, each row of the left matrix is combined with each column of the right one, element by element, and the results are added:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}a\,x+b\,y\\c\,x+d\,y\end{pmatrix},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\g&h\end{pmatrix}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}a\,e+b\,g&a\,f+b\,h\\c\,e+d\,g&c\,f+d\,h\end{pmatrix}.}$$

With two matrices, the order matters. Matrices are added element by element. The number $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det\mathbf M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\,d-b\,c}$ is called the determinant. If it is not zero, the inverse matrix exists, which solves $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf M\,\mathbf x}$ for $\mathbf x$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf M^{-1}\mathbf y,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf M^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{a\,d-b\,c}\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}.}$$

The product $\mathbf M\,\mathbf M^{-1}$ is the identity matrix $\mathbf 1$ with ones on the diagonal and zeros elsewhere. It changes nothing it is multiplied by.

  • controlled source – A controlled source supplies a value proportional to another voltage or another current elsewhere in the circuit.
  • matrix – A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers, here almost always with two rows and two columns.
  • determinant – The number $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det\mathbf M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\,d-b\,c}$ is called the determinant.
