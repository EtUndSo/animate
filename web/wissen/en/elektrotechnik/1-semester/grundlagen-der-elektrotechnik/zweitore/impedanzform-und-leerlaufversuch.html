What this stage is about:

The first choice: The currents are the causes. This stage shows why an open port isolates the four numbers one at a time.

The idea. Each voltage is a sum of a part due to $I_1$ and a part due to $I_2$. To see one part alone, the other must vanish, so its current must be zero. A current is zero when its port is open. With port 2 open, only the parts due to $I_1$ remain in both equations. So one test gives two parameters, and the test with the ports swapped gives the other two.

Definition 3.1 (Impedance form, Z form)

The impedance form, also resistance form, takes the currents as causes:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{11}I_1+Z_{12}I_2,\quad U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{21}I_1+Z_{22}I_2,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\text{in short}\quad\mathbf U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Z\cdot\mathbf I,\quad\mathbf Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}Z_{11}&Z_{12}\\mathbb{Z}_{21}&Z_{22}\end{pmatrix}.}$$
(3.1)

The Z parameters follow from two open-circuit tests:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left.\frac{U_1}{I_1}\right|_{I_2=0},\quad Z_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left.\frac{U_2}{I_1}\right|_{I_2=0},\quad Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left.\frac{U_1}{I_2}\right|_{I_1=0},\quad Z_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left.\frac{U_2}{I_2}\right|_{I_1=0}.}$$
(3.2)

⁠How to read it: $Z_{11}$ is the open-circuit input resistance, $Z_{22}$ the open-circuit output resistance, $Z_{21}$ the open-circuit transfer resistance in the forward direction and $Z_{12}$ the one in the reverse direction. All have the unit $\Omega$, typically ohms to megohms. Parameters with equal indices are called two-terminal quantities: They are the resistance of a terminal pair with the other side fixed in its termination. Parameters with different indices are called transfer quantities: They link the two sides. The letter $Z$ stands for impedance. It generalizes resistance to capacitors and coils with AC quantities. Here all $Z$ are real resistances, for which $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{ii}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_{ii}}$. Validity: Both currents must be freely selectable (Stage 6).

Equivalent circuit. The two equations can be drawn (Fig. 4 left). A sum of voltages is a series connection. $Z_{11}I_1$ depends on the port's own current, which makes it a resistor. $Z_{12}I_2$ depends on the other port's current, which makes it a controlled voltage source (CCVS). Port 2 is built in exactly the same way. This picture shows the mechanism of the open circuit: At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, the source $Z_{12}I_2$ supplies zero volts and $Z_{22}$ carries no current (Fig. 4 right). $U_1$ drops across $Z_{11}$ alone, and $U_2$ is the source voltage $Z_{21}I_1$.

Example

On the T two-port, a current of 100 mA is impressed at port 1, and port 2 stays open. The current runs through $R_\mathrm{a}$ and $R_\mathrm{b}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.5}$ V. $R_\mathrm{c}$ carries no current, nothing drops across it, and $U_2$ is the voltage across $R_\mathrm{b}$: 2 V. So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{35\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\Omega}$. The same from port 2 gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ V, so

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}R_\mathrm{a}+R_\mathrm{b}&R_\mathrm{b}\\mathbb{R}_\mathrm{b}&R_\mathrm{b}+R_\mathrm{c}\end{pmatrix}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}35&20\\20&40\end{pmatrix}\Omega.}$$

⁠The bridge. The tests need no ground line. Because its branch resistances are equal, the bridge on the right of Fig. 2 divides a current at port 1 in half; both branches have $R_\mathrm{x}+R_\mathrm{y}$. Port 2 is open, and the voltage between the branch midpoints is $\frac{I_1}{2}R_\mathrm{y}-\frac{I_1}{2}R_\mathrm{x}$. By symmetry, the same holds in reverse:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(R_\mathrm{x}+R_\mathrm{y})\parallel(R_\mathrm{x}+R_\mathrm{y})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{R_\mathrm{x}+R_\mathrm{y}}{2},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{R_\mathrm{y}-R_\mathrm{x}}{2}.}$$
(3.3)

⁠Limiting cases: For $R_\mathrm{b}\to0$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: The shunt branch is a wire, and the ports are decoupled. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{x}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{y}}$, the bridge is balanced (Chapter 4), and again $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

QuestionWhy a current and not a voltage at the input? I'm more likely to have a voltage source at hand.

That works too. All that counts is that the other port is open. You then measure $U_1$ and $I_1$ and form the quotient. The impressed current merely fits the way of thinking of this form: It is the cause, the voltages are the effects.

Exercise 3 ★★ The bridge as a two-port

In the bridge from Fig. 2, let $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{x}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{30\,\Omega}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{y}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\Omega}$.

  1. Determine $\mathbf Z$ and from it a T network with the same four parameters.

  2. With 100 mA at port 1 and port 2 open: What are $U_1$ and $U_2$?

  3. Swap the values, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{x}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{y}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{30\,\Omega}$. What changes in $\mathbf Z$ and in the T network? What does the result mean?

Solution

(a) With (3.3): $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{40\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$. T network: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{b}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{a}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{c}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{30\,\Omega}$. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{40\,\Omega\cdot100\,\mathrm{mA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega\cdot100\,\mathrm{mA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ V. Directly: 50 mA through each branch, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\mathrm{mA}\cdot(50-30)\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ V. (c) $Z_{11}$ and $Z_{22}$ stay the same, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-10\,\Omega}$. The T network would have $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{b}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-10\,\Omega}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{a}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{c}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\Omega}$. The negative element does not exist as a component; here the T is only a calculation aid. It says: For the same $I_1$, $U_2$ has the opposite sign.
