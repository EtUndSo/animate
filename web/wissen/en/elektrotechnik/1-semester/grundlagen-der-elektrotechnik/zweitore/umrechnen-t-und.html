What this stage is about:

The Z form and the Y form describe the same two-port. This stage shows how to get from one to the other, and when a form does not exist at all.

The idea. The Y form and the Z form are the same two equations. One is solved for the voltages, the other for the currents. So the transition is the solving of a system of equations, and that is what the inverse matrix from the section “From the ground up” does.

Theorem 6.1 (Conversion between Z and Y)

The conductance matrix and the resistance matrix are inverses of each other, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Y\cdot\mathbf Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf 1}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Z^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\det\mathbf Z}\begin{pmatrix}Z_{22}&-Z_{12}\\-Z_{21}&Z_{11}\end{pmatrix},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Y^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\det\mathbf Y}\begin{pmatrix}Y_{22}&-Y_{12}\\-Y_{21}&Y_{11}\end{pmatrix}}$$
(6.1)

with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det\mathbf Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{11}Z_{22}-Z_{12}Z_{21}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det\mathbf Y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Y_{11}Y_{22}-Y_{12}Y_{21}}$.

⁠How to read it: The whole matrix is inverted, not each number on its own. Each element gets the whole denominator, which is why each Y parameter depends on all the Z parameters. Validity: The determinant must not be zero.

Derivation: Two ways to the inverse

⁠With matrices. From $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Y\mathbf U}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Z\mathbf I}$ follows $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Y\mathbf Z\,\mathbf I}$ for every $\mathbf I$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Y\mathbf Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf 1}$.

⁠With short-circuit tests on the Z equivalent circuit. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{21}I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-Z_{22}I_2}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{Z_{21}}{Z_{22}}I_1}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\bigl(Z_{11}-Z_{12}\frac{Z_{21}}{Z_{22}}\bigr)I_1}$. This gives

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{I_1}{U_1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{Z_{22}}{Z_{11}Z_{22}-Z_{12}Z_{21}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{I_2}{U_1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{Z_{21}}{Z_{11}Z_{22}-Z_{12}Z_{21}}.}$$

At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-Z_{11}I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{12}I_2}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{Z_{12}}{Z_{11}}I_2}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\bigl(Z_{22}-Z_{21}\frac{Z_{12}}{Z_{11}}\bigr)I_2}$. This gives

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{Z_{12}}{Z_{11}Z_{22}-Z_{12}Z_{21}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{Z_{11}}{Z_{11}Z_{22}-Z_{12}Z_{21}}.}$$

T and $\pi$. The Z matrix of the T network from Stage 3 can be inverted with (6.1); the Y matrix of the $\pi$ network can be read directly from short-circuit tests. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{a}R_\mathrm{b}+R_\mathrm{a}R_\mathrm{c}+R_\mathrm{b}R_\mathrm{c}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\det\mathbf Z}$ and the conductances $G_1$, $G_3$ in shunt and $G_2$ in series,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Y_\mathrm{T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1D\begin{pmatrix}R_\mathrm{b}+R_\mathrm{c}&-R_\mathrm{b}\\-R_\mathrm{b}&R_\mathrm{a}+R_\mathrm{b}\end{pmatrix},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Y_\pi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}G_1+G_2&-G_2\\-G_2&G_2+G_3\end{pmatrix}.}$$
(6.2)

Setting the two equal gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-Y_{12}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Y_{11}+Y_{12}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Y_{22}+Y_{21}}$. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\mathbf Y_\pi$ becomes the pair $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(G_1+G_2)U_1-G_2U_2}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-G_2U_1+G_2U_2}$. This is the star-delta transformation from Chapter 5: T is the star, $\pi$ the delta. Description by Z parameters leads naturally to the T network, description by Y parameters to the dual $\pi$ network. Conversely, every two-port with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{21}}$ has a T and a $\pi$ equivalent circuit without sources (Stage 10).

Example

Here $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det\mathbf Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{35\cdot40\,\Omega^2-20\cdot20\,\Omega^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1000\,\Omega^2}$, so

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{1000\,\Omega^2}\begin{pmatrix}40&-20\\-20&35\end{pmatrix}\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}40&-20\\-20&35\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{mS},}$$

exactly the result of the short-circuit tests. The $\pi$ network has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20}$ mS, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{40\,\mathrm{mS}-20\,\mathrm{mS}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20}$ mS, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{15}$ mS, that is, 50 $\Omega$, 50 $\Omega$ and 66.7 $\Omega$. With the same source and load, it again gives 7.75 V and 3 V.

⁠Limiting cases: Not every form exists. A series resistor $R$ and a shunt resistor $R$, each alone between the ports, have

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Y_\text{series}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{G\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Z_\text{shunt}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.}$$

Both determinants are zero. The series resistor has no Z form: There $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-I_1}$ always holds, the currents cannot be chosen freely, so they are no good as causes. The shunt resistor has no Y form because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_2}$ always holds. The same applies to the ideal current-controlled current source: Its currents are rigidly coupled.

QuestionWhy is $Y_{11}$ positive but $Y_{21}$ negative?

That comes from the reference arrows. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, a positive voltage at port 1 drives a current in, $Y_{11}>0$. This current comes out at the other port, but it is counted going in. That is why the transfer conductances of passive circuits like T and $\pi$ are negative.

Exercise 6 ★ There and back

A two-port has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{55\,\Omega}$.

  1. Calculate $\mathbf Y$.

  2. Determine the resistors of the T and the $\pi$ network.

  3. Write down $\mathbf Y$ of a series resistor of 100 $\Omega$. Why does $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf Y^{-1}}$ fail?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det\mathbf Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\cdot55\,\Omega^2-10\cdot10\,\Omega^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1000\,\Omega^2}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{55}$ mS, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Y_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-10}$ mS, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20}$ mS. (b) T: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{b}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{a}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{c}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{45\,\Omega}$. $\pi$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ mS, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{45}$ mS, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ mS, that is, 100 $\Omega$ in series, 22.2 $\Omega$ and 100 $\Omega$ in shunt. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Y_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ mS, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Y_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Y_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-10}$ mS, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det\mathbf Y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. There is no inverse, because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-I_1}$ always holds and the currents cannot be chosen freely.
