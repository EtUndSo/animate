Stage 7: Hybrid form

What this stage is about:

With an amplifier, it is easy to specify the current at the input and the voltage at the output. This stage mixes the causes.

The idea. Nothing forces you to take the same kind of cause at both ports. If you take $I_1$ and $U_2$, then $U_1$ and $I_2$ are the effects. This suits components like the bipolar transistor: Its input is controlled by a current, and its output acts like a current source with a large internal resistance. The tests again follow from the choice: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ means a short circuit at the output, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ an open circuit at the input.

Definition 7.1 (Hybrid form, H form)

The hybrid form takes $I_1$ and $U_2$ as causes:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H_{11}I_1+H_{12}U_2,\quad I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H_{21}I_1+H_{22}U_2,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\text{in short}\quad \begin{pmatrix}U_1\\I_2\end{pmatrix}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf H\begin{pmatrix}I_1\\U_2\end{pmatrix}.}$$
(7.1)

The H parameters are

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left.\frac{U_1}{I_1}\right|_{U_2=0},\quad H_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left.\frac{I_2}{I_1}\right|_{U_2=0},\quad H_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left.\frac{U_1}{U_2}\right|_{I_1=0},\quad H_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\left.\frac{I_2}{U_2}\right|_{I_1=0}.}$$
(7.2)

⁠How to read it: $H_{11}$ is the short-circuit input resistance in $\Omega$, typically a few k$\Omega$ for the transistor. $H_{21}$ is the forward short-circuit current ratio, the current gain, without a unit, about 100 to 500. $H_{12}$ is the open-circuit reverse voltage ratio, without a unit, around $10^{-4}$. $H_{22}$ is the open-circuit output conductance in S, a few tens of µ S. The units are mixed, hence the name. The matrix is also called the series-parallel matrix (Stage 9). In the equivalent circuit (Fig. 9 left), voltages add at the input: a series connection of $H_{11}$ and a VCVS $H_{12}U_2$. Currents add at the output: a parallel connection of $H_{22}$ and a CCCS $H_{21}I_1$.

Example

The T two-port with 100 mA at the input and the output short-circuited: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.5}$ V, and the current divides in half, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-50}$ mA, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{25\,\Omega}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.5}$. With the input open and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ V: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ V, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{25}$ mS, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$. The amplifier model of the chapter has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{22}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50}$ µ S, that is, an output resistance of 20 k$\Omega$, and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. This is an approximation that amounts to around 1 % (Exercise 7). At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20}$ mV, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ µ A flows, and the source at the output drives 1 mA into $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{20\,\text{k}\Omega\parallel5\,\text{k}\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ k$\Omega$. This gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-4}$ V, a voltage gain of $-200$. The minus comes from the fact that $H_{21}I_1$ runs into port 2, so the current flows through the load from below.

⁠Limiting cases: For $H_{22}\to0$ the output becomes an ideal current source, for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{11}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ the input becomes a short circuit. The ideal current-amplifier model is exactly that: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H_{21}I_1}$.

QuestionWhy does $H_{21}$ have a minus in the T two-port but not in the amplifier?

The T two-port contains no source. A current into port 1 can only run out of port 2, $I_2$ is negative, and its magnitude is smaller than $I_1$. In the amplifier, $H_{21}$ is the law of a controlled source, which itself sets the direction and size of the current and is fed from an auxiliary source.

Exercise 7 ★★ How much does the reverse action matter?

The amplifier model is given a small reverse action $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5\cdot10^{-5}}$, with the other values the same, load 5 k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ µ A.

  1. Do $U_2$ and $I_2$ change compared with the example? Justify with (7.1).

  2. What is $U_1$ now? By what percentage does it change?

  3. What are the voltage gain and the input resistance?

Solution

(a) No. The second line of (7.1) does not contain $H_{12}$, and $I_1$ is fixed: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-4}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.8}$ mA. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\,\mathrm{mV}+5\cdot10^{-5}\cdot(-4\,\mathrm{V})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{19.8}$ mV, 1 % less. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2/U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-202}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{19.8\,\mathrm{mV}/10\,\mu\text{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.98}$ k$\Omega$. The reverse action is small here, which is why it is often neglected.
