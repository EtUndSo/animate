The answer in three sentences

  1. Charge is a property of electrons and protons, the charged building blocks of atoms. Like charges repel, opposite charges attract, and the sum of all charges is conserved.

  2. Every charge produces a field around itself. The field $\vec E$ determines the force per charge at every location: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q\,\vec E}$.

  3. In a static field, the work done on a charge depends only on start and end, not on the path. That is why every point has a potential $\varphi$, and the voltage $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_1-\varphi_2}$ is the energy per charge that the field gives up on the way from point 1 to point 2.

Key points

  • Quantity = numerical value $\cdot$ unit. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\,\mathrm{J/C}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1\,\mathrm{C}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\,\mathrm{A\,s}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1\,\mathrm{N}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s^2}}$.

  • Work: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int\vec F\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec s}$, on a straight path $F\,s\cos\alpha$. Kinetic energy: $\frac12mv^2$.

  • Charge: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(N_\mathrm{p}-N_\mathrm{e})\,e}$; it is conserved.

  • Coulomb: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k\,|Q_\mathrm{A}Q_\mathrm{B}|/r^2}$; like charges repel, opposite charges attract.

  • Field: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q\vec E}$, direction as for the force on a positive charge; point charge $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k|Q_0|/r^2}$.

  • Field lines: from $+$ to $-$, never cross, perpendicular to conductors; in metal $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

  • Work of the field: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q\int_{P_1}^{P_2}\vec E\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec s}$; curl-free: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\oint\vec E\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

  • Potential: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi(P)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_P^{P_0}\vec E\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec s}$; point charge $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{kQ_0/r}$; $\vec E$ points toward falling $\varphi$.

  • Voltage: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_1-\varphi_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{P_1}^{P_2}\vec E\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{W_{12}/Q}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_{21}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-U_{12}}$; uniform field $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U/d}$.

  • Ground: $\varphi:=0$; its location changes no voltage.

  • Charge in the field: $\frac12mv^2+Q\varphi$ constant; from rest $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{2|QU|/m}}$.

  • Induction: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_\mathrm{ind}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-N\,\mathrm{d}\Phi/\mathrm{d} t}$; the field is then no longer curl-free.

Looking ahead to Chapter 2. In a wire, the field drives very many charges at once: charge flows and a current arises. Chapter 2 shows why voltage and current are proportional and how they divide in a network.

One principle behind all the results. The force on a charge and its energy depend on the other charges around it and on the charge itself. Dividing by the charge itself leaves a property of the location: force becomes field strength, energy becomes potential.
