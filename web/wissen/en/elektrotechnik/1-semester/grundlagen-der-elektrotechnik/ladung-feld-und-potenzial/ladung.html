What this stage is about:

This stage settles the first question: What is charge, how does a charged body come about, and what is conserved in the process?

The idea. Frictional electricity, lightning as the discharge of separated charges through the air, and magnetism cannot be explained by mechanics. If a glass rod is rubbed with a silk cloth and a hard-rubber rod with fur, the two rods attract each other, while two hard-rubber rods repel (Fig. 3). Rubbing creates nothing new; electrons change bodies. Different materials bind their outer electrons with different strength. At the points of contact, electrons pass to the material that binds them more strongly, and rubbing creates many such points. A body with an electron deficit is positively charged, one with an electron excess negatively.

Definition 2.1 (Charge)

A body with $N_\mathrm{p}$ protons and $N_\mathrm{e}$ electrons carries the charge

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(N_\mathrm{p}-N_\mathrm{e})\,e,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.602\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{[Q]}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{A\,s}.}$$
(2.1)

The elementary charge $e$ is the smallest charge occurring freely. An electron carries $-e$, a proton $+e$. In a closed system, the sum of all charges stays constant (conservation of charge).

⁠How to read it: Charge occurs only in multiples of $e$; one coulomb is $6.24\cdot10^{18}$ elementary charges. Rubbed rods carry around 10 nC. Validity: These steps are noticeable only for individual particles; even 1 nC is six billion electrons. In circuits, charge is therefore treated as a continuous quantity.

Derivation: Why rubbing does not change the total charge

Two bodies together carry $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_1+Q_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\bigl[(N_\mathrm{p1}+N_\mathrm{p2})-(N_\mathrm{e1}+N_\mathrm{e2})\bigr]e}$. If an electron passes from 1 to 2, $N_\mathrm{e1}$ decreases by one and $N_\mathrm{e2}$ increases by one; the bracket stays the same. The charges “generated” by rubbing always add up to zero.

The names are older than the electron. In 1733 DuFay distinguished vitreous and resinous electricity; around 1750 Franklin called one positive and the other negative. The Greek word for amber, elektron, gave the field its name.

Conductors and insulators. In a conductor such as metal, at least one electron per atom can wander through the entire body. In an insulator such as glass or rubber, the electrons stay with their atoms. That is why charge on an insulator stays where it was produced, whereas on metal it spreads immediately.

Example

Sphere A ($+10$ nC) lacks $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{N}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q/e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6.24\cdot10^{10}}$ electrons. A plastic sphere of 1 g contains around $3.2\cdot10^{23}$ electrons, so only one in $5\cdot10^{12}$ is missing.

⁠Limiting case: For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{N_\mathrm{p}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{N_\mathrm{e}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. A neutral body does not contain little charge; it contains equal amounts of both signs.

QuestionWhy, of all things, is the lack of electrons called positive?

Because the signs were assigned before electrons were known. Franklin called rubbed glass positive; only much later did it turn out that glass loses electrons in the process. The convention stayed, so the electron carries negative charge. There is nothing special about this physically.

Exercise 2 ★ Counting charges

  1. After rubbing, a glass rod carries $+8$ nC. How many electrons has it given up? What charge does the cloth carry if both were neutral beforehand?

  2. Two identical metal spheres carry $+8$ nC and $-2$ nC. They touch and are separated. What charge does each carry? How many electrons have moved across, and in which direction?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{N}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}/(1.602\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.99\cdot10^{10}}$ electrons. The cloth has taken them up and carries $-8$ nC. (b) The total charge $+6$ nC is conserved and divides evenly between identical spheres: $+3$ nC each. The negative sphere therefore gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{5\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}/e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.12\cdot10^{10}}$ electrons to the positive one.

Summary

Terms
  • electron deficit, electron excess – A body with an electron deficit is positively charged, one with an electron excess negatively.
  • elementary charge – The elementary charge $e$ is the smallest charge occurring freely.
  • conservation of charge – In a closed system, the sum of all charges stays constant (conservation of charge).
  • conductor – Conductors and insulators. In a conductor such as metal, at least one electron per atom can wander through the entire body.
  • insulator – In an insulator such as glass or rubber, the electrons stay with their atoms.
