What this is about#

A small rectangular battery is labeled 9 V. This figure is a voltage. It does not say how much energy the battery contains, but how much energy each unit of charge gives up as it passes from one terminal to the other: 9 joules per coulomb. Charge is the property of certain particles of exerting and feeling electric forces. The joule is the unit of energy, the coulomb the unit of charge. The question of this chapter is: Why is voltage always a difference between two points, and what does it do to a charge?

The answer leads through two concepts. An electric field determines, for every location, what force acts on a charge there. The potential determines, for every location, how much energy per coulomb a charge has there relative to a freely chosen zero point, the reference point.

The reference model for this chapter

  • ⁠Pair of plates: two flat metal plates A and B in vacuum (a space empty of air), separation $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{d}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ mm, small compared with the plate width. A 9 V battery is connected with its plus terminal to A and its minus terminal to B. The axis $x$ runs from A ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$) to B. Between the plates there is the uniform field $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ kV/m (Stages 5 and 8).

  • ⁠Two spheres: small spheres with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_\mathrm{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{+10}$ nC and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_\mathrm{B}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-20}$ nC at the distance $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ cm. Sphere A has the mass 1 g.

  • ⁠Circuit: the same battery across two resistors in series, 6 V across the first, 3 V across the second. A resistor is a component in which passing charge gives up energy as heat (Chapter 2).

  • ⁠Constants: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.602\cdot10^{-19}}$ C, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{m_\mathrm{e}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9.109\cdot10^{-31}}$ kg, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{k}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8.99\cdot10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}$; the stages explain them.

Summary

Terms
  • voltage – A small rectangular battery is labeled 9 V. This figure is a voltage.
  • charge – It does not say how much energy the battery contains, but how much energy each unit of charge gives up as it passes from one terminal to the other: 9 joules per coulomb.
  • electric field – An electric field determines, for every location, what force acts on a charge there.
  • potential, reference point – The potential determines, for every location, how much energy per coulomb a charge has there relative to a freely chosen zero point, the reference point.
  • resistor – A resistor is a component in which passing charge gives up energy as heat (Chapter 2).
