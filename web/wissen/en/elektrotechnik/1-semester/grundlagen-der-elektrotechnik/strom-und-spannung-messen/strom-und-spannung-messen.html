What this is about#

A voltage divider is supposed to have 3 V across it, and the meter shows 2.73 V. The divider is fine and the reading is correct: it shows the voltage that has been present since the meter was connected. That is because a meter is a component with an internal resistance, the resistance between its terminals. The question is: Why does every meter change the circuit it measures, how large is the error, and why does a bridge measure more accurately than an instrument alone?

The reference model for this chapter

  • ⁠Instrument: a meter movement (the part of the device that converts current into a pointer deflection) with full-scale deflection at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50}$ µ A and its own resistance $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ k$\Omega$. As an ammeter in the measuring range (the range of values it can display) up to 10 mA it has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{iA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10\,\Omega}$; as a voltmeter in the range up to 10 V it has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{iV}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{200}$ k$\Omega$ (Stage 2). Its reading deviates by at most 1 % of the full-scale value.

  • ⁠Object under test: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.8}$ k$\Omega$ on an ideal voltage source $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9}$ V (it holds 9 V at any current). Without a meter, 5 mA flow.

  • ⁠Divider: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{60}$ k$\Omega$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{30}$ k$\Omega$ in series across 9 V; there are 3 V across $R_2$.

  • ⁠Bridge: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.8}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ k$\Omega$ at 9 V; $R_4$ is wanted. For small changes, four resistors of 350 $\Omega$ each at 9 V.
