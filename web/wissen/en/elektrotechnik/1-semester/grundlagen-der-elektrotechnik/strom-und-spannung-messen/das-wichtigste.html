The answer in three sentences

  1. An ammeter (current meter) sits in series in the circuit and adds its internal resistance to it. A voltmeter (voltage meter) sits in parallel and opens a second current path. Both show the value of the changed circuit.

  2. The relative error is about $R_\mathrm i/R$ for the ammeter and about $R/R_\mathrm i$ for the voltmeter. If both measure at once, only one reading is right: correct-current measurement (the current is right) or correct-voltage measurement (the voltage is right).

  3. The Wheatstone bridge (two voltage dividers on one source with an instrument between them) compares resistances. At balance (adjusted to a reading of zero), no current flows through the instrument. That is why its internal resistance, its scale and the source voltage drop out of the result $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_3R_2/R_1}$.

Key points

  • A meter is a resistor and shows the value of the changed circuit.

  • Ammeter: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_R/I_R'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+R_\mathrm i/R}$. Voltmeter: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_L/U_L'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+R_L/R_\mathrm i}$.

  • Extending the range: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{Shunt}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{old}I_\mathrm{old}/(I_\mathrm{new}-I_\mathrm{old})}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm V}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_\mathrm{old}(\alpha-1)}$.

  • Correct current $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U/I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R+R_\mathrm{iA}}$ for large $R$, correct voltage $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U/I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R\parallel R_\mathrm{iV}}$ for small $R$.

  • Balance: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_3R_2/R_1}$, independent of internal resistance, scale and source voltage.

  • Unbalanced: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm d}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac14U_\mathrm q\,\Delta R/R}$. Without the common voltage, a small range is enough.

One principle behind all the results. To the circuit, a meter is one more resistor. All the errors follow from the same rules as in Chapter 2: mesh rule, node rule, Ohm's law.
