Stage 4: Why the current grows exponentially

What this stage is about:

The barrier is in place. This stage applies an external voltage and shows why the current grows exponentially in one direction and stays almost zero in the other.

The idea. An external voltage $U$ between the p-side (anode) and the n-side (cathode) drops almost entirely across the region, because only there are charge carriers missing. The neutral regions conduct well. So the barrier changes to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W_\mathrm B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{e(U_\mathrm{D0}-U)}$ (Fig. 5). Two electron currents run across the region:

  • ⁠Uphill: majority electrons of the n-region. Only the fraction $\mathrm{e}^{-W_\mathrm B/(k_\mathrm BT)}$ gets over the barrier. Every $k_\mathrm BT$ less barrier increases it by a factor of 2.718.

  • ⁠Downhill: minority electrons of the p-region that reach the edge of the region. For them the potential step is a slope, and they always get through. Their number does not depend on the barrier.

The same applies to holes in the opposite direction. Without voltage both currents are equally large, so nothing flows.

Theorem 4.1 (Shockley equation)

For the ideal diode with $U$ from anode to cathode and $I$ from anode to cathode,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm S\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{U/U_T}-1\bigr),}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{k_\mathrm BT}{e}.}$$
(4.1)

⁠How to read it: $I_\mathrm S$ is the reverse saturation current (reverse current for short), $10^{-15}$ to $10^{-9}$ A depending on area and doping. The thermal voltage $U_T$ is the thermal energy $k_\mathrm BT$ in volts, divided by $e$, so 25.85 mV at 300 K. Important: $k_\mathrm BT$ alone is an energy; only $k_\mathrm BT/e$ is a voltage. The factor of 10 (one decade) costs $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_T\ln10}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{59.5}$ mV, so about 60 mV. Validity: (4.1) holds for $U<U_\mathrm{D0}$ and without breakdown (Stage 5). In the forward direction, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{ I_\mathrm S\mathrm{e}^{U/U_T}}$ is enough.

Derivation: From the barrier to the characteristic curve

  1. Uphill: the current is proportional to the fraction above the barrier, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\text{up}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{c\,\mathrm{e}^{-(U_\mathrm{D0}-U)/U_T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_0\,\mathrm{e}^{U/U_T}}$. Here $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{c\,\mathrm{e}^{-U_\mathrm{D0}/U_T}}$ collects everything that does not depend on $U$.

  2. Downhill: $I_\text{down}$ does not depend on $U$.

  3. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ no current flows, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\text{down}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_0}$. The net current is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\text{up}-I_\text{down}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_0(\mathrm{e}^{U/U_T}-1)}$.

  4. Electrons and holes have the same barrier, and their currents add with the same factor: $I_0$ becomes $I_\mathrm S$.

Simplification: in reality the size of $I_\mathrm S$ is determined by the diffusion of the minority in the neutral regions behind the space-charge region. The factor $\mathrm{e}^{U/U_T}$ remains, because it sets the carrier density at the edge of the region.

Example

With (4.1), the reference model gives:

$I$1 µ A10 µ A100 µ A1 mA10 mA
$U$0.476 V0.536 V0.595 V0.655 V0.714 V

At 0.655 V the barrier has shrunk to $2.3\,U_T$, and the fraction has risen from $10^{-12}$ to 0.1.

⁠Limiting cases: At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. For $U\ll-U_T$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-I_\mathrm S}$ remains: already at $-5\,U_T$ it is 99.3 % of that. From $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{119}$ mV on, the term $-1$ is smaller than 1 % of the first term.

Reverse direction. At $U<0$ the barrier grows and the region becomes wider. The uphill current dies out, and only the minority current remains. It does not depend on how high the barrier is, only on how many carriers reach its edge. That is why it saturates at $I_\mathrm S$. Real diodes block with pA to nA, because additional pairs are generated in the region.

QuestionWhat happens when $U$ becomes larger than $U_\mathrm{D0}$? Is the barrier gone then?

The formula no longer applies there. At such a high current, the carriers that have crossed the barrier are as dense in the foreign region as the doping, and the neutral regions have a noticeable resistance (bulk resistance, a few $\Omega$). The rest of the external voltage drops there, and the characteristic curve becomes flatter and almost straight. The barrier never becomes negative.

Going deeper, not needed for the rest: Temperature

$I_\mathrm S$ contains $n_\mathrm i^2$ and grows by a factor of about 4 per 10 K. This outweighs the growth of $U_T$: at a fixed current the voltage falls by about 2 mV/K. Real diodes often follow $\mathrm{e}^{U/(mU_T)}$ with $1\le m\le2$.

Exercise 4 ★ Decades

  1. The diode of the reference model shows 0.655 V at 1 mA. Without a calculator, estimate $U$ at 100 mA and at 10 µ A.

  2. By how many mV does $U$ rise when the current doubles? When it triples?

  3. What current flows at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$ V and at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.2}$ V?

Solution

(a) 100 mA is two decades more: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0.655\,\mathrm{V}+2\cdot0.060\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.775}$ V (exactly 0.774 V). 10 µ A is two decades less: 0.535 V (exactly 0.536 V). (b) Doubling: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_T\ln2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{17.9}$ mV. Tripling: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_T\ln3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{28.4}$ mV. (c) At $-1$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{U/U_T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-10^{-14}}$ A. At $-0.2$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{-7.7}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.4\cdot10^{-4}}$, so likewise $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-10^{-14}}$ A to within 0.05 %.
