Stage 7: Linear equivalent circuits

What this stage is about:

With several diodes, several exponential equations become coupled, and that can no longer be done by hand. This stage replaces the diode with linear parts and gives a method that always works.

The idea. Over three decades of current, $U_\mathrm D$ changes by only 180 mV. So the characteristic curve is almost a right angle, and the component almost a switch. The price of the model: you must know the switching state of each diode. You assume it and check it afterward.

Definition 7.1 (Two diode models)

Ideal diode: a short circuit when conducting, an open circuit when blocking.

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm D>0\ \Rightarrow\ U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D<0\ \Rightarrow\ I_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.}$$
(7.1)

Diode with forward voltage: a voltage source $U_\mathrm F$ when conducting, an open circuit when blocking.

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm D>0\ \Rightarrow\ U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm F,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D<U_\mathrm F\ \Rightarrow\ I_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.}$$
(7.2)

⁠How to read it: $U_\mathrm F$ is called the forward voltage, also the forward drop. It is neither a material value like $U_\mathrm{D0}$ nor a threshold, but a model value: you choose the voltage at the expected current. Common values are 0.6 V or 0.7 V. The model diode has 0.6 V at 0.12 mA and 0.7 V at 5.8 mA. Validity: In the reference model, exactly $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.31}$ mA. The ideal diode gives 5.00 mA (16 % too much), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.7}$ V gives 4.30 mA ($-0.2$ %), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6}$ V gives 4.40 mA (2 % too much). The ideal diode is sufficient only when the voltages in the circuit are large compared with 0.7 V.

The method.

  1. Choose an equivalent circuit for the diodes.

  2. Make a plausible assumption about which diode conducts and which blocks.

  3. Replace the diodes and calculate all currents and voltages.

  4. Check: conducting diodes need $I_\mathrm D>0$, blocking ones $U_\mathrm D<U_\mathrm F$. If there is a contradiction, make a new assumption.

$n$ diodes have $2^n$ combinations, so a good first assumption pays off. A look at the potentials helps.

Example

Fig. 9 bottom: 5 V and 2 V feed node X through 1 k$\Omega$ and a diode each; from there $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}$ leads to ground. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.7}$ V.

⁠Assumption 1: Both conduct. The node rule (Chapter 2) at X reads $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(4.3\,\mathrm{V}-\varphi_\mathrm X)/R_1+(1.3\,\mathrm{V}-\varphi_\mathrm X)/R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_\mathrm X/R_3}$. This gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm X}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.24}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{D1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.06}$ mA and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{D2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.94}$ mA. The negative current is a contradiction.

⁠Assumption 2: D$_1$ conducts, D$_2$ blocks. Then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(5\,\mathrm{V}-0.7\,\mathrm{V})/3\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.43}$ mA and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm X}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.87}$ V. Across D$_2$ is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{D2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\mathrm{V}-2.87\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.87}$ V. That is smaller than $U_\mathrm F$, no contradiction. The APs are D$_1$ (0.7 V | 1.43 mA) and D$_2$ ($-0.87$ V | 0). The full simulation gives 2.89 V and 1.45 mA.

⁠View of the potentials: D$_2$ conducts only if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm X<2\,\mathrm{V}-0.7\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.3}$ V. The 5-V source alone already raises X to 2.87 V, so the cathode of D$_2$ lies above the anode. The higher voltage prevails. Assumption 3 (only D$_2$ conducts) would give $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{D1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.13}$ V, assumption 4 (both block) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{D1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ V, both larger than $U_\mathrm F$ and thus contradictions.

⁠Limiting case: At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ V both branches are equal, and each diode carries half.

QuestionWhy is a negative current a contradiction? A voltage source can absorb current, after all.

The equivalent source can, the diode cannot: in the reverse direction only $I_\mathrm S$ flows. The model applies only for $I_\mathrm D>0$. A negative result shows that the calculation used the diode outside this range.

Exercise 8 ★★ Who wins?

In the example, the 2-V source becomes the source $U_2$.

  1. Let $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.5}$ V. Which diodes conduct? Calculate $\varphi_\mathrm X$ and all diode currents.

  2. From which $U_2$ on does D$_2$ begin to conduct?

Solution

(a) Assumption: both conduct. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(4.3\,\mathrm{V}-\varphi_\mathrm X)/R_1+(3.8\,\mathrm{V}-\varphi_\mathrm X)/R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_\mathrm X/R_3}$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm X}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.24}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{D1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.06}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{D2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.56}$ mA. Both positive, no contradiction. Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1.62\,\mathrm{mA}\cdot2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.24}$ V. (b) As long as D$_2$ blocks, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm X}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.87}$ V. D$_2$ begins to conduct when $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2-0.7\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.87}$ V, that is, at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.57}$ V.

Exercise 9 ★★★ Protection diodes

The input of a chip is node X. A voltage $U_\mathrm e$ feeds it through $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}$. D$_1$ leads from X (anode) to the supply $+5$ V. D$_2$ leads from ground (anode) to X. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.7}$ V.

  1. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V, $12$ V and $-3$ V, determine which diode conducts, as well as $\varphi_\mathrm X$ and the current through $R$.

  2. Sketch $\varphi_\mathrm X$ versus $U_\mathrm e$.

  3. Why can one of the four combinations be ruled out without calculation?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V: no diode conducts, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm X}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V. Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{D1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{D2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-3}$ V. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ V: D$_1$ conducts, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm X}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.7}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6.3}$ mA flows into the supply. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-3}$ V: D$_2$ conducts, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm X}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.7}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2.3}$ mA, the current comes from ground. (b) A ramp $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_\mathrm X}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_\mathrm e}$ between $-0.7$ V and 5.7 V, horizontal outside: the input stays protected. (c) If both conducted, $\varphi_\mathrm X$ would have to be 5.7 V and $-0.7$ V at the same time.
