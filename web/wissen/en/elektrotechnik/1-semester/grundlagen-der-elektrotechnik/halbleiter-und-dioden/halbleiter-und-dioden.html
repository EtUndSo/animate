What this is about#

A charger turns the AC voltage of the wall outlet into the DC voltage for the rechargeable battery. The component that does this is called a diode: it lets current through in one direction (forward direction) and blocks it in the other (reverse direction). It consists of a semiconductor, a material whose conductivity lies between that of a metal and that of an insulator and can be set deliberately. The question of this chapter is: How does a piece of silicon become a valve for current, and how do you calculate with a component that does not obey Ohm's law?

The answer runs through six terms. Doping means building in a few foreign atoms, which fixes how many charge carriers (mobile charges) the crystal has. At the boundary between two differently doped regions, the pn junction, the space-charge region forms by itself, a thin layer without mobile charges that acts as an energy barrier. The characteristic curve is the graph of current versus voltage, the operating point the pair of voltage and current that settles in a circuit.

The reference model for this chapter

  • ⁠Diode: silicon, reverse saturation current $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10^{-14}}$ A (the small current in the reverse direction), thermal voltage $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{25.85}$ mV (thermal energy per charge) at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{300}$ K (27 °C). The switch model uses the forward voltage $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.7}$ V (voltage across the conducting diode).

  • ⁠pn junction: $10^{16}$ foreign atoms per cm$^3$ on each side, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n_\mathrm i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10^{10}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}}}$ free electrons in pure silicon, relative permittivity $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varepsilon_\mathrm r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{11.7}$.

  • ⁠Circuit: source $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ V, resistor $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}$, diode in series.

  • ⁠Rectifier: sinusoidal voltage with a peak value of 12 V and 50 Hz, load 1 k$\Omega$.

  • ⁠Constants: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.602\cdot10^{-19}}$ C, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{k_\mathrm B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.3806\cdot10^{-23}}$ J/K.
