Stage 6: The operating point

What this stage is about:

For a resistor, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R\,I}$ gives the result at once. The diode follows a different curve. This stage finds the voltage and current of the diode in a circuit.

The idea. Two conditions must hold at the same time. The diode allows only points on its characteristic curve. The rest of the circuit, source and resistor, allows only points on the load line (Chapter 5). The circuit finds the intersection by itself: if $U_\mathrm D$ is too small, the resistor supplies more current than the diode lets through. Charge piles up at the node between the two and raises its potential until both currents are equal.

Definition 6.1 (Operating point)

The operating point (OP) is the point on the characteristic curve fixed by the current through the component and the voltage across it. Operating point analysis means determining it from the given circuit. Operating point synthesis means choosing the components so that a desired OP results.

The linear case. If the diode is replaced by a resistor $R_1$, the component equation, the mesh rule and, from them, the load line hold:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_{R1}}{R_1},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_R+U_{R1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R\,I+U_{R1},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_0-U_{R1}}{R}.}$$
(6.1)

Two linear equations can be solved; with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{250\,\Omega}$ it follows that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_0/(R+R_1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ mA and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_{R1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ V. Solving the system of equations means intersecting the two lines.

Theorem 6.2 (Operating point of the diode)

For the series circuit, both of these hold:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_0-U_\mathrm D}{R}}$$
$$\text{and}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{I_\mathrm S\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm D/U_T}-1\bigr).}$$
(6.2)

The characteristic curve rises everywhere, the line falls everywhere, so there is exactly one intersection.

⁠How to read it: $U_\mathrm D$ is the voltage $U$ from (4.1). You draw the load line through two points, ignoring the diode: no load ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_0}$) and short circuit ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_0/R}$). The characteristic curve comes from the equation or a datasheet. Validity: Components whose characteristic curve runs back or whose state depends on their history (hysteresis) can have several intersections. Several nonlinear components are solved numerically or with equivalent circuits (Stage 7).

(6.2) cannot be solved for $U_\mathrm D$, because $U_\mathrm D$ appears both in the exponent and outside it. You solve graphically or by iteration, that is, by repeated substitution: estimate $U_\mathrm D$, calculate $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(U_0-U_\mathrm D)/R}$, then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_T\ln(I/I_\mathrm S+1)}$, and repeat.

Example

Start with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.7}$ V. Step 1: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.300}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.69245}$ V. Step 2: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.3076}$ mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.69249}$ V. So the OP is (0.692 V | 4.31 mA), and 4.31 V is across the resistor. If $U_0$ is doubled to 10 V, the current almost doubles, to 9.29 mA, and $U_\mathrm D$ rises by only 20 mV to 0.712 V.

⁠Limiting cases: At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.3}$ µ A, and almost the whole source voltage stays across the diode. For negative $U_0$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-I_\mathrm S}$, and the source is entirely across the diode.

QuestionWhy not calculate the other way around: estimate $U_\mathrm D$, get $I$ from the exponential function, then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_0-R\,I}$?

Because this route diverges. An error of 1 mV changes the diode current by 3.9 %, that is, 0.17 mA, and this gives an error of 167 mV across the resistor. In the first method the error shrinks by that same factor each step, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r/R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.006}$ (Stage 8). The flat logarithm damps, the steep exponential function amplifies.

Exercise 7 ★★ Finding and setting the operating point

The diode of the reference model is in series with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{470\,\Omega}$ across $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V.

  1. Determine no load and short circuit of the load line.

  2. Iterate from 0.7 V until three digits no longer change.

  3. Operating point synthesis: which $R$ gives the current 2 mA at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ V? What current flows with the standard value 2.2 k$\Omega$?

  4. The diode is turned around. What do you measure across the diode and the resistor?

Solution

(a) No load $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V, short circuit $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3\,\mathrm{V}/470\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6.38}$ mA. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.7}$ V gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.894}$ mA, which gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6958}$ V, then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.903}$ mA and again $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6958}$ V. OP: (0.696 V | 4.90 mA). (c) At 2 mA, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.673}$ V, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(5\,\mathrm{V}-0.673\,\mathrm{V})/2\,\mathrm{mA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.16\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}$. With 2.2 k$\Omega$, 1.97 mA flow. (d) The diode blocks: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-10^{-14}}$ A, 4.7 pV across the resistor, $-3$ V across the diode.
