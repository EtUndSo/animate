Stage 8: Small changes around the operating point

What this stage is about:

Often a small signal rides on a DC voltage, and only the change counts. This stage shows that for small changes the diode acts like a resistor whose value is set by the direct current.

The idea. Close to the OP, every smooth curve coincides with its tangent (Fig. 10). The mechanism: 1 mV more lowers the barrier by 1 meV. This raises the fraction above the barrier by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1/25.85}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.9}$ %, and always by the same percentage of the present current. So a large current means a large absolute change and thus a small resistance.

Theorem 8.1 (Differential resistance)

At the OP with the direct current $I$, for small changes $u_\mathrm D$ and $i$,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\mathrm{d} U_\mathrm D}{\mathrm{d} I}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_T}{I+I_\mathrm S}}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{U_T}{I},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{r\,i.}$$
(8.1)

⁠How to read it: $r$ is called the differential resistance; at 1 mA it is 25.85 $\Omega$. Lowercase letters are the deviations from the OP, the small signal. Validity: $|u_\mathrm D|\ll U_T$. At 1 mV the error is 2 %, at 5 mV 10 %, at 25.85 mV already 72 %.

Derivation: Slope of the characteristic curve

Differentiating (4.1): $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{d} I/\mathrm{d} U_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(I_\mathrm S/U_T)\,\mathrm{e}^{U_\mathrm D/U_T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(I+I_\mathrm S)/U_T}$. The reciprocal is $r$. The exponential function reproduces itself under differentiation, which is why only $I$ appears in the result, and $I_\mathrm S$ drops out in the forward direction. Two diodes with different $I_\mathrm S$ have the same $r$ at the same current.

Example

In the reference model, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{25.85\,\mathrm{mV}/4.31\,\mathrm{mA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6.0\,\Omega}$. A sine with a peak value of 100 mV is added to the source. For the changes, $R$ and $r$ form a voltage divider:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\hat u_\mathrm D}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100\,\mathrm{mV}\cdot\frac{6.0\,\Omega}{1000\,\mathrm{\Omega}+6.0\,\Omega}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.60\,\mathrm{mV},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\hat\imath}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{100\,\mathrm{mV}}{1006\,\mathrm{\Omega}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{99.4\,\mu\text{A}.}$$

The exact calculation for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.1}$ V gives $+0.59$ mV and $+99.4$ µ A. Almost all of the signal appears across the resistor; 0.6 % remains at the diode.

⁠Limiting cases: For $I\to0$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{U_T/I_\mathrm S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.6\,\mathrm{T\Omega}}$, and the diode isolates. For large $I$, $r$ tends to zero; in reality the bulk resistance remains.

QuestionWhy is the resistance of the diode not $U_\mathrm D/I$?

That would be the reciprocal of the slope of the line from the origin to the OP: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0.692\,\mathrm{V}/4.31\,\mathrm{mA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{161\,\Omega}$. It describes which resistor would carry the same current at the OP, but it is not a property of the characteristic curve at this point. How strongly the current responds to a change is told only by the tangent, and that is 27 times steeper. For an ohmic resistor the two coincide.

Exercise 10 ★★ Slope and tangent

  1. Calculate $r$ at 2 mA and at 20 mA.

  2. At 2 mA the voltage rises by 10 mV. What current does the tangent predict, and what does the characteristic curve give? Why does the tangent lie below?

  3. Three identical diodes in series carry 2 mA from 12 V through a resistor. Calculate the voltage across the three diodes and the resistor. By how much does the voltage across the diodes change when the source rises by 1 V?

Solution

(a) 12.9 $\Omega$ and 1.29 $\Omega$. (b) Tangent: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\,\mathrm{mA}+10\,\mathrm{mV}/12.9\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.77}$ mA. Characteristic curve: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\,\mathrm{mA}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{0.387}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.94}$ mA. An exponential curve bends upward and always lies above its tangent. (c) Voltage $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{3\cdot0.673\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.02}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(12\,\mathrm{V}-2.02\,\mathrm{V})/2\,\mathrm{mA}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}$. The three diodes together have $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{38.8\,\Omega}$. So 1 V becomes $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1\,\mathrm{V}\cdot38.8/5039}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{7.7}$ mV.
