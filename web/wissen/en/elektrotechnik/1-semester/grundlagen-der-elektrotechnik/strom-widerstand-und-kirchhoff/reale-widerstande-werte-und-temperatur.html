Stage 4: Real resistors: values and temperature

What this stage is about:

So far, $R$ was a number from material and shape. A purchased component has a standardized value and a tolerance, and its resistance changes with temperature.

The idea. Manufacturing scatters by a percentage. That is why the values on offer are graded so that successive values have the same percentage spacing. For temperature, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{n\,e\,\mu}$ decides: heat changes either the mobility $\mu$ or the charge carrier density $n$.

Types of construction. Metal-film or carbon-film resistors carry a thin layer on a ceramic body. This is the usual design for 0.1 to 2 W, and the value is printed on them as a color code. Wire-wound resistors withstand high power and temperature and are suited to small values. SMD resistors (Surface Mounted Device) are soldered directly onto the circuit board. They are very small, allow a high packing density, tolerate little power and, because they have no connecting wires, are suited to high frequencies.

Definition 4.1 (E series)

An E series E$N$ contains $N$ values per decade; according to DIN IEC 60063 there are E3, E6, E12, E24, E48, E96 and E192:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{w}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\bigl(\sqrt[N]{10}\,\bigr)^{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt[N]{10^{i}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0,1,\dots,N-1.}$$
(4.1)

⁠How to read it: $w$ is the prefactor; the decade (power of ten) is chosen freely. The step is a fixed factor; for E12, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sqrt[12]{10}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.21}$. The standard values are rounded: 1.0; 1.2; 1.5; 1.8; 2.2; 2.7; 3.3; 3.9; 4.7; 5.6; 6.8; 8.2. Some deviate slightly from (4.1) for historical reasons, such as 2.7 instead of 2.61. Why this factor? E12 belongs to the tolerance $\pm10\,\%$: the ranges of neighboring values then adjoin without gaps, and every manufactured piece belongs to a standard value. Likewise, E6 belongs to $\pm20\,\%$ and E24 to $\pm5\,\%$.

Color code. Leaded resistors usually carry four rings: two digits, the multiplier $10^x$, the tolerance. The digits are black 0, brown 1, red 2, orange 3, yellow 4, green 5, blue 6, violet 7, gray 8, white 9. As multipliers there are also gold $10^{-1}$ and silver $10^{-2}$. The tolerance is brown 1 %, red 2 %, green 0.5 %, blue 0.25 %, violet 0.1 %, gold 5 %, silver 10 %. Yellow, violet, red, gold means $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{47\cdot10^2\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.7\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}$ with $\pm5\,\%$, that is 4.47 to 4.94 k$\Omega$. $R_1$ of the chain carries red, black, red, gold.

Theorem 4.2 (Temperature dependence)

Near a reference temperature $\vartheta_0$,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R(\vartheta)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R(\vartheta_0)\,\bigl(1+\alpha_{\vartheta_0}\,(\vartheta-\vartheta_0)\bigr).}$$
(4.2)

⁠How to read it: $\vartheta$ is the temperature in °C, in data sheets usually $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vartheta_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20}$ °C. The temperature coefficient $\alpha$ has the unit 1/K: copper $3.9\cdot10^{-3}$, tungsten $4.5\cdot10^{-3}$, carbon $-0.5\cdot10^{-3}$, constantan around $10^{-5}$. For $\alpha>0$, the material is called PTC (positive temperature coefficient) or cold conductor: it conducts better when cold. For $\alpha<0$, it is called NTC (negative temperature coefficient) or hot conductor. Validity: (4.2) is the tangent to the true curve. For metals it reaches far, for semiconductors only a few kelvins (Fig. 5 left).

Mechanism. Metals (copper, silver, platinum, tungsten): $n$ is fixed, a warmer lattice vibrates more strongly, the flight time $T$ falls, $\mu$ falls, $R$ rises. Semiconductors (silicon, germanium): at room temperature only a few electrons are free. Heat frees more (Chapter 9), $n$ grows much faster than $\mu$ falls, $R$ falls. Compensated alloys such as constantan: the collisions with the irregularly embedded impurity atoms predominate and hardly depend on temperature. Alternatively, a PTC and an NTC are placed in series so that the increase of one offsets the decrease of the other ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1\alpha_1+R_2\alpha_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$).

Example

The wire warms up from 20 °C to 70 °C. From (4.2) follows the factor $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1+3.9\cdot10^{-3}\cdot50}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.195}$, so 40.6 m$\Omega$ instead of 34 m$\Omega$. An extreme case is the tungsten filament of the lamp: the factor 12 from $1.5\,\Omega$ to $18\,\Omega$ corresponds by (4.2) to around 2460 °C, as a rough approximation over this large span.

⁠Limiting cases: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vartheta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\vartheta_0}$ gives $R(\vartheta_0)$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\alpha}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ gives a constant value.

QuestionWhy do incandescent lamps usually burn out at switch-on?

Cold, the filament has only $1.5\,\Omega$; at 9 V, 6 A flow in the first moment instead of 0.5 A. This surge heats the filament in fractions of a second. Its resistance rises, which lowers the current, until $18\,\Omega$ are reached. At thin spots the heating is greatest, and that is where the filament breaks.

Exercise 4 ★★ Standard values and temperature

  1. A resistor carries blue, gray, red, gold. What are its value and range?

  2. Calculate the step factor of E24. Which tolerance makes neighboring ranges just touch?

  3. A copper coil has exactly $10\,\Omega$ at 20 °C and $12\,\Omega$ when warm. How warm is it?

  4. A copper resistor of $100\,\Omega$ is to be made temperature-independent with carbon in series. What value does the carbon need?

Solution

(a) Blue 6, gray 8, red $10^2$, gold $\pm5\,\%$: $6.8\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$, range 6.46 to 7.14 k$\Omega$.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sqrt[24]{10}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.10}$. From $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(1+t)/(1-t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.10}$ follows $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.8\,\%}$, rounded $\pm5\,\%$.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vartheta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20\text{\,°C}+(12/10-1)/(3.9\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{71}$ °C.

(d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{Cu}\,\alpha_\mathrm{Cu}+R_\mathrm C\,\alpha_\mathrm C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100\,\Omega\cdot3.9/0.5}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{780\,\Omega}$.
