What this is about#

Close a circuit and the lamp lights up at that very moment. Yet the electrons in the wire crawl around 2000 times slower than a snail. A current is moving charge, measured as charge per time. A resistor is a component that slows this motion and converts its energy into heat. The question of this chapter is: How does a voltage become a current, and how does that current divide in a network of many components?

The answer consists of three laws. Ohm's law links voltage and current at a component. The node rule says that no current disappears at a junction. The mesh rule says that the voltages along a closed path add up to zero.
