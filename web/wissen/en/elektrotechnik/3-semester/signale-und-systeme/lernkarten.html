Chapter 1: LTI Systems and Convolution

  1. Termsignal
    A signal is the course of a physical quantity over time, written as a function $s(t)$.
    Stage 1: Describing signals
  2. Termperiod, frequency
    Likewise, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sin(2\pi t/T)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sin(2\pi Ft)}$ is the stretched sine with period $T$ and frequency $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/T}$.
    Stage 1: Describing signals
  3. Fill in the blankfor an oscillation, delaying is the same as rotating the phase, and for $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}$ it is ______ the same as multiplying by the number $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\varphi}$.
    for an oscillation, delaying is the same as rotating the phase, and for $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}$ it is even the same as multiplying by the number $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\varphi}$.
    Stage 1: Describing signals
  4. Termfrequency response
    The number $H(F)$ depends on the frequency and is therefore called the frequency response.
    Stage 2: A first system, the RC circuit
  5. FormulaTheorem 2.1 (Transfer function of the RC low-pass)
    $$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Re}\{H\}&=\frac{1}{1+x^2},\qquad \operatorname{Im}\{H\}=\frac{-x}{1+x^2},\\ |H(F)|&=\sqrt{\operatorname{Re}\{H\}^2+\operatorname{Im}\{H\}^2}=\sqrt{H\,H^*}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}},\qquad \varphi(F)=-\arctan x. \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 2: A first system, the RC circuit
  6. Termsystem
    A system assigns an output signal to an input signal.
    Stage 3: What an LTI system is
  7. Termtransformation
    Such a mapping from functions to functions is called a transformation.
    Stage 3: What an LTI system is
  8. TermLTI system
    A linear, time-invariant system is called an LTI system for short (LTI stands for linear time-invariant).
    Stage 3: What an LTI system is
  9. FormulaDefinition 3.1 (LTI system)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\Bigl\{\sum_ia_is_i(t)\Bigr\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_ia_i\operatorname{Tr}\{s_i(t)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_ia_ig_i(t);}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{s(t-t_0)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g(t-t_0).}$$
    Stage 3: What an LTI system is
  10. TermDirac impulse, impulse response
    The rectangle of area 1 becomes the Dirac impulse $\delta(t)$, an infinitely short impulse of area 1. Its response is called the impulse response $h(t)$.
    Stage 4: The convolution integral
  11. FormulaTheorem 4.1 (Convolution integral)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(\tau)\,\delta(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(\tau)\,h(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.}$$
    Stage 4: The convolution integral
  12. Fill in the blankthe output of an ______ is the superposition of its impulse responses, shifted to every instant of the input and weighted by the input there.
    the output of an LTI system is the superposition of its impulse responses, shifted to every instant of the input and weighted by the input there.
    Stage 4: The convolution integral
  13. Termconvolution product
    The convolution integral is written in short as the convolution product $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)*h(t)}$.
    Stage 6: Rules of convolution
  14. Termideally distortion-free
    A system with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\delta(t)}$ passes every signal on unchanged; it is called ideally distortion-free.
    Stage 6: Rules of convolution
  15. Termdistribution
    $\delta(t)$ is therefore a distribution: an object that is defined only by its effect under an integral, namely by (4.1).
    Stage 7: The Dirac impulse
  16. Termideal delay element
    A system with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\delta(t-t_0)}$ delays every signal by $t_0$; it is called an ideal delay element.
    Stage 7: The Dirac impulse
  17. Termgeneralized differentiation
    And because the step is the running integral of the impulse, functions with jumps may be differentiated: a jump of height $A$ becomes $A\,\delta$. This is called generalized differentiation.
    Stage 7: The Dirac impulse
  18. FormulaTheorem 7.1 (Properties of the Dirac impulse)
    $$\begin{aligned} &\text{Weight:} && [a\,\delta(t)]*s(t)=a\,s(t),\qquad a_1\delta(t)+a_2\delta(t)=(a_1+a_2)\,\delta(t),\\ &\text{Sifting:} && \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(\tau)\,s(-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau=s(0),\qquad s(t)\,\delta(t-T)=s(T)\,\delta(t-T),\\ &\text{Area:} && \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}a\,\delta(t)\,\mathrm{d} t=a,\\ &\text{Scaling:} && \delta(bt)=\frac{1}{|b|}\,\delta(t),\qquad \delta(-t)=\delta(t),\\ &\text{Shifting:} && \delta(t-t_0)*s(t)=s(t-t_0),\\ &\text{Integral:} && \varepsilon(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t\delta(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t}\varepsilon(t)=\delta(t). \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 7: The Dirac impulse
  19. Termintegrator
    A system with the impulse response $\varepsilon(t)$ forms the running integral of its input; it is called an integrator.
    Stage 8: Integrating and differentiating
  20. Termdifferentiator
    An integrator followed by a differentiator leaves every signal unchanged; the cascade is ideally distortion-free.
    Stage 8: Integrating and differentiating
  21. Termcausal
    A system is called causal if the output does not begin before the input.
    Stage 9: Causal and stable
  22. Termstable
    A system is called stable (more precisely, BIBO stable, bounded input, bounded output) if it responds to every bounded input $|s(t)|\le A<\infty$ with a bounded output.
    Stage 9: Causal and stable
  23. FormulaDefinition 9.1 (Causal, stable)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0\quad\text{for }t<0.}$$
    Stage 9: Causal and stable
  24. FormulaTheorem 9.2 (Stability condition)
    $$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|h(\tau)|\,\mathrm{d}\tau<\infty.$$
    Stage 9: Causal and stable

Chapter 2: Laplace Transform

  1. FormulaThe reference model for this chapter
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{RC}\,\mathrm{e}^{-t/RC}\varepsilon(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t),\qquad H(p)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{1+pRC}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{p+1},\quad \operatorname{Re} p>-1,}$$
    Overview
  2. Termgrowth rate
    The real part $\sigma$ (in $1/\mathrm s$) is the growth rate: for $\sigma<0$ the building block decays, for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ it oscillates with constant amplitude, for $\sigma>0$ it grows.
    Stage 1: Why $\mathrm{e}^{pt}$ in particular
  3. Termabsolutely integrable
    An absolutely integrable signal ($\int|s(t)|\,\mathrm{d} t<\infty$, so it decays fast enough on both sides) can be built from them.
    Stage 2: A signal made entirely of building blocks
  4. TermFourier representation
    An absolutely integrable signal ($\int|s(t)|\,\mathrm{d} t<\infty$, so it decays fast enough on both sides) can be built from them. This is the Fourier representation.
    Stage 2: A signal made entirely of building blocks
  5. Termspectrum
    The function $\mathcal{F}\{s\}(f)$ is called the spectrum; it gives the weight of the oscillation with frequency $f$.
    Stage 2: A signal made entirely of building blocks
  6. Termdensity
    $S(p)$ itself is a density: weight per frequency interval.
    The trick: damp, decompose, multiply back
  7. FormulaTheorem 2.1 (Inversion formula of the Laplace transform)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{j}}\int_{\sigma-\mathrm{j}\infty}^{\sigma+\mathrm{j}\infty}S(p)\,\mathrm{e}^{pt}\,\mathrm{d} p }\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}S(\sigma+\mathrm{j}2\pi f)\,\mathrm{e}^{(\sigma+\mathrm{j}2\pi f)t}\,\mathrm{d} f.}$$
    The trick: damp, decompose, multiply back
  8. FormulaThe basic transform pairs (used throughout the chapter)
    $$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{e}^{p_0t}\,\varepsilon(t)\quad&\longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{p-p_0},&&\operatorname{Re} p>\operatorname{Re} p_0,\\ -\mathrm{e}^{p_0t}\,\varepsilon(-t)\quad&\longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{p-p_0},&&\operatorname{Re} p<\operatorname{Re} p_0,\\ \delta(t)\quad&\longleftrightarrow\quad1,&&\text{all }p,\\ t\,\varepsilon(t)\quad&\longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{p^2},&&\operatorname{Re} p>0. \end{aligned}$$
    Region of convergence: which damping rates work
  9. Fill in the blankThe region of convergence is the set of all damping rates $\sigma$ for which $s(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma t}$ becomes ______.
    The region of convergence is the set of all damping rates $\sigma$ for which $s(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma t}$ becomes absolutely integrable.
    Region of convergence: which damping rates work
  10. FormulaTheorem 3.1 (Convolution theorem)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)*h(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G(p)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S(p)\,H(p),}$$
    Stage 3: Convolution becomes multiplication
  11. Fill in the blankConvolution becomes multiplication because the ______ only multiplies each building block $\mathrm{e}^{pt}$ by the number $H(p)$.
    Convolution becomes multiplication because the system only multiplies each building block $\mathrm{e}^{pt}$ by the number $H(p)$.
    Stage 3: Convolution becomes multiplication
  12. Termstability
    A conjugate pair of poles $p_r,p_r^*$ together gives a real damped oscillation. This is also the core of stability: a causal building block $\mathrm{e}^{p_r t}\varepsilon(t)$ decays if and only if $\operatorname{Re} p_r<0$.
    Stage 4: The integral becomes a sum
  13. Termnatural response
    The pole of the system gives the natural response of the RC circuit, the decay with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{RC}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s. This holds in general: the output $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{SH}$ contains the poles of the input and the poles of the system.
    Stage 4: The integral becomes a sum
  14. Fill in the blankA right-sided ______ with a rational Laplace transform is a finite sum of building blocks $\mathrm{e}^{p_r t}$. The poles are the exponents, and the partial fraction coefficients are the weights.
    A right-sided signal with a rational Laplace transform is a finite sum of building blocks $\mathrm{e}^{p_r t}$. The poles are the exponents, and the partial fraction coefficients are the weights.
    Stage 4: The integral becomes a sum

Chapter 3: Fourier Series and Transform

  1. Fill in the blankThe ______ is the number by which an LTI system multiplies a pure oscillation.
    The frequency response is the number by which an LTI system multiplies a pure oscillation.
    Stage 1: Oscillations as eigenfunctions
  2. TermDC component
    $S_p(0)$ is the DC component, the mean value of the signal.
    Stage 2: Periodic signals, the Fourier series
  3. Termorthogonal
    Oscillations of different frequencies are therefore called orthogonal.
    Stage 2: Periodic signals, the Fourier series
  4. FormulaTheorem 2.1 (Fourier series)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1T\int_{t_1}^{t_1+T}s_p(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi kFt}\,\mathrm{d} t,}$$
    Stage 2: Periodic signals, the Fourier series
  5. Termpower
    The average power of the signal is the sum of the powers of the individual harmonics.
    Stage 3: Power, convergence and LTI systems
  6. Termpartial sum
    If you take only the harmonics up to $|k|\le N$, the partial sum is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_{p,N}(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{k=-N}^{N}S_p(k)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi kFt}}$.
    Stage 3: Power, convergence and LTI systems
  7. FormulaTheorem 3.1 (Parseval for Fourier series)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1T\int_0^T|s_p(t)|^2\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}|S_p(k)|^2.}$$
    Stage 3: Power, convergence and LTI systems
  8. Termspectral density
    $S(f)$ is a spectral density: weight per frequency interval.
    Stage 4: From periodic to non-repeating signals
  9. FormulaTheorem 4.1 (Fourier transform)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi ft}\,\mathrm{d} t,\qquad s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}S(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi ft}\,\mathrm{d} f.}$$
    Stage 4: From periodic to non-repeating signals
  10. Termeven, odd
    A complex function is called even if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^*(-t)}$, and odd if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-x^*(-t)}$.
    Stage 5: Symmetries in the signal and in the spectrum
  11. FormulaTheorem 5.1 (Symmetries)
    $$s_g(t)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\operatorname{Re}\{S(f)\},\qquad s_u(t)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\mathrm{j}\operatorname{Im}\{S(f)\}.$$
    Stage 5: Symmetries in the signal and in the spectrum
  12. Fill in the blankFor a real ______, the negative half of the spectrum is the conjugate mirror image of the positive half. It contains no new information.
    For a real signal, the negative half of the spectrum is the conjugate mirror image of the positive half. It contains no new information.
    Stage 5: Symmetries in the signal and in the spectrum
  13. Termenergy density
    $|S(f)|^2$ is the energy density over frequency.
    Stage 6: The rules of calculation (theorems)
  14. FormulaTheorem 6.1 (Theorems of the Fourier transform)
    $$\begin{aligned} &\text{Superposition:} && \textstyle\sum_ia_is_i(t)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\sum_ia_iS_i(f)\\ &\text{Conjugation:} && s^*(t)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S^*(-f)\\ &\text{Similarity (scaling):} && s(bt)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\frac{1}{|b|}S\Bigl(\frac fb\Bigr),\quad b\neq0;\qquad s(-t)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S(-f)\\ &\text{Shift:} && s(t-t_0)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi ft_0},\qquad\delta(t-t_0)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi ft_0}\\ &\text{Derivative:} && \frac{\mathrm{d}^n}{\mathrm{d} t^n}s(t)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;(\mathrm{j}2\pi f)^nS(f)\\ &\text{Symmetry (duality):} && S(t)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;s(-f)\\ &\text{Modulation:} && \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}s(t)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S(f-F)\\ &\text{Frequency-domain derivative:} && -\mathrm{j}2\pi t\,s(t)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} f}S(f)\\ &\text{Convolution, multiplication:} && s_1*s_2\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S_1S_2,\qquad s_1s_2\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S_1*S_2\\ &\text{Areas:} && \int s(t)\mathrm{d} t=S(0),\qquad\int S(f)\mathrm{d} f=s(0)\\ &\text{Parseval:} && \int|s(t)|^2\mathrm{d} t=\int|S(f)|^2\mathrm{d} f \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 6: The rules of calculation (theorems)
  15. FormulaTheorem 7.1 (Rectangle, si and triangle)
    $$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{rect}\Bigl(\frac tT\Bigr)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;|T|\operatorname{si}(\pi Tf),\\ \operatorname{si}\Bigl(\frac{\pi t}{T}\Bigr)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;|T|\operatorname{rect}(Tf),\\ \Lambda(t)=\operatorname{rect}(t)*\operatorname{rect}(t)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\operatorname{si}^2(\pi f),\\ \operatorname{si}(\pi t)*\operatorname{si}(\pi t)&=\operatorname{si}(\pi t). \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 7: Rectangle, si function and triangle
  16. Termline spectrum
    A periodic signal has a line spectrum: Dirac impulses at the harmonics, with the Fourier coefficients as weights.
    Stage 8: Singular signals and line spectra
  17. TermDirac comb
    The Dirac comb (impulses spaced $T$ apart) again has a Dirac comb as its spectrum, with the spacing $1/T$ and the weight $1/|T|$ (Fig. 7).
    Stage 8: Singular signals and line spectra
  18. FormulaTheorem 8.1 (Singular pairs)
    $$\begin{aligned} \delta(t)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;1, & c&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;c\,\delta(f),\\ \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\delta(f-F), & \cos(2\pi Ft)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\tfrac12\delta(f+F)+\tfrac12\delta(f-F),\\ & & \sin(2\pi Ft)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\tfrac{\mathrm{j}}{2}\delta(f+F)-\tfrac{\mathrm{j}}{2}\delta(f-F),\\ \textstyle\sum_kS_p(k)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi kFt}&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\textstyle\sum_kS_p(k)\,\delta(f-kF),\\ \textstyle\sum_n\delta(t-nT)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\frac{1}{|T|}\textstyle\sum_k\delta\Bigl(f-\frac kT\Bigr),\\ \varepsilon(t)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\tfrac12\delta(f)+\frac{1}{\mathrm{j}2\pi f}, & \operatorname{sgn}(t)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\frac{1}{\mathrm{j}\pi f},\\ \int_{-\infty}^ts(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\frac{S(f)}{\mathrm{j}2\pi f}+\tfrac12S(0)\,\delta(f). \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 8: Singular signals and line spectra
  19. TermHilbert transform
    Convolution with $\frac{1}{\pi f}$ is called the Hilbert transform.
    Stage 9: Hilbert transform and analytic signal
  20. Termanalytic component
    $s_+$ is called the analytic component, $\hat s$ the Hilbert transform of $s$.
    Stage 9: Hilbert transform and analytic signal
  21. Termall-pass
    The LTI system with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\pi t}}$ has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\mathrm{j}\operatorname{sgn}(f)}$: magnitude 1 at all frequencies (an all-pass), phase $-90^\circ$ for $f>0$ and $+90^\circ$ for $f<0$.
    Stage 9: Hilbert transform and analytic signal
  22. FormulaTheorem 9.1 (Hilbert relation for causal signals)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Re}\{S(f)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Im}\{S(f)\}*\frac{1}{\pi f},\qquad\operatorname{Im}\{S(f)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\operatorname{Re}\{S(f)\}*\frac{1}{\pi f}.}$$
    Stage 9: Hilbert transform and analytic signal
  23. Fill in the blankThe ______ shifts the phase of every oscillation by $90^\circ$ without changing its amplitude.
    The Hilbert transformer shifts the phase of every oscillation by $90^\circ$ without changing its amplitude.
    Stage 9: Hilbert transform and analytic signal
  24. Termspectrogram
    Plotting $|S_T|$ over $t$ and $f$ gives a spectrogram.
    Stage 10: Short-time Fourier transform
  25. FormulaTheorem 10.1 (Short-time Fourier transform)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_T(f,t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(\tau)\,w(\tau-t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1c\sum_ns(\tau)\,w(\tau-nT),\qquad S(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1c\sum_nS_T(f,nT).}$$
    Stage 10: Short-time Fourier transform

Chapter 4: Sampling and Discrete-Time Systems

  1. Termsamples
    The signal is therefore measured at fixed intervals $T$, and one works with the samples $s(nT)$.
    Overview
  2. Termanalog
    An analog signal is continuous in both.
    Stage 1: Signal classes and ideal sampling
  3. Termdiscrete-time
    A discrete-time signal has values only at the times $nT$, but these with arbitrary precision.
    Stage 1: Signal classes and ideal sampling
  4. Termdigital
    A digital signal is in addition stepped in amplitude (quantized), because a computer can represent only finitely many numbers.
    Stage 1: Signal classes and ideal sampling
  5. Termideal sampler
    Mathematically, the rectangle is replaced by a Dirac impulse. This is the ideal sampler.
    Stage 1: Signal classes and ideal sampling
  6. Termspectral images
    The copies are called spectral images.
    Stage 2: The spectrum of the sampled signal
  7. FormulaTheorem 2.1 (Spectrum of the ideally sampled signal)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_\mathrm a(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1T\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}S\Bigl(f-\frac kT\Bigr)\qquad(T>0).}$$
    Stage 2: The spectrum of the sampled signal
  8. TermNyquist rate
    The minimum rate $2f_\mathrm g$ is called the Nyquist rate.
    Stage 3: The sampling theorem and reconstruction
  9. Termalias
    A frequency above $f_\mathrm A/2$ appears after reconstruction as a false, lower frequency, its alias.
    Stage 3: The sampling theorem and reconstruction
  10. Termanti-aliasing filter
    Afterwards the two can no longer be separated. This is why the signal is filtered with a low-pass filter before every sampler (anti-aliasing filter).
    Stage 3: The sampling theorem and reconstruction
  11. FormulaTheorem 3.1 (Sampling theorem)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T\le\frac{1}{2f_\mathrm g}\qquad\text{that is}\qquad f_\mathrm A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1T\ge2f_\mathrm g.}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_\mathrm a(t)*\bigl[2f_\mathrm gT\operatorname{si}(2\pi f_\mathrm gt)\bigr],}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(nT)\,\operatorname{si}\Bigl(\pi\frac{t-nT}{T}\Bigr).}$$
    Stage 3: The sampling theorem and reconstruction
  12. Termdiscrete convolution
    Normalized to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, this is the discrete convolution.
    Stage 5: Discrete-time signals and discrete convolution
  13. Termunit impulse
    The unit impulse $\delta(n)$ is an ordinary sequence, not a distribution: it has the value 1 at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.
    Stage 5: Discrete-time signals and discrete convolution
  14. Termshift-invariant
    A discrete-time system is called linear if the superposition principle holds, and shift-invariant if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{s(n-m)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g(n-m)}$ for every integer $m$.
    Stage 5: Discrete-time signals and discrete convolution
  15. TermLSI system
    A system with both properties is called an LSI system.
    Stage 5: Discrete-time signals and discrete convolution
  16. Termdifference
    The counterpart of the derivative is the difference $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(n)-s(n-1)}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\delta(n)-\delta(n-1)}$.
    Stage 5: Discrete-time signals and discrete convolution
  17. Termaccumulation
    The counterpart of the integral is the accumulation $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{m\le n}s(m)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(n)*\varepsilon(n)}$.
    Stage 5: Discrete-time signals and discrete convolution
  18. TermIIR filter
    With feedback ($b_p\neq0$) the impulse response can be infinitely long: an IIR filter (infinite impulse response).
    Stage 5: Discrete-time signals and discrete convolution
  19. Termperiodic convolution
    The product of two sequences gives the periodic convolution of the spectra over one period.
    Stage 7: The discrete-time Fourier transform
  20. FormulaTheorem 7.1 (Discrete-time Fourier transform)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_\mathrm a(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi nf},\qquad s(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}S_\mathrm a(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi nf}\,\mathrm{d} f.}$$
    Stage 7: The discrete-time Fourier transform
  21. FormulaTheorem 8.1 (Discrete Fourier transform)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_d(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}s_d(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi kn/M},\qquad s_d(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1M\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}S_d(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi kn/M},\qquad k,n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0,\dots,M-1.}$$
    Stage 8: The discrete Fourier transform (DFT)
  22. Termfast Fourier transform
    For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1024}$ that is 10,240 instead of 1,048,576 multiplications, roughly a hundred times fewer. This is the fast Fourier transform (FFT).
    Stage 9: FFT and fast convolution
  23. Termunit circle
    On the unit circle $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|z|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, $S(z)$ is the ordinary spectrum $S_\mathrm a(f)$, provided the unit circle lies in the region of convergence.
    Stage 11: The z-transform
  24. FormulaTheorem 11.1 (z-transform)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S(z)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(n)\,z^{-n},\qquad s(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{j}}\oint S(z)\,z^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d} z,}$$
    Stage 11: The z-transform

Chapter 5: Lowpass and Bandpass Systems

  1. Termphase delay
    the phase delay is the delay of a single oscillation of frequency $f$.
    Stage 1: The distortionless system
  2. Termgroup delay
    The group delay is the delay of a group of neighboring frequencies, that is, of an envelope (Stage 11).
    Stage 1: The distortionless system
  3. FormulaDefinition 1.1 (Characteristic quantities of an LTI system)
    $$\begin{aligned} &\text{Attenuation:} && a(f)=-20\lg|H(f)|\ \text{dB}\quad\text{or}\quad a(f)=-\ln|H(f)|\ \text{Np},\\ &\text{Phase attenuation angle:} && b(f)=-\varphi(f),\\ &\text{Phase delay:} && t_\mathrm p(f)=-\frac{\varphi(f)}{2\pi f},\\ &\text{Group delay:} && t_\mathrm g(f)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\varphi(f)}{\mathrm{d} f}. \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 1: The distortionless system
  4. Termsignal duration
    The signal duration $t_m$ of an impulse response is taken to be the width of a rectangle with the same height $h_{\max}$ and the same area.
    Stage 2: The ideal lowpass
  5. FormulaTheorem 2.1 (Ideal lowpass)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}\Bigl(\frac{f}{2f_\mathrm g}\Bigr)\qquad\bullet\!\!-\!\!\circ\qquad h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2f_\mathrm g\operatorname{si}(2\pi f_\mathrm gt).}$$
    Stage 2: The ideal lowpass
  6. Termecho
    Each sample $h(nT)$ is an echo: an si function shifted by $nT$.
    Stage 5: Echo method and pulse-shaping filters
  7. Termequivalent lowpass
    The result is the equivalent lowpass $H_T(f)$.
    Stage 7: The ideal bandpass and its equivalent lowpass
  8. Termsymmetric
    If there is an $f_0$ for which $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_T(-f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H_T^*(f)}$ holds (so that $h_T$ is real), the bandpass is called symmetric.
    Stage 7: The ideal bandpass and its equivalent lowpass
  9. Termcomplex envelope, complex carrier
    $s_T(t)$ is called the complex envelope, $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t}$ the complex carrier.
    Stage 8: Complex envelope and quadrature components
  10. Termquadrature components
    $s_{Tr}$ and $s_{Ti}$ are called quadrature components (the in-phase and the quadrature component): one modulates the cosine of the carrier, the other its sine.
    Stage 8: Complex envelope and quadrature components
  11. Termenvelope
    $|s_T|$ is the envelope, which you see on an oscilloscope as the “outline” of the signal, and $\varphi_T$ is the phase deviation of the carrier.
    Stage 8: Complex envelope and quadrature components
  12. FormulaTheorem 8.1 (Quadrature representation)
    $$\begin{aligned} s(t)&=s_{Tr}(t)\cos(2\pi f_0t)-s_{Ti}(t)\sin(2\pi f_0t),\\ s_{Tr}(t)&=s(t)\cos(2\pi f_0t)+\hat s(t)\sin(2\pi f_0t),\qquad s_{Ti}(t)=\hat s(t)\cos(2\pi f_0t)-s(t)\sin(2\pi f_0t),\\ s(t)&=|s_T(t)|\cos\bigl(2\pi f_0t+\varphi_T(t)\bigr),\qquad|s_T|=\sqrt{s_{Tr}^2+s_{Ti}^2}. \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 8: Complex envelope and quadrature components
  13. FormulaTheorem 9.1 (Transmission in the equivalent lowpass domain)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G_T(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac12S_T(f)\,H_T(f)\qquad\bullet\!\!-\!\!\circ\qquad g_T(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac12\bigl[s_T(t)*h_T(t)\bigr],\qquad g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}\bigl\{g_T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t}\bigr\}.}$$
    Stage 9: Bandpass signal through a bandpass system
  14. FormulaTheorem 11.1 (Delays in a bandpass)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g_T(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_T(t-t_\mathrm g)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t_\mathrm p},\qquad g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}\bigl\{s_T(t-t_\mathrm g)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0(t-t_\mathrm p)}\bigr\}.}$$
    Stage 11: Phase and group delay in a bandpass
  15. Termhighpass
    A highpass is a lowpass shifted to half the sampling rate, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12}$.
    Stage 12: Discrete-time bandpass and highpass filters
  16. Fill in the blanka ______ highpass is obtained from a lowpass by flipping every other sign.
    a digital highpass is obtained from a lowpass by flipping every other sign.
    Stage 12: Discrete-time bandpass and highpass filters

Chapter 6: Correlation Functions

  1. Termsignal energy
    Without the factor $1/R$, the expression is called the signal energy.
    Stage 1: Energy and power
  2. Termautocorrelation function, cross-correlation function
    For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s}$ it is called the autocorrelation function (ACF) $\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)$, otherwise the cross-correlation function (CCF).
    Stage 3: Correlation function and correlation product
  3. Termcorrelation product
    The second equals sign is the heart of the matter: correlation is convolution with the flipped, conjugated signal. That is why it is also called the correlation product.
    Stage 3: Correlation function and correlation product
  4. FormulaDefinition 3.1 (Correlation function for energy signals)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sg}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s^*(t)\,g(t+\tau)\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s^*(-\tau)*g(\tau).}$$
    Stage 3: Correlation function and correlation product
  5. FormulaTheorem 3.2 (Properties of correlation functions)
    $$\begin{aligned} &\varphi^E_{gs}(\tau)=\varphi^{E*}_{sg}(-\tau),\qquad\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)=\varphi^{E*}_{ss}(-\tau)\ \ (\text{even for real }s),\\ &|\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)|\le\varphi^E_{ss}(0)=E_s. \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 3: Correlation function and correlation product
  6. Termenergy spectral density
    $|S(f)|^2$ is called the energy spectral density.
    Stage 4: Energy spectral density
  7. Termcross energy spectral density
    $S^*G$ is called the cross energy spectral density.
    Stage 4: Energy spectral density
  8. FormulaTheorem 4.1 (Wiener–Khinchin for energy signals)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s^*(-\tau)*s(\tau)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S^*(f)\,S(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|S(f)|^2,\qquad\varphi^E_{sg}(\tau)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S^*(f)\,G(f).}$$
    Stage 4: Energy spectral density
  9. FormulaTheorem 5.1 (Wiener–Lee)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{gg}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)*\varphi^E_{hh}(\tau)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;|G(f)|^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|S(f)|^2\,|H(f)|^2,}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sg}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)*h(\tau)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;|S(f)|^2\,H(f).}$$
    Stage 5: Correlation and LTI systems
  10. Termfast correlation
    Computed with the FFT, this is called fast correlation.
    Stage 7: Discrete-time and periodic correlation
  11. FormulaTheorem 7.1 (Discrete-time correlation)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sg}(m)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s^*(n)\,g(n+m)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s^*(-m)*g(m),\qquad E_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_n|s(n)|^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}|S_\mathrm a(f)|^2\,\mathrm{d} f,}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{ss}(m)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;|S_\mathrm a(f)|^2\ (\text{period }1),\qquad\varphi^E_{gg}(m)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^E_{ss}(m)*\varphi^E_{hh}(m).}$$
    Stage 7: Discrete-time and periodic correlation

Chapter 7: Random Signals

  1. Termsample function
    Each one is called a sample function or realization.
    Stage 1: The random process
  2. Termrandom process
    The imagined totality of all of them is called a random process $s(t)$.
    Stage 1: The random process
  3. Termrandom variable
    The values of all sample functions at a fixed instant $t_1$ form a random variable $s(t_1)$.
    Stage 1: The random process
  4. Termexpected value
    $\mathrm E\{\cdot\}$ is called the expected value or ensemble average: you average over all sample functions at the same instant, that is, vertically in Fig. 1.
    Stage 1: The random process
  5. FormulaDefinition 1.1 (Ensemble averages)
    $$\begin{aligned} \mathrm E\{s(t_1)\}&=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac1M\sum_{k=1}^M{}^ks(t_1),\qquad \mathrm E\{F[s(t_1)]\}=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac1M\sum_{k=1}^MF\bigl[{}^ks(t_1)\bigr],\\ \varphi_{ss}(t_1,t_2)&=\mathrm E\{s(t_1)\,s(t_2)\}. \end{aligned}$$
    Stage 1: The random process
  6. Termstationary
    A process is called stationary if none of its averages change when all observation times are shifted by the same time $t_0$.
    Stage 2: Stationarity and ergodicity
  7. Termergodic
    A stationary process is called ergodic if, in addition, every time average of a sample function equals the corresponding ensemble average.
    Stage 2: Stationarity and ergodicity
  8. Termvariance, AC power
    The variance $\sigma_s^2$ is the power minus the power of the DC component, that is, the AC power.
    Stage 3: Mean value, power, variance
  9. FormulaTheorem 4.1 (Properties of the ACF of stationary processes)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{ss}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L_s,\qquad\varphi_{ss}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{ss}(-\tau),\qquad|\varphi_{ss}(\tau)|\le\varphi_{ss}(0).}$$
    Stage 4: Autocorrelation, covariance and cross-correlation
  10. Fill in the blank______ add only if the signals are uncorrelated. Otherwise the cross term is added. Two identical signals give four times the power, two opposite ones give zero power.
    Powers add only if the signals are uncorrelated. Otherwise the cross term is added. Two identical signals give four times the power, two opposite ones give zero power.
    Stage 4: Autocorrelation, covariance and cross-correlation
  11. Termcumulative distribution function
    The cumulative distribution function $P_s(x)$ is the fraction of the ensemble (for ergodic processes: the fraction of time) in which the signal lies below the threshold $x$.
    Stage 5: Distribution function and probability density
  12. Termprobability density function
    The probability density function $p_s(x)$ is never negative, has the area 1, and $p_s(x)\,\mathrm{d} x$ is the probability that the signal lies between $x$ and $x+\mathrm{d} x$.
    Stage 5: Distribution function and probability density
  13. Termquantizer
    A quantizer replaces every value by the nearest of $M$ levels.
    Stage 5: Distribution function and probability density
  14. FormulaDefinition 5.1 (Distribution)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_s(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{Prob}[s(t)\le x],\qquad p_s(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\mathrm{d} P_s(x)}{\mathrm{d} x},\qquad P_s(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^xp_s(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.}$$
    Stage 5: Distribution function and probability density
  15. Termjoint distribution
    The joint distribution of two variables is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_{sg}(x,y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{Prob}[s\le x\ \text{and}\ g\le y]}$, its density is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p_{sg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\partial^2P_{sg}}{\partial x\,\partial y}}$.
    Stage 6: Joint distribution, independence, uncorrelatedness
  16. Termstatistically independent
    $s$ and $g$ are called statistically independent if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p_{sg}(x,y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{p_s(x)\,p_g(y)}$.
    Stage 6: Joint distribution, independence, uncorrelatedness
  17. Termuncorrelated
    $s$ and $g$ are called statistically independent if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p_{sg}(x,y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{p_s(x)\,p_g(y)}$. They are called uncorrelated if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mu_{sg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{sg}-m_sm_g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.
    Stage 6: Joint distribution, independence, uncorrelatedness
  18. FormulaTheorem 7.1 (Gaussian distribution)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p_s(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_s^2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-(x-m_s)^2/(2\sigma_s^2)},\qquad P_s(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12+\frac12\operatorname{erf}\Bigl(\frac{x-m_s}{\sqrt{2\sigma_s^2}}\Bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\operatorname{erfc}\Bigl(\frac{m_s-x}{\sqrt{2\sigma_s^2}}\Bigr)}$$
    Stage 7: The Gaussian distribution
  19. FormulaTheorem 8.1 (Wiener–Lee for stationary processes)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{gg}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{ss}(\tau)*\varphi^E_{hh}(\tau),\qquad\Phi_{gg}(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Phi_{ss}(f)\,|H(f)|^2,}$$
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{ss}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi_{ss}(f)\,\mathrm{d} f.}$$
    Stage 8: Random signals in LTI systems, power spectrum
  20. Termwhite noise
    A zero-mean process with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Phi_{ss}(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{N_0}$ for all $f$ is called white noise.
    Stage 9: White noise and thermal noise
  21. Termmatched filter
    The best impulse response is the signal itself, time-reversed and shifted by $T$: the matched filter (correlation filter).
    Stage 10: The matched filter (correlation filter)
  22. Termcorrelator
    A correlator is equivalent: it multiplies the received signal by $s(t)$ and integrates over the duration of the signal.
    Stage 10: The matched filter (correlation filter)
  23. FormulaTheorem 10.1 (Matched filter)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Bigl(\frac{S_a}{N}\Bigr)_{\max}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{E_s}{N_0},\qquad\text{achieved with}\qquad h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\,s(T-t),\quad H(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\,S^*(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi fT}.}$$
    Stage 10: The matched filter (correlation filter)
  24. TermDiscrete-time white noise
    Discrete-time white noise has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Phi_{ss,\mathrm a}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{N}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{ss}(m)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{N\delta(m)}$: successive numbers are uncorrelated.
    Stage 12: Discrete-time random signals

  2. FormulaTheorem 1.1 (Orthogonal expansion)
    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{b_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_0^Tf(t)\,s_i(t)\,\mathrm{d} t.}$$

  3. Termper-unit-length parameters
