What this stage is about:

A periodic signal repeats itself after $T$. It can be built from oscillations, but only from very specific ones. This stage shows which ones, and how to find their weights.

The idea. An oscillation $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi ft}$ repeats itself after $T$ if and only if $fT$ is an integer. So a signal with the period $T$ contains only the frequencies $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_k}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{kF}$ with the fundamental frequency $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/T}$, the harmonics:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_p(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}S_p(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi kFt}.}$$
(2.1)

This is the Fourier series (synthesis). $S_p(k)$ is the complex weight of the $k$th harmonic.

Real signals. For a real signal, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_p^*}$. Conjugating (2.1) turns $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi kFt}$ into the oscillation with $-k$. Comparing the coefficients gives

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(-k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_p^*(k).}$$

Each pair $k,-k$ then gives $2\operatorname{Re}\{S_p(k)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi kFt}\}$, a real cosine oscillation. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|S_p(k)|\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\varphi_p(k)}}$:

$$\begin{aligned} s_p(t)&=S_p(0)+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|S_p(k)|\cos\bigl(2\pi kFt+\varphi_p(k)\bigr)\\ &=S_p(0)+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Bigl[\operatorname{Re}\{S_p(k)\}\cos(2\pi kFt)-\operatorname{Im}\{S_p(k)\}\sin(2\pi kFt)\Bigr]. \end{aligned}$$
(2.2)(2.3)

The phase $\varphi_p(k)$ is found as for any complex number, with $\arctan$ and the quadrant correction ($+\pi$ for a negative real part). $S_p(0)$ is the DC component, the mean value of the signal.

Finding the weights: orthogonality. How do you extract a single $S_p(n)$ from the sum? Multiply by $\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi nFt}$ and average over one period. In the process, all other harmonics vanish:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac1T\int_0^T\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi(k-n)Ft}\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{cases}1&k=n\\0&k\neq n\end{cases}.}$$
(2.4)

For $k\neq n$, the phasor $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi(k-n)Ft}$ makes exactly $|k-n|$ full turns during the period, and its mean value is zero. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{k}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{n}$ it stands still. Oscillations of different frequencies are therefore called orthogonal. What remains is:

Theorem 2.1 (Fourier series)

A signal with the period $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/F}$ and finite power (the mean of $|s(t)|^2$ over one period is finite) has the representation (2.1) with

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1T\int_{t_1}^{t_1+T}s_p(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi kFt}\,\mathrm{d} t,}$$
(2.5)

where $t_1$ is arbitrary (analysis). The equals sign in (2.1) holds in the mean-square sense: the mean of $\bigl|s_p(t)-\sum_{|k|\le N}S_p(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi kFt}\bigr|^2$ over one period, the power of the error, tends to zero as $N\to\infty$. At individual points, for example at jumps, the series need not give the value of the signal.

⁠How to read it: $S_p(k)$ is the mean value of the signal after it has been “counter-rotated” with the $k$th harmonic. If the harmonic is contained in the signal, the phasor no longer rotates at this point, and its share remains after averaging. $S_p(0)$ is simply the mean value.

Derivation: Analysis formula

Multiply (2.1) by $\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi nFt}$, integrate over one period, interchange sum and integral:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_0^Ts_p(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi nFt}\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_kS_p(k)\int_0^T\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi(k-n)Ft}\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_p(n)\,T.}$$

For $k\neq n$ the integral is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi(k-n)FT}-1}{\mathrm{j}2\pi(k-n)F}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi(k-n)}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. Since the integrand has the period $T$, you may integrate over any interval of length $T$.

Examplea finite series

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_p(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+2\cos(2\pi Ft)+\sin(4\pi Ft)}$. With Euler's formulas you can read off the coefficients without integrating:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\cos(2\pi Ft)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sin(4\pi Ft)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{2\mathrm{j}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}4\pi Ft}-\frac{1}{2\mathrm{j}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}4\pi Ft},}$$

so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(\pm1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\tfrac{\mathrm{j}}{2}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(-2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac{\mathrm{j}}{2}}$, all others zero. Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(-2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_p^*(2)}$.

Examplethe square wave

For the reference model (1 for $|t|\le T_1/2$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T/2}$):

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{T_1}{T},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1T\int_{-T_1/2}^{T_1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi kFt}\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\sin(\pi kT_1/T)}{\pi k}\quad(k\neq0).}$$
(2.6)

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T/2}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(\pm1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/\pi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.318}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(\pm2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(\pm3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.106}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(\pm5)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.064}$. The even harmonics are missing because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sin(\pi k/2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ for even $k$. All coefficients are real because the signal is even, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(-t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)}$: the imaginary part of $S_p(k)$ is $-\frac1T\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}s(t)\sin(2\pi kFt)\,\mathrm{d} t$ and vanishes because the integrand is odd: it changes sign under $t\to-t$, so the contributions left and right of zero cancel. Figure 1 shows them for two duty cycles: the narrower the pulse, the more slowly the coefficients decay.

QuestionWhy are there no frequencies between the harmonics in a periodic signal?

Because every oscillation in the sum must itself have the period $T$; otherwise the sum would not be periodic. An oscillation with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.5F}$ has made only one and a half turns after $T$ and is in a different position than at the start. No weight, however cleverly chosen, can compensate for this, because different frequencies are orthogonal and cannot cancel each other.

Exercise 1 ★ Reading off coefficients

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_p(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12+\cos\bigl(2\pi Ft+\frac\pi3\bigr)+\frac12\sin(6\pi Ft)}$.

  1. Find all $S_p(k)$ using Euler's formulas. Check $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(-k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_p^*(k)}$.

  2. Give $|S_p(1)|$ and $\varphi_p(1)$ and compare with (2.2).

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\cos(x+\frac\pi3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi/3}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j} x}+\frac12\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi/3}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j} x}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac12\sin(3x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{4\mathrm{j}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}3x}-\frac{1}{4\mathrm{j}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}3x}}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\pi Ft}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac12,\quad S_p(\pm1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac12\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{j}\pi/3}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.25\pm0.433\mathrm{j},\quad S_p(3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\tfrac{\mathrm{j}}{4},\quad S_p(-3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac{\mathrm{j}}{4},}$$

all others zero. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(-1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_p^*(1)}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(-3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_p^*(3)}$ hold.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|S_p(1)|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_p(1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{60^\circ}$. In (2.2): $2\cdot\frac12\cos(2\pi Ft+60^\circ)$, exactly the cosine term of the signal. (Power by Parseval: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac14+2\cdot\frac14+2\cdot\frac1{16}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.875}$.)

Exercise 2 ★★ The narrow square wave

Square wave with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T/4}$.

  1. Compute $S_p(0)$, $S_p(1)$, $S_p(2)$ and $S_p(4)$ from (2.6).

  2. Why is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(4)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$? Interpret it using orthogonality: how many periods of the fourth harmonic fit into the pulse?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac14}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\sin(\pi/4)}{\pi}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.225}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\sin(\pi/2)}{2\pi}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.159}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(4)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\sin\pi}{4\pi}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

(b) The fourth harmonic has the period $T/4$, exactly the pulse width. The analysis formula integrates $\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi4Ft}$ over the pulse, that is, over exactly one full turn of the phasor. The mean value is zero. In general, $S_p(k)$ vanishes when an integer number of periods of the $k$th harmonic fits into the pulse.

Summary

Terms
  • fundamental frequency, harmonics – So a signal with the period $T$ contains only the frequencies $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_k}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{kF}$ with the fundamental frequency $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/T}$, the harmonics.
  • Fourier series – This is the Fourier series (synthesis).
  • DC component – $S_p(0)$ is the DC component, the mean value of the signal.
  • orthogonal – Oscillations of different frequencies are therefore called orthogonal.
