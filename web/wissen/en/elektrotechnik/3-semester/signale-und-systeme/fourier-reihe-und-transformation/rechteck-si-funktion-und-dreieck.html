What this stage is about:

Three transform pairs come up all the time. With the theorems, they follow from a single calculation.

Rectangle. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{si}(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sin(x)/x}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi ft}\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\pi f}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\pi f}}{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\sin(\pi f)}{\pi f}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{si}(\pi f).}$$

Theorem 7.1 (Rectangle, si and triangle)
$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{rect}\Bigl(\frac tT\Bigr)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;|T|\operatorname{si}(\pi Tf),\\ \operatorname{si}\Bigl(\frac{\pi t}{T}\Bigr)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;|T|\operatorname{rect}(Tf),\\ \Lambda(t)=\operatorname{rect}(t)*\operatorname{rect}(t)&\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\operatorname{si}^2(\pi f),\\ \operatorname{si}(\pi t)*\operatorname{si}(\pi t)&=\operatorname{si}(\pi t). \end{aligned}$$
(7.1)(7.2)(7.3)(7.4)

⁠How to read it: A rectangle of duration $T$ has a spectrum of height $T$ (its area) with zeros at all non-zero multiples of $1/T$. Twice as long means half as wide in the spectrum (Fig. 6). (7.2) follows from symmetry, (7.3) from the convolution theorem, and (7.4) holds because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{rect}(f)\cdot\operatorname{rect}(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}(f)}$ in the frequency domain: an ideal low-pass filter does not change a signal that is already limited to its band.

LTspiceSpectrum of a rectangular pulse

⁠Draw: just one voltage source with PULSE(0 1 0 1u 1u {T0} 16): a rectangle of duration $T_0$, repeated every 16 s.

⁠Directives: .param T0=1, .step param T0 list 1 2, .tran 0 16 0 1m, .options plotwinsize=0, .four 0.0625 40 V(in).

⁠What you see: The repetition every 16 s turns the spectrum into lines spaced $1/16$ Hz apart (Stage 4). .four reports their amplitudes $\frac{2}{16}\,|T_0\operatorname{si}(\pi T_0f)|$, i.e. samples of the si envelope. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s the lines at 1 Hz and 2 Hz are zero, for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ s already those at 0.5 Hz, 1 Hz, 1.5 Hz and 2 Hz. The DC component doubles from 0.0625 to 0.125. So double the duration means half the width and double the height, as in Fig. 6. In the FFT view you see the envelope and its zeros directly.

Exercise 8 ★ Areas and energy of the si function

Compute $\int_{-\infty}^\infty\operatorname{si}(\pi t)\mathrm{d} t$ and $\int_{-\infty}^\infty\operatorname{si}^2(\pi t)\mathrm{d} t$ without integration. Use the area theorem and Parseval.

Solution

Area theorem: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int\operatorname{si}(\pi t)\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathcal{F}\{\operatorname{si}(\pi t)\}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. Parseval: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int\operatorname{si}^2(\pi t)\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int\operatorname{rect}^2(f)\mathrm{d} f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$.

Exercise 9 ★★ Triangle and repeated convolution

  1. Find the spectrum of $\Lambda(t/2)$ (triangle with half-width 2 s). Where are the zeros?

  2. What is the spectrum of $\operatorname{rect}*\operatorname{rect}*\operatorname{rect}$? Why do the side lobes decay faster the more often you convolve?

Solution

(a) $\Lambda(t)\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\operatorname{si}^2(\pi f)$ and similarity with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$: $\Lambda(t/2)\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\,2\operatorname{si}^2(2\pi f)$. Zeros at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k/2}$, $k\neq0$.

(b) $\operatorname{si}^3(\pi f)$. Each further convolution multiplies the spectrum by $\operatorname{si}(\pi f)$, which decays like $1/(\pi f)$ in the side lobes. For the $M$-fold convolution, the side lobes decay like $1/f^M$. In the time domain: the rectangle jumps (decay $1/f$), the triangle has kinks ($1/f^2$), the threefold convolution has a continuous derivative ($1/f^3$). The smoother a signal, the faster its spectrum decays.
