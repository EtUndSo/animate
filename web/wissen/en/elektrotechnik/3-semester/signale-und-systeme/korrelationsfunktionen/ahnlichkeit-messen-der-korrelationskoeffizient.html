Stage 2: Measuring similarity, the correlation coefficient

What this stage is about:

How do you measure with a single number how similar two signals are? You measure how large their difference is.

The idea. The energy of the difference $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Delta(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)-g(t)}$ is small when the signals are similar. Multiplied out:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_\Delta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int|s-g|^2\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{E_s+E_g-\int s^*g\,\mathrm{d} t-\int g^*s\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{E_s+E_g-2\operatorname{Re}\int s^*(t)g(t)\,\mathrm{d} t.}$$

So the difference lies solely in the integral of the product. To keep the size of the signals from counting, you normalize both to the energy 1. Then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_\Delta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2-2\operatorname{Re}\,p_{sg}}$ with the correlation coefficient

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p_{sg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s^*(t)\,g(t)\,\mathrm{d} t}{\sqrt{E_sE_g}}.}$$
(2.1)

⁠How to read it: $|p_{sg}|\le1$ (Cauchy–Schwarz inequality). $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ means $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\,g}$ with $a>0$: the same shape. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$ means $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-a\,g}$: the same shape, opposite sign. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ means $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int s^*g\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$; the signals are orthogonal, like the harmonics of a Fourier series in Chapter 3. Example: $\operatorname{rect}(t)$ and $\Lambda(t)$ have $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int\operatorname{rect}\cdot\Lambda\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.75}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_{\operatorname{rect}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_\Lambda}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac23}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.919}$.

Exercise 2 ★★ Correlation coefficients

Compute $p$ for (a) $\operatorname{rect}(t)$ and $\operatorname{rect}(t-0.5)$, (b) $\operatorname{rect}(t)$ and $\Lambda(t)$ (result above), (c) $\operatorname{rect}(t)$ and $\operatorname{sgn}(t)\operatorname{rect}(t)$.

Solution

(a) The rectangles overlap over a length of 0.5, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$, and both energies are 1: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int\operatorname{rect}\cdot\Lambda\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}(1-|t|)\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.75}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_\Lambda}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\int_0^1(1-t)^2\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac23}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{0.75}{\sqrt{2/3}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.919}$. Very similar shapes. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int\operatorname{sgn}(t)\operatorname{rect}^2(t)\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$; the signals are orthogonal, although they are equal on the right half.
