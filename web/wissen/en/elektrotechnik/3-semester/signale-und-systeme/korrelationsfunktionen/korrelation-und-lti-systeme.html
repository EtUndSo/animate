What this stage is about:

What does an LTI system do to the ACF and the energy spectral density? And how do you measure a system with cross-correlation?

Theorem 5.1 (Wiener–Lee)

For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s*h}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{gg}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)*\varphi^E_{hh}(\tau)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;|G(f)|^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|S(f)|^2\,|H(f)|^2,}$$
(5.1)
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sg}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)*h(\tau)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;|S(f)|^2\,H(f).}$$
(5.2)
Derivation: Wiener–Lee

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{gg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g^*(-\tau)*g(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{[s^*(-\tau)*h^*(-\tau)]*[s(\tau)*h(\tau)]}$. Rearrange using the commutative and associative laws: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{[s^*(-\tau)*s(\tau)]*[h^*(-\tau)*h(\tau)]}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^E_{ss}*\varphi^E_{hh}}$. Likewise, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s^*(-\tau)*s(\tau)*h(\tau)}$.

⁠How to read it: The system multiplies the energy at each frequency by $|H|^2$. The phase of the system plays no role for the energy. Equation (5.2) is a measurement method: if the input has an ACF that is almost a weighted Dirac impulse $c\,\delta(\tau)$ (for example a very short pulse), then the cross-correlation between input and output is almost $c\,h(\tau)$, that is, the impulse response up to the factor $c$. Unlike $|H|^2$, it also contains the phase.

Exercise 5 ★★ Energy at the output

The exponential pulse $\varepsilon(t)\mathrm{e}^{-t}$ passes through an RC circuit with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{RC}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s.

  1. Find $|G(f)|^2$ and from it the output energy. Hint: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d} x}{(1+x^2)^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac\pi2}$.

  2. Check in the time domain with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t)}$ (Chapter 1, two RC circuits in cascade).

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|G|^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|S|^2|H|^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{(1+(2\pi f)^2)^2}}$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\pi f}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d} x}{(1+x^2)^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac\pi2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac14}$.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_0^\infty t^2\mathrm{e}^{-2t}\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{2}{2^3}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac14}$. Both ways agree. The energy drops from $\frac12$ to $\frac14$ because the second RC circuit attenuates the higher frequencies further.
