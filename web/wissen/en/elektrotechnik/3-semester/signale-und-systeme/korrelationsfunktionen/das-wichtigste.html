The answer in three sentences

  1. The similarity of two signals is measured by the integral of their product. The correlation function does this for every shift $\tau$; it is a convolution with the flipped signal.

  2. The Fourier transform of the autocorrelation function is the energy spectral density $|S(f)|^2$. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ it equals the energy (Parseval).

  3. An LTI system multiplies energy spectral densities by $|H(f)|^2$ and convolves autocorrelation functions with the autocorrelation function of $h$.

Key points

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int|s|^2\mathrm{d} t}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\lim\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^T|s|^2\mathrm{d} t}$.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p_{sg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\int s^*g\,\mathrm{d} t}{\sqrt{E_sE_g}}}$, $|p|\le1$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: orthogonal.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sg}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int s^*(t)g(t+\tau)\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s^*(-\tau)*g(\tau)}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{gs}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^{E*}_{sg}(-\tau)}$.

  • ACF: even (for real signals), maximum $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{ss}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{E_s}$, twice the duration.

  • $\varphi^E_{ss}\leftrightarrow|S|^2$, $\varphi^E_{sg}\leftrightarrow S^*G$; Parseval $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int|s|^2\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int|S|^2\mathrm{d} f}$.

  • Wiener–Lee: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{gg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^E_{ss}*\varphi^E_{hh}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|G|^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|S|^2|H|^2}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^E_{ss}*h}$.

  • Bandpass: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sgT}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12s_T^*(-\tau)*g_T(\tau)}$.

  • Discrete: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi(m)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum s^*(n)g(n+m)}$, $\leftrightarrow|S_\mathrm a|^2$; periodic: $S_d^*G_d$.

One principle behind all the results. Correlation is convolution with a flipped signal. That is why all the tools of convolution apply:

ResultWhere it comes from
Correlation coefficientenergy of the difference of two normalized signals (Stage 2)
Correlation productconvolution with the flipped, conjugated signal (Stage 3)
ACF even, maximum at 0flipping, and a signal is most similar to itself (Stage 3)
Energy spectral density, Parsevalflipping and conjugating become $S^*$, convolution becomes a product (Stage 4)
Wiener–Leeassociative law of convolution (Stage 5)
Discrete-time, periodic, DFTthe same calculation with sums (Stage 7)
