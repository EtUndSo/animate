What this stage is about:

Before comparing signals, you need a measure of their “size”. In system theory this is the energy, or, for infinitely long signals, the power.

In a resistor $R$, the energy $\frac1R\int u^2\mathrm{d} t$ is converted into heat during the time from $t_1$ to $t_2$. Without the factor $1/R$, the expression is called the signal energy:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|s(t)|^2\,\mathrm{d} t.}$$
(1.1)

If it is finite, $s$ is called an energy signal (for example, any pulse). If a signal lasts infinitely long without decaying (a cosine, a step), its energy is infinite. Then you measure the average power:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|s(t)|^2\,\mathrm{d} t.}$$
(1.2)

If it is finite, $s$ is called a power signal. For periodic signals, averaging over one period is sufficient. For Dirac impulses, energy and power are not defined ($\delta^2$ does not exist).

Exercise 1 ★ Energy or power?

Find the energy or the power of (a) $a\operatorname{rect}(t/T)$, (b) $\varepsilon(t)\mathrm{e}^{-t}$, (c) $A\cos(2\pi Ft)$, (d) $\varepsilon(t)$.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a^2|T|}$. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_0^\infty\mathrm{e}^{-2t}\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12}$. (c) Infinite energy, power $\frac{A^2}{2}$ (the mean value of $\cos^2$ is $\frac12$). (d) Infinite energy, power $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\lim\frac{1}{2T}\int_0^T1\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12}$: the signal is 1 for half of the time.
