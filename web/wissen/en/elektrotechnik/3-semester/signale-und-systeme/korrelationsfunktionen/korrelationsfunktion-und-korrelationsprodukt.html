Stage 3: Correlation function and correlation product

What this stage is about:

An echo is similar to the transmitted pulse, but shifted. You therefore compare for every shift $\tau$.

Definition 3.1 (Correlation function for energy signals)
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sg}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s^*(t)\,g(t+\tau)\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s^*(-\tau)*g(\tau).}$$
(3.1)

For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s}$ it is called the autocorrelation function (ACF) $\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)$, otherwise the cross-correlation function (CCF). Divided by $\sqrt{E_sE_g}$, it is called normalized.

⁠How to read it: You shift $g$ to the left by $\tau$ and form the integral of the product. The second equals sign is the heart of the matter: correlation is convolution with the flipped, conjugated signal. That is why it is also called the correlation product. Unlike convolution, nothing is flipped here; both signals run in the same direction.

Derivation: Correlation as convolution

Set $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\vartheta}$ in (3.1): $\int s^*(-\vartheta)\,g(\tau-\vartheta)\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta$. This is the convolution integral of $s^*(-\tau)$ with $g(\tau)$.

Theorem 3.2 (Properties of correlation functions)
$$\begin{aligned} &\varphi^E_{gs}(\tau)=\varphi^{E*}_{sg}(-\tau),\qquad\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)=\varphi^{E*}_{ss}(-\tau)\ \ (\text{even for real }s),\\ &|\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)|\le\varphi^E_{ss}(0)=E_s. \end{aligned}$$
(3.2)(3.3)

A time-limited signal has an ACF of twice the duration.

⁠How to read it: A signal is most similar to itself without a shift, and there the ACF is its energy. The ACF of a rectangle is the triangle: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{ss}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}(-\tau)*\operatorname{rect}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Lambda(\tau)}$, twice as wide as the rectangle (Fig. 1, left).

Examplefinding an echo

The transmitted signal is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}(t)}$; the received signal is the attenuated, delayed echo $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5\operatorname{rect}(t-3)}$. The CCF is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{sg}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5\,\Lambda(\tau-3)}$: its maximum lies at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, the delay, and its height $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0.5}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5\cdot E_s}$ indicates the attenuation (Fig. 1, right). This is how a radar measures distance.

QuestionConvolution and correlation look almost the same. What is the difference?

In convolution, $\int s(\tau)h(t-\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau$, the second signal runs backward ($-\tau$): the system “remembers” the past. In correlation, $\int s^*(t)g(t+\tau)\mathrm{d} t$, both run forward: you place one shape exactly over the other and measure how well they match. Mathematically, the difference is precisely the flipping; that is why correlation is convolution with the flipped signal.

LTspiceFinding an echo with a moving integrator

A filter that at every moment integrates over the last second forms $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{t-1}^{t}g(u)\,\mathrm{d} u}$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}(t)}$ this is exactly the CCF (3.1), delayed by 0.5 s: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi^E_{sg}(t-0.5)}$. The filter computes it for all $\tau$ while the signal is coming in.

The height 0.5 is the attenuation of the echo.

This filter integrates over two windows that match the double rectangle, and V(y) is its ACF, shifted by 3 s. Compare with your result from Exercise 3(a).

Exercise 3 ★★ Echo and repetition

  1. A signal consists of two rectangles: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}(t)+\operatorname{rect}(t-2)}$. Find its ACF. How can you see the repetition in it?

  2. Check (3.3) for this signal.

Solution

(a) With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{rect}(-\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}(\tau)}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{rect}*\operatorname{rect}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Lambda}$, the four partial products give

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{ss}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\Lambda(\tau)+\Lambda(\tau-2)+\Lambda(\tau+2).}$$

The side peaks at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\pm2}$ show the spacing of the repetition. There, in each case, one of the two rectangles lies over the other. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ both lie on top of each other, which gives twice the height.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi^E_{ss}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{E_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+1}$, and the side peaks have only the value 1.
