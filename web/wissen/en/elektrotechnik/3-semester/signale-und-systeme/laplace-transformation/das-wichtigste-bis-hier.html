The answer in three sentences

  1. An LTI system can only multiply the building block $\mathrm{e}^{pt}$ by a number $H(p)$, because for $\mathrm{e}^{pt}$ shifting is the same as multiplying.

  2. Every signal with a region of convergence is a superposition of such building blocks. $S(p)$ gives the weight with which the building block $\mathrm{e}^{pt}$ is contained in the signal.

  3. This is why convolution becomes multiplication, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G(p)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H(p)\,S(p)}$. If $S(p)$ is rational, the signal is a finite sum $\sum_r A_r\mathrm{e}^{p_r t}$ with the poles as exponents.

Key points so far

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{p(t-\tau)}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-p\tau}\mathrm{e}^{pt}}$, hence $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{pt}*h}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H(p)\,\mathrm{e}^{pt}}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(p)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int h(t)\mathrm{e}^{-pt}\mathrm{d} t}$.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sigma+\mathrm{j}2\pi f}$: $\sigma$ is the growth rate, $f$ the frequency of the building block.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{j}}\int_{\sigma-\mathrm{j}\infty}^{\sigma+\mathrm{j}\infty}S(p)\mathrm{e}^{pt}\mathrm{d} p}$, $\sigma$ in the region of convergence; every such line gives the same $s(t)$.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S(\sigma+\mathrm{j}2\pi f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathcal{F}\{s(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma t}\}}$; for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ the Fourier transform.

  • Region of convergence: depends only on $\sigma$; right-sided: a right half-plane, left-sided: a left half-plane, two-sided: a strip.

  • $\mathrm{e}^{p_0t}\varepsilon(t)\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{p-p_0}$ for $\operatorname{Re} p>\operatorname{Re} p_0$; $-\mathrm{e}^{p_0t}\varepsilon(-t)\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{p-p_0}$ for $\operatorname{Re} p<\operatorname{Re} p_0$.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G(p)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S(p)H(p)}$; differentiation $\to p$, integration $\to1/p$.

  • Rational and right-sided: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{A_0\delta(t)+\sum_rA_r\mathrm{e}^{p_rt}\varepsilon(t)}$, poles = exponents. Causal and stable: all poles on the left.

One principle behind all the results. Almost every result about the Laplace transform follows from these three sentences. The table matches each result to its source, and the stages explain it.
