What this stage is about:

The ideal lowpass passes all frequencies up to $f_\mathrm g$ unchanged and blocks all frequencies above. This stage shows what this sharp cutoff means in the time domain.

Theorem 2.1 (Ideal lowpass)
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}\Bigl(\frac{f}{2f_\mathrm g}\Bigr)}$$
$$\bullet\!\!-\!\!\circ$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2f_\mathrm g\operatorname{si}(2\pi f_\mathrm gt).}$$
(2.1)

⁠How to read it: the impulse response extends to infinity in both directions. So the ideal lowpass is not causal: it would have to respond to an impulse before the impulse arrives. It is a mathematical limiting object that can only be approximated (Stages 4, 6).

Duration and bandwidth. The signal duration $t_m$ of an impulse response is taken to be the width of a rectangle with the same height $h_{\max}$ and the same area:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{h_{\max}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(t)\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{H(0)}{h_{\max}}.}$$
(2.2)

For the ideal lowpass, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h_{\max}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2f_\mathrm g}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{2f_\mathrm g}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_\mathrm g\cdot t_m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12}$. In general, lowpass filters satisfy $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_\mathrm g\cdot t_m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\text{const.}}$: double the bandwidth means half the response duration. This is the similarity theorem from Chapter 3.

Overshoot. For the si function, the ratio of the side maximum with the largest magnitude, $a_1$, to the main maximum $a_0$ is

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\ddot u}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Bigl|\frac{a_1}{a_0}\Bigr|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{21.7\,\%,}$$

independent of $f_\mathrm g$ (the first side minimum of $\operatorname{si}(\pi u)$ lies at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.43}$ and is $-0.217$). Changing $f_\mathrm g$ only compresses the curve; it does not change its shape.

Exercise 2 ★ Ideal lowpass for speech

An ideal lowpass with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_\mathrm g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ kHz. What are the signal duration of the impulse response and the overshoot? What changes for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_\mathrm g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8}$ kHz?

Solution

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{2f_\mathrm g}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{125}$ µ s, overshoot 21.7 %. At 8 kHz the signal duration halves to 62.5 µ s. The overshoot remains 21.7 %, because the impulse response is only compressed.
