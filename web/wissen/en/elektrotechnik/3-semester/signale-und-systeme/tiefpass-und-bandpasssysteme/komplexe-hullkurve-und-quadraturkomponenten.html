Stage 8: Complex envelope and quadrature components

What this stage is about:

What holds for systems also holds for signals: a bandpass signal is a lowpass signal on a carrier. This stage shows the different ways of writing this down.

Transforming (7.2) back with the modulation rule gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12h_T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t}+\frac12\bigl[h_T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t}\bigr]^*}$, and so, because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{z+z^*}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\operatorname{Re} z}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}\bigl\{h_T(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t}\bigr\},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}\bigl\{s_T(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t}\bigr\}.}$$
(8.1)

$s_T(t)$ is called the complex envelope, $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t}$ the complex carrier. Half their product is the analytic component from Chapter 3, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_+(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12s_T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t}}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2s_+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t}}$.

Theorem 8.1 (Quadrature representation)

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_{Tr}+\mathrm{j} s_{Ti}}$ and the Hilbert transform $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\hat s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s*\frac{1}{\pi t}}$:

$$\begin{aligned} s(t)&=s_{Tr}(t)\cos(2\pi f_0t)-s_{Ti}(t)\sin(2\pi f_0t),\\ s_{Tr}(t)&=s(t)\cos(2\pi f_0t)+\hat s(t)\sin(2\pi f_0t),\qquad s_{Ti}(t)=\hat s(t)\cos(2\pi f_0t)-s(t)\sin(2\pi f_0t),\\ s(t)&=|s_T(t)|\cos\bigl(2\pi f_0t+\varphi_T(t)\bigr),\qquad|s_T|=\sqrt{s_{Tr}^2+s_{Ti}^2}. \end{aligned}$$
(8.2)(8.3)(8.4)

⁠How to read it: $s_{Tr}$ and $s_{Ti}$ are called quadrature components (the in-phase and the quadrature component): one modulates the cosine of the carrier, the other its sine. $|s_T|$ is the envelope, which you see on an oscilloscope as the “outline” of the signal, and $\varphi_T$ is the phase deviation of the carrier. If the complex envelope is real (symmetric case), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_T(t)\cos(2\pi f_0t)}$: an amplitude-modulated cosine.

Derivation: Quadrature components

(8.2): $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Re}\{(s_{Tr}+\mathrm{j} s_{Ti})(\cos+\mathrm{j}\sin)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_{Tr}\cos-s_{Ti}\sin}$. For (8.3): $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2s_+}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s+\mathrm{j}\hat s}$ (Chapter 3), so

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(s+\mathrm{j}\hat s)(\cos-\mathrm{j}\sin)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(s\cos+\hat s\sin)+\mathrm{j}(\hat s\cos-s\sin).}$$

Exercise 8 ★★ Determining complex envelopes

Determine $s_T$, $s_{Tr}$, $s_{Ti}$ and $|s_T|$ with respect to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ Hz for (a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\bigl[1+0.5\cos(2\pi\cdot1\,\mathrm{Hz}\cdot t)\bigr]\cos(2\pi\cdot10\,\mathrm{Hz}\cdot t)}$, (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\cos(2\pi\cdot10\,\mathrm{Hz}\cdot t+0.3)}$, (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sin(2\pi\cdot10\,\mathrm{Hz}\cdot t)}$. Check (c) with (8.2).

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+0.5\cos(2\pi t)}$, real: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_{Tr}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_T}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_{Ti}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|s_T|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+0.5\cos(2\pi t)}$ between 0.5 and 1.5. An amplitude-modulated cosine.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}0.3}}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_{Tr}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\cos0.3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.955}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_{Ti}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sin0.3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.296}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|s_T|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. The envelope is constant; only the phase is shifted by 0.3.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sin x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}\{-\mathrm{j}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j} x}\}}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\mathrm{j}}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_{Tr}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_{Ti}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|s_T|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0\cdot\cos-(-1)\sin}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sin}$.
