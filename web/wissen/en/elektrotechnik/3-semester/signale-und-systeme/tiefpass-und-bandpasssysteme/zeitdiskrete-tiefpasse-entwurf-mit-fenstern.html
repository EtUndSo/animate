What this stage is about:

How do you design a digital lowpass? Exactly like the causal analog one: take the ideal lowpass, delay it, truncate it with a window.

The ideal discrete-time lowpass has the periodically repeated rectangular frequency response and the impulse response

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2f_\mathrm g\operatorname{si}(2\pi f_\mathrm gn),}$$

so in the reference model ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_\mathrm g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2}$) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.4}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(\pm1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.303}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(\pm2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.094}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(\pm3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.062}$. It, too, is not causal. You multiply it by a window $w(n)$ of $2N+1$ values, shift it by $N$ and obtain an FIR filter with linear phase. The window determines the result (Fig. 4):

  • ⁠Rectangular window: sharpest transition, but high side lobes. In the reference model ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{N}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$), the stopband from $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.3}$ on still reaches $-26.6$ dB, and the passband fluctuates by about $\pm0.5$ dB.

  • ⁠Hann window: softer transition, but a much deeper stopband: from $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.3}$ on at most $-45.9$ dB, passband flat to within $0.05$ dB.

The reason is the same as for the short-time Fourier transform in Chapter 3: the spectrum of the window is convolved with the ideal, and the hard edges of the rectangle produce slowly decaying side lobes.

Exercise 6 ★ Half-band filter

Compute the coefficients $h(0)$ to $h(3)$ of the ideal discrete-time lowpass with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_\mathrm g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.25}$. What do you notice, and why does this save computation in an FIR filter?

Solution

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5\operatorname{si}(\pi n/2)}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(\pm1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1\pi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.318}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(\pm2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(\pm3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{1}{3\pi}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.106}$. All even coefficients except $h(0)$ are zero, because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sin(\pi n/2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ for even $n$. In the FIR filter these multiplications are dropped, almost half of them.
