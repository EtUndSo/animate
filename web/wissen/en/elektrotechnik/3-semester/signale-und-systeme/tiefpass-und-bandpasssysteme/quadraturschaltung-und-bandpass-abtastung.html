What this stage is about:

How are the quadrature components obtained in practice? And how many samples does a bandpass signal need?

Quadrature circuit. If you multiply a bandpass signal by $\cos(2\pi f_0t)$ and filter it with a lowpass, $\frac12s_{Tr}$ remains. With $-\sin(2\pi f_0t)$, $\frac12s_{Ti}$ remains. The reason is trigonometric:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s\cos}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_{Tr}\cos^2-s_{Ti}\sin\cos}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac12s_{Tr}+\underbrace{\tfrac12s_{Tr}\cos(4\pi f_0t)-\tfrac12s_{Ti}\sin(4\pi f_0t)}_{\text{at }2f_0,\text{ removed by the lowpass}},}$$

and likewise $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-s\sin}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12s_{Ti}+(\text{components at }2f_0)}$. The prerequisite is that the band ends below $2f_0$. In the lowpass domain you then filter with four lowpass filters ($h_{Tr}$, $\pm h_{Ti}$) according to (9.1). At the output you modulate back onto the carrier according to (8.2), or you form the envelope $\sqrt{g_{Tr}^2+g_{Ti}^2}$ (Fig. 6).

Sampling theorem for bandpass signals. With respect to the center frequency, a bandpass signal of bandwidth $\Delta f$ has quadrature components that are lowpass signals up to $\Delta f/2$. Each of them can be sampled at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1/T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Delta f}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_ns_{Tr}(nT)\operatorname{si}\Bigl(\pi\frac{t-nT}{T}\Bigr)\cos(2\pi f_0t)-\sum_ns_{Ti}(nT)\operatorname{si}\Bigl(\pi\frac{t-nT}{T}\Bigr)\sin(2\pi f_0t),}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\Delta f}.}$$
(10.1)

In total you need $2\Delta f$ values per second, as many as for a lowpass signal with the cutoff frequency $\Delta f$. So what matters is the bandwidth, not the highest frequency.

Exercise 9 ★★ Sampling a radio signal

A signal occupies the band from 10.0 to 10.2 MHz.

  1. What sampling rate would the ordinary sampling theorem require?

  2. With the quadrature circuit, how many samples per second suffice in total, and at what rate is each branch sampled?

Solution

(a) At least $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\cdot10.2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20.4}$ MHz.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Delta f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2}$ MHz. Each branch is sampled at 0.2 MHz ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ µ s), together 0.4 MHz, about fifty times less.
