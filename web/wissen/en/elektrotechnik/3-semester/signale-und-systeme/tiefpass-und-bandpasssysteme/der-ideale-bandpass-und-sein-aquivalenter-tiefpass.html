Stage 7: The ideal bandpass and its equivalent lowpass

What this stage is about:

A bandpass passes a band around the center frequency $f_0$. This stage shows that it can be described completely by a lowpass and the frequency $f_0$.

Ideal bandpass. Two rectangles of width $\Delta f$ at $\pm f_0$ ($f_0>\Delta f/2$):

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rect}\Bigl(\frac{f}{\Delta f}\Bigr)*\bigl[\delta(f+f_0)+\delta(f-f_0)\bigr]\;\bullet\!\!-\!\!\circ\;h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Delta f\operatorname{si}(\pi\Delta f\,t)\cdot2\cos(2\pi f_0t).}$$
(7.1)

The impulse response is an oscillation at $f_0$ whose envelope is the impulse response of a lowpass with the bandwidth $\Delta f/2$. The modulation rule from Chapter 3 explains this: it is a lowpass whose spectrum is shifted to $\pm f_0$.

Equivalent lowpass. For a real impulse response, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(-f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H^*(f)}$; the negative half contains nothing new. Keep only the positive frequencies, double them and shift them to the left by a carrier frequency $f_0$. The result is the equivalent lowpass $H_T(f)$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac12H_T(f-f_0)+\tfrac12H_T^*(-f-f_0),}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_T(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0\ \text{for}\ f\le-f_0.}$$
(7.2)

$H_T$ need not be symmetric about $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, so its impulse response $h_T(t)$ can be complex. If there is an $f_0$ for which $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_T(-f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H_T^*(f)}$ holds (so that $h_T$ is real), the bandpass is called symmetric. For the ideal bandpass with $f_0$ at the center, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_T(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\operatorname{rect}(f/\Delta f)}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h_T(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\Delta f\operatorname{si}(\pi\Delta f\,t)}$, real and even. This is a special case: even symmetric bandpass filters generally have an imaginary part in the spectrum.

Exercise 7 ★★ Equivalent lowpass with a different reference

Ideal bandpass from 9 to 11 Hz.

  1. Determine $H_T$ and $h_T$ for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ Hz.

  2. Determine $H_T$ and $h_T$ for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9}$ Hz. Why is $h_T$ complex now? Do both describe the same bandpass?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ Hz: after the shift, the band lies symmetrically from $-1$ to $1$ Hz. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\operatorname{rect}(f/2)}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\operatorname{si}(2\pi t)}$, real.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9}$ Hz: the band lies from 0 to 2 Hz. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\operatorname{rect}\bigl(\frac{f-1}{2}\bigr)}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h_T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\operatorname{si}(2\pi t)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi t}}$. Complex, because $H_T$ is not symmetric about $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. Both describe the same bandpass: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Re}\{4\operatorname{si}(2\pi t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi9t}\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\operatorname{si}(2\pi t)\cos(2\pi\cdot10t)}$, the same as in (a) and as (7.1).
