Stage 11: Phase and group delay in a bandpass

What this stage is about:

Why are there two delays? Because the carrier and the envelope can be delayed differently.

The idea. Let a bandpass have a constant magnitude and a slightly curved phase in the passband. Around $f_0$, approximate the phase by its tangent:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi(f_0)+(f-f_0)\frac{\mathrm{d}\varphi}{\mathrm{d} f}\Big|_{f_0}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2\pi f_0t_\mathrm p-2\pi(f-f_0)t_\mathrm g}$$

with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_\mathrm p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t_\mathrm p(f_0)}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_\mathrm g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t_\mathrm g(f_0)}$ from (1.4) and (1.5). In the lowpass domain ($f-f_0\to f$) this is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_T(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\operatorname{rect}(f/\Delta f)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t_\mathrm p}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi ft_\mathrm g}}$: a constant phase and a delay.

Theorem 11.1 (Delays in a bandpass)
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g_T(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_T(t-t_\mathrm g)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0t_\mathrm p},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}\bigl\{s_T(t-t_\mathrm g)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f_0(t-t_\mathrm p)}\bigr\}.}$$
(11.1)

⁠How to read it: the envelope, which carries the information, is delayed by the group delay. The carrier is delayed by the phase delay. The transmission is distortionless in the strict sense only if the two are equal. That is, the phase line passes through the origin (or through a multiple of $2\pi$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$). For the information, a constant group delay is usually enough: if $t_\mathrm g$ is not constant, different frequency components of the envelope arrive at different times, and pulses spread out (delay distortion).

LTspiceEnvelope and carrier arrive at different times

⁠Draw: the resonant circuit from the LTspice hint in Stage 9 ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ Hz, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Delta f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ Hz). In the .ac analysis its phase at 10 Hz is $0^\circ$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_\mathrm p(f_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. If you switch the phase axis to “Group Delay”, it shows $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_\mathrm g(f_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.16}$ s at 10 Hz.

⁠Experiment: as the input a behavioural source bv, a carrier with a slowly varying envelope:

V=(1+0.5*cos(2*pi*0.2*time))*cos(2*pi*10*time)

Directives .tran 0 20 0 0.2m and .options plotwinsize=0.

⁠What you see: Plot V(in) and V(out) on top of each other and zoom to 14.5 s to 15.5 s. The maximum of the envelope arrives at the output about 0.16 s later, but the zero crossings of the carrier lie at the same places as at the input. This is exactly what (11.1) says: the envelope is delayed by $t_\mathrm g$, the carrier by $t_\mathrm p$.

Exercise 10 ★★ Reading off delays

At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ MHz, a bandpass has the phase $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi(f_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\pi/2}$ and the slope $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\mathrm{d}\varphi}{\mathrm{d} f}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2\pi\cdot2\,\mu\text{s}}$.

  1. Compute $t_\mathrm p$ and $t_\mathrm g$.

  2. By how much is the envelope of a pulse delayed at the output? Is the carrier then still in the same position relative to the envelope?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_\mathrm p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{\varphi(f_0)}{2\pi f_0}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\pi/2}{2\pi\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{Hz}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.25}$ µ s. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_\mathrm g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ µ s.

(b) The envelope arrives 2 µ s later. The carrier is shifted by only 0.25 µ s. Relative to the envelope it is therefore rotated by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\pi f_0(t_\mathrm g-t_\mathrm p)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.5\pi}$, which modulo $2\pi$ equals $1.5\pi$, that is $-\pi/2$: a quarter period. The information in the envelope remains undistorted as long as $t_\mathrm g$ is constant over the band.
