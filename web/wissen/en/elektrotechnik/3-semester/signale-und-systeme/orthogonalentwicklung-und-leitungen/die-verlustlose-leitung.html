What this stage is about:

Without losses ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{G'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$), the characteristic impedance and the velocity become independent of frequency. The wave solution then holds for arbitrary signals, not only for sinusoids.

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{G'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\gamma}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{j}2\pi f\sqrt{L'C'}}$: no attenuation ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\alpha}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$), and $\beta$ is proportional to $f$. From this:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{\frac{L'}{C'}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{L'C'}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial s^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L'C'\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial t^2}.}$$
(4.1)
Theorem 4.1 (d'Alembert solution)

Every solution of the wave equation (4.1) has the form

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u(s,t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u_\mathrm{fw}(s-vt)+u_\mathrm{bw}(s+vt)}$$
(4.2)

with arbitrary (twice differentiable) functions $u_\mathrm{fw}$ (forward) and $u_\mathrm{bw}$ (backward).

⁠How to read it: An arbitrary signal shape travels unchanged along the line at the velocity $v$. This is because all frequency components are equally fast and are attenuated equally little: the line is a distortion-free system in the sense of Chapter 5, with the delay $l/v$. The wave equation for $u$ follows from (2.1) by differentiating the first equation with respect to $s$ and the second with respect to $t$ and eliminating $i$.

Examplecoaxial cable

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\Omega}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$ pF/m give $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Z^2C'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{250}$ nH/m and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{L'C'}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\cdot10^8}$ m/s, two thirds of the speed of light (because of the dielectric in the cable). A signal needs 50 ns for 10 m of cable. If the cable is terminated with 75 $\Omega$ instead of 50 $\Omega$, then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{75-50}{75+50}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2}$: 20 % of the voltage amplitude travels back as an echo.

⁠Draw: a voltage source with PULSE(0 2 0 1n 1n 10n 1) and an internal resistance res with 50 to net in. From in a lossless line tline with Td=50n Z0=50 (the 10-m coaxial cable from the example) to net end, and there res with {RE} to ground. Because the internal resistance equals $Z$, a wave of 1 V sets off, and the echo is fully absorbed at the source.

⁠What you see: At in, first the pulse of 1 V. After $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\cdot50\,\mathrm{ns}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$ ns the echo returns with the height $r\cdot1$ V: $-1$, $0$, $0.2$ and $+1$, exactly $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{R_\mathrm E-Z}{R_\mathrm E+Z}}$. At end, after 50 ns, there is the sum of forward and backward wave, $(1+r)\cdot1$ V. Set Td and Z0 to your values from Exercise 4(a), set RE to 100 and compare the echo height with your result from Exercise 4(b).

Exercise 3 ★★ Checking d'Alembert

Show by substitution that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u(s,t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u_\mathrm{fw}(s-vt)}$ solves the wave equation (4.1) with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/\sqrt{L'C'}}$. How is the associated current related to the voltage? Hint: first transmission-line equation with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

Solution

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s-vt}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial s^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u_\mathrm{fw}''(x)}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial t^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{v^2u_\mathrm{fw}''(x)}$. The wave equation requires $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u''}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L'C'v^2u''}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{L'C'}}$. Current: with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-L'\frac{\partial i}{\partial t}}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_\mathrm{fw}'(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-L'\frac{\partial i}{\partial t}}$. The ansatz $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u_\mathrm{fw}(x)/Z}$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\partial i}{\partial t}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac vZu'_\mathrm{fw}}$, and this is consistent because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L'v/Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L'\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{L'C'}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{C'}{L'}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. In the forward wave, voltage and current are everywhere in the ratio $Z$.

Exercise 4 ★★ Cable and termination

A lossless cable has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{300}$ nH/m and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{120}$ pF/m.

  1. Compute $Z$, $v$ and the propagation delay for 5 m.

  2. With what resistance must it be terminated so that nothing is reflected? What reflection coefficient results with 100 $\Omega$?

  3. Up to what frequency can the 5 m still be treated as “short” if the line is to be at most one tenth of the wavelength long?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{300/120}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\text{nH}}{\text{pF}}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{2.5\cdot10^3}\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\Omega}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{300\cdot10^{-9}\cdot120\cdot10^{-12}}}\,\mathrm{m/s}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{3.6\cdot10^{-17}}}\,\mathrm{m/s}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.67\cdot10^8}$ m/s. Propagation delay for 5 m: 30 ns.

(b) With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\Omega}$. With 100 $\Omega$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{100-50}{150}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac13}$.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\lambda}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{v/f\ge50}$ m, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f\le\frac{1.67\cdot10^8}{50}\,\mathrm{Hz}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.3}$ MHz.
