Stage 6: Rules of convolution

What this stage is about:

In many respects convolution behaves like multiplication. Each rule has a meaning for systems.

The convolution integral is written in short as the convolution product $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)*h(t)}$. Equation (4.1) then reads $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)*\delta(t)}$.

  1. ⁠Identity element. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s*\delta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s}$: the Dirac impulse plays the role of the number one. A system with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\delta(t)}$ passes every signal on unchanged; it is called ideally distortion-free.

  2. ⁠Commutative law. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s*h}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{h*s}$. Meaning: input and impulse response may swap roles. The system with the impulse response $s$ responds to the input $h$ exactly as the system $h$ responds to the input $s$.

  3. ⁠Associative law. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(f*s)*h}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{f*(s*h)}$. Meaning: two systems in cascade act like one system whose impulse response is the convolution of the two impulse responses.

  4. ⁠Distributive law. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f*(s+h)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{f*s+f*h}$. Meaning: two systems connected in parallel, whose outputs are added, act like one system with the sum of the impulse responses.

  5. ⁠Complex signals. Because convolution is linear, it splits into real and imaginary parts:

    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s*h}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\bigl[\operatorname{Re} s*\operatorname{Re} h-\operatorname{Im} s*\operatorname{Im} h\bigr]+\mathrm{j}\bigl[\operatorname{Re} s*\operatorname{Im} h+\operatorname{Im} s*\operatorname{Re} h\bigr].}$$
    (6.1)
Derivation: Commutative law

Substitute $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t-\tau}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t-\tau'}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\mathrm{d}\tau'}$. The limits $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mp\infty}$ become $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\pm\infty}$; swapping them cancels the minus sign:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s*h}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(\tau)\,h(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(t-\tau')\,h(\tau')\,\mathrm{d}\tau'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{h*s.}$$

Exercise 7 ★★ Two RC circuits in cascade

Two RC circuits with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{RC}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s are connected in cascade. Assume that the second does not load the first (for example, with a voltage follower in between).

  1. Compute the impulse response of the cascade, $h*h$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t)}$.

  2. When is it at its maximum, and what is its area?

  3. Why is the condition “does not load” needed?

Solution

(a) For $t\ge0$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(h*h)(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_0^t\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-(t-\tau)}\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-t}\int_0^t\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,\mathrm{e}^{-t}.}$$

So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h_{\text{cascade}}(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t)}$.

(b) Derivative: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(1-t)\mathrm{e}^{-t}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s, maximum $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.368}$. Area: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_0^\infty t\mathrm{e}^{-t}\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\cdot1}$ (product of the areas).

(c) Without decoupling, the second RC circuit draws current from the first capacitor. The voltage across the first capacitor is then no longer that of an unloaded RC circuit, and the cascade does not have $h*h$ as its impulse response. It remains an LTI system, but with a different impulse response. Convolving the individual responses assumes that no system acts back on its input.
