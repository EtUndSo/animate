The answer in three sentences

  1. Every signal is a superposition of infinitely many short impulses: at each instant $\tau$, one impulse with the weight $s(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau$.

  2. An LTI system responds to a shifted impulse with the shifted impulse response $h(t-\tau)$ (time invariance), and to a sum of impulses with the sum of the responses (linearity).

  3. Therefore the output is the convolution $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int s(\tau)\,h(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau}$, and $h(t)$ describes the system completely.

Key points

  • Shifting $t\to t-t_0$ delays, stretching $t\to t/T$ widens; $a\operatorname{rect}\bigl(\frac{t-t_0}{T}\bigr)$: center $t_0$, duration $|T|$, height $a$.

  • RC low-pass: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(F)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{1}{1+\mathrm{j}2\pi FRC}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(2\pi FRC)^2}}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\arctan(2\pi FRC)}$; step response $1-\mathrm{e}^{-t/RC}$.

  • LTI: superposition principle and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{s(t-t_0)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g(t-t_0)}$.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int s(\tau)\delta(t-\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int s(\tau)h(t-\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s*h}$.

  • Evaluating: flip, shift, multiply, integrate.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s*\delta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s}$; commutative, associative (cascade: $h_1*h_2$), distributive (parallel: $h_1+h_2$).

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)\delta(t-T)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(T)\delta(t-T)}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\delta(bt)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\delta(t)/|b|}$ for $b\neq0$;$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\delta(t-t_0)*s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t-t_0)}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t}\varepsilon}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\delta}$.

  • Step response $=\int h$, impulse response $=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t}$ step response.

  • Causal: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ for $t<0$. Stable: $\int|h|\mathrm{d} t<\infty$.

What comes next. Convolution takes a lot of work: every time $t$ needs an integral of its own. The next part decomposes signals in such a way that the integral becomes a simple multiplication.

One principle behind all the results. The table links each result of this chapter to the principle “decompose, send one building block through, reassemble”.
