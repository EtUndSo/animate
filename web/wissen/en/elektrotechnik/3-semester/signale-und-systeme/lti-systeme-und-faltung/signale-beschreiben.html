Stage 1: Describing signals

What this stage is about:

Before studying systems, you need a language for signals: a few basic shapes and two operations on the time axis, shifting and stretching.

A signal is the course of a physical quantity over time, written as a function $s(t)$. In system theory one usually works with normalized quantities: times in multiples of 1 s, voltages in multiples of 1 V. Equations between physical quantities then become equations between pure numbers, and $\mathrm{e}^{-t}$ can be written down without any trouble with units.

Elementary signals. Some signals can be described by a simple expression and come up again and again (Fig. 1):

$$\begin{gathered} \sin(2\pi t),\qquad \mathrm{e}^{-\pi t^2}\ \text{(Gaussian)},\qquad \varepsilon(t)=\begin{cases}0&t<0\\1&t\ge0\end{cases},\\ \operatorname{rect}(t)=\begin{cases}1&|t|\le\frac12\\0&|t|>\frac12\end{cases},\qquad \Lambda(t)=\begin{cases}1-|t|&|t|\le1\\0&|t|>1\end{cases}. \end{gathered}$$

The step $\varepsilon(t)$ and the rectangle $\operatorname{rect}(t)$ jump. Which value holds exactly at the jump is a matter of convention. A common choice there is $\tfrac12$: then rectangles placed side by side add up to exactly 1 everywhere.

Shifting and stretching. New signals arise from a given one when the time axis is transformed:

  • ⁠Shifting: $t\to t-t_0$. For $t_0>0$ the signal is delayed by $t_0$, that is, moved to the right.

  • ⁠Stretching: $t\to t/T$. For $|T|>1$ the signal becomes wider, for $0<|T|<1$ narrower, and for $T<0$ it is also flipped.

Combining both, plus an amplitude $a$, gives for example the rectangular pulse

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\operatorname{rect}\Bigl(\frac{t-t_0}{T}\Bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{cases}a & |t-t_0|\le |T|/2\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}}$$
(1.1)

with center $t_0$, duration $|T|$ and height $a$. To check this, substitute the argument $(t-t_0)/T$ into the definition of $\operatorname{rect}$. Likewise, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sin(2\pi t/T)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sin(2\pi Ft)}$ is the stretched sine with period $T$ and frequency $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/T}$.

QuestionWhy does $t-t_0$ shift the signal to the right, even though something is subtracted?

At time $t$ the new signal shows the value that the old one had at time $t-t_0$, that is, earlier. Everything the old signal did, the new one does $t_0$ later. The easiest check is at a distinctive point: the edge of $\varepsilon(t-t_0)$ lies where the argument becomes zero, at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t_0}$.

The complex exponential. The most important signal for system theory is

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\cos(2\pi Ft)+\mathrm{j}\sin(2\pi Ft).}$$

By Euler's formula, a complex conjugate pair yields the real oscillations:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\cos(2\pi Ft)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}}{2},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sin(2\pi Ft)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}}{2\mathrm{j}}.}$$

For an oscillation, a delay by $t_0$ appears as a phase shift:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\cos\bigl(2\pi F(t-t_0)\bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\cos(2\pi Ft+\varphi)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}\bigl\{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\varphi}\bigr\},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2\pi Ft_0.}$$
(1.2)

⁠In short: for an oscillation, delaying is the same as rotating the phase, and for $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}$ it is even the same as multiplying by the number $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\varphi}$.

Exercise 1 ★ Shifting, stretching, phase

  1. Where does $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\operatorname{rect}\bigl(\frac{t-3}{4}\bigr)}$ begin and end, and how high is the pulse?

  2. Write down a rectangular pulse that has the height 0.5 V from $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ s.

  3. Sketch $s(-t)$ for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon(t-1)}$. Where is the edge?

  4. A cosine with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50}$ Hz is delayed by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ ms. What is its phase $\varphi$ afterwards?

Solution

(a) Center $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, duration $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$: from $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$, height 2.

(b) Center 2.5 s, duration 3 s: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5\operatorname{rect}\bigl(\frac{t-2.5}{3}\bigr)}$.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(-t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon(-t-1)}$. The edge lies where the argument becomes zero: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-t-1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$. The value is 1 where the argument is $\ge0$, that is, for $t\le-1$. A flipped step: 1 to the left of $-1$, 0 to the right of it.

(d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2\pi Ft_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2\pi\cdot50\,\mathrm{Hz}\cdot5\,\mathrm{ms}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\pi/2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-90^\circ}$. A delay of a quarter period is a quarter turn.
