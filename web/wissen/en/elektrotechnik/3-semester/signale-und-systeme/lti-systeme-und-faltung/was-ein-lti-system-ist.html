Stage 3: What an LTI system is

What this stage is about:

The RC circuit has two properties on which everything else rests: it is linear and time-invariant. This stage defines both and shows what they make possible.

A system assigns an output signal to an input signal:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Tr}\{s(t)\}.}$$

Such a mapping from functions to functions is called a transformation. It does not map one numerical value to another: the output at time $t$ may depend on the entire past course of the input. In the RC circuit, $u_2(t)$ depends on how much charge has accumulated up to $t$.

Definition 3.1 (LTI system)

A system is called

  • linear if the superposition principle holds for arbitrary constants $a_i$:

    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\Bigl\{\sum_ia_is_i(t)\Bigr\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_ia_i\operatorname{Tr}\{s_i(t)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_ia_ig_i(t);}$$
    (3.1)

  • time-invariant if for every shift $t_0$:

    $$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{s(t-t_0)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g(t-t_0).}$$
    (3.2)

A linear, time-invariant system is called an LTI system for short (LTI stands for linear time-invariant).

In the RC circuit, linearity comes from the fact that the differential equation (2.1) contains neither products nor squares of $u_1$ and $u_2$. Time invariance comes from the fact that $R$ and $C$ do not change with time. Every network of $R$, $L$ and $C$ is described by linear differential equations with constant coefficients. This does not hold for every LTI system, though: an ideal delay element, for example, which merely delays its input by a fixed time, has no such equation.

What the two properties allow. If you know the response $g_0$ to a rectangular pulse $s_0$, you know, without further calculation, the response to any sum of shifted and scaled rectangular pulses. Figure 4 shows this for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_0(t)+0.5\,s_0(t-2)}$: the output is $g_0(t)+0.5\,g_0(t-2)$.

QuestionIs $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2s(t)+1}$ linear? Its characteristic is a straight line, after all.

No. For a linear system, zero input must give zero output: setting all $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{a_i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ in (3.1) gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{0\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. Here $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{0\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. The superposition principle fails as well: two inputs applied separately give $2s_1+2s_2+2$ in total, but applied together they give $2s_1+2s_2+1$. A straight line that does not pass through the origin is called affine, not linear.

Exercise 3 ★ LTI or not?

Check each system for linearity and time invariance: (a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3\,s(t-2)}$, (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)^2}$, (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)+1}$, (d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,s(t)}$, (e) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(2t)}$, (f) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^ts(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau}$.

Solution

(a) Linear and time-invariant (gain 3, delay 2): LTI. (b) Not linear: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{2s\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4s^2\neq2s^2}$. Time-invariant. (c) Not linear (affine, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{0\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$). Time-invariant. (d) Linear. Not time-invariant: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{s(t-t_0)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,s(t-t_0)}$, but $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t-t_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(t-t_0)\,s(t-t_0)}$. The factor $t$ is a component that changes with time. (e) Linear. Not time-invariant: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{s(t-t_0)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(2t-t_0)}$, but $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t-t_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(2t-2t_0)}$. Compressing time also compresses every shift. (f) LTI. Linear, because the integral is linear. Time-invariant: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_{-\infty}^ts(\tau-t_0)\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{t-t_0}s(\vartheta)\mathrm{d}\vartheta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g(t-t_0)}$. Its impulse response is $\varepsilon(t)$ (Stage 8).
