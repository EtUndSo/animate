Stage 5: Evaluating the convolution

What this stage is about:

The integral (4.2) has to be evaluated for concrete signals. The key is to draw both factors against the integration variable $\tau$.

The recipe. For a fixed instant $t$:

  1. Draw $s(\tau)$ against $\tau$.

  2. Draw $h(-\tau)$: flip $h$ about the vertical axis.

  3. $h(t-\tau)$: shift the flipped $h$ to the right by $t$.

  4. ⁠Multiply the two and integrate over $\tau$: the result is $g(t)$.

As $t$ increases, the flipped $h$ slides from left to right across $s$. Wherever the two do not overlap, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

Why flipped? As a function of $\tau$, the argument $t-\tau$ decreases as $\tau$ increases. The beginning of $h$ (age zero) lies at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t}$, and older impulses lie further to the left. For the RC circuit, $h$ is largest at the beginning: the system “remembers” the most recent past most strongly.

ExampleRC circuit and rectangular pulse

The impulse response of the RC circuit with the time constant $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{RC}$ is a decaying exponential pulse of area 1. The short impulse charges the capacitor abruptly to $1/T$, after which it discharges through $R$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1T\,\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-t/T}.}$$
(5.1)

Input: a rectangle of height $a$ from $0$ to $T_0$. Figure 6 shows the position of the two factors at two instants. There are three regions:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{cases}0 & t<0,\\[2pt] a\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-t/T}\bigr) & 0\le t\le T_0,\\[2pt] a\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{T_0/T}-1\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{-t/T} & t>T_0.\end{cases}}$$
(5.2)
Derivation: Response to the rectangular pulse

⁠$t<0$: The shifted $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero only for $\tau\le t<0$, and $s(\tau)$ only for $\tau\ge0$. No overlap, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

⁠$0\le t\le T_0$: Overlap for $0<\tau<t$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_0^ta\,\frac1T\,\mathrm{e}^{-(t-\tau)/T}\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac aT\,\mathrm{e}^{-t/T}\int_0^t\mathrm{e}^{\tau/T}\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac aT\,\mathrm{e}^{-t/T}\cdot T\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{t/T}-1\bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-t/T}\bigr).}$$

⁠$t>T_0$: Overlap only for $0<\tau<T_0$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac aT\,\mathrm{e}^{-t/T}\int_0^{T_0}\mathrm{e}^{\tau/T}\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{T_0/T}-1\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{-t/T}.}$$

In the reference model ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{a}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s): $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(0.5)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.3935}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6321}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2325}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.0855}$. This is the same curve as $g_0$ in Fig. 4.

QuestionWhy do we flip $h$ and not $s$?

You may also flip $s$ instead of $h$: the substitution $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t-\tau}$ turns $\int s(\tau)\,h(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau$ into $\int s(t-\tau')\,h(\tau')\,\mathrm{d}\tau'$. Flip whichever function makes the shifting easier to follow, usually the one with the simpler shape.

Exercise 5 ★★ Rectangle with rectangle

Compute $\operatorname{rect}(t)*\operatorname{rect}(t)$ with the recipe. Distinguish $|t|>1$, $-1\le t\le0$ and $0\le t\le1$. Which elementary signal comes out?

Solution

The flipped rectangle is again $\operatorname{rect}$ (it is symmetric); after shifting, it occupies $[t-\frac12,t+\frac12]$. The integral is the length of the overlap with $[-\frac12,\frac12]$:

  • $|t|>1$: no overlap, 0.

  • $-1\le t\le0$: overlap from $-\frac12$ to $t+\frac12$, length $1+t$.

  • $0\le t\le1$: overlap from $t-\frac12$ to $\frac12$, length $1-t$.

Together, $1-|t|$ for $|t|\le1$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{rect}*\operatorname{rect}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Lambda}$. Numerically: 1.000, 0.500 and 0.200 at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, 0.5 and 0.8.

Exercise 6 ★★ Checking the rectangle response

  1. Show that (5.2) is continuous at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T_0}$. Why must it be continuous?

  2. Where does $g(t)$ have its maximum, and how large is it in the reference model?

  3. Show that the area under $g(t)$ equals $aT_0$, and justify this without calculation using $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int h\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$.

Solution

(a) At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T_0}$: $a(1-\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T})$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{a(\mathrm{e}^{T_0/T}-1)\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a(1-\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T})}$, which are equal. Physically: the capacitor voltage cannot jump, because that would require an infinite current. Mathematically: the convolution of a bounded signal with an integrable $h$ is continuous.

(b) During the pulse $g$ rises, and afterwards it falls. The maximum is at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T_0}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(1\,\mathrm{s})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.632}$.

(c) By calculation:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int g\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\Bigl[T_0-T\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T}\bigr)\Bigr]+a\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{T_0/T}-1\bigr)T\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{aT_0.}$$

Without calculation: the area of a convolution is the product of the areas, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int g\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int\!\!\int s(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int s\,\mathrm{d}\tau\cdot\int h\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{aT_0\cdot1}$. Numerically: 1.000.
