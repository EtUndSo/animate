What this stage is about:

With the RC low-pass (Fig. 2) we can work out how a system responds to two typical inputs: a complex exponential and a step.

The differential equation. The current through $R$ is $(u_1-u_2)/R$. All of it flows into the capacitor and charges it, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C\,\dot u_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(u_1-u_2)/R}$. Rearranged:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{RC\,\frac{\mathrm{d} u_2}{\mathrm{d} t}+u_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u_1.}$$
(2.1)

Input $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}$: the output is a number times the input. If $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}}$ is applied, the output in the steady state has the same form, only with a different amplitude and phase: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H(F)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}}$. The number $H(F)$ depends on the frequency and is therefore called the frequency response. The reason is that differentiation does not change the form, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{j}2\pi F\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}}$. Substituting into (2.1):

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H(F)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}\,(1+\mathrm{j}2\pi FRC)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi Ft}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H(F)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{j}2\pi FRC}.}$$

AC analysis with phasors gives the same result: the capacitor has the impedance $1/(\mathrm{j}2\pi FC)$, and the circuit is a voltage divider,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\underline U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1/(\mathrm{j}2\pi FC)}{R+1/(\mathrm{j}2\pi FC)}\,\underline U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{j}2\pi FRC}\,\underline U_1.}$$

The real voltage belonging to a phasor is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}\{\sqrt2\,|\underline U|\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}(2\pi Ft+\varphi)}\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt2\,|\underline U|\cos(2\pi Ft+\varphi)}$ (RMS phasor).

Theorem 2.1 (Transfer function of the RC low-pass)

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\pi FRC}$:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Re}\{H\}&=\frac{1}{1+x^2},\qquad \operatorname{Im}\{H\}=\frac{-x}{1+x^2},\\ |H(F)|&=\sqrt{\operatorname{Re}\{H\}^2+\operatorname{Im}\{H\}^2}=\sqrt{H\,H^*}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}},\qquad \varphi(F)=-\arctan x. \end{aligned}$$
(2.2)(2.3)

⁠How to read it: $|H|$ tells you by what factor the amplitude shrinks. $\varphi$ is negative: the oscillation lags by the angle $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\arctan x}$. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, that is, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/(2\pi RC)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.16}$ Hz in the reference model, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.707}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-45^\circ}$. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.0995}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-84.3^\circ}$ (Fig. 3). Limiting cases: $F\to0$ gives $H\to1$ (the capacitor has time to charge fully); $F\to\infty$ gives $H\to0$ with $\varphi\to-90^\circ$ (the capacitor acts like a short circuit).

The phase in general. For an arbitrary complex number $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{Re}\{H\}+\mathrm{j}\operatorname{Im}\{H\}}$,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\arctan\frac{\operatorname{Im}\{H\}}{\operatorname{Re}\{H\}}+\lambda\pi+l\cdot2\pi,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\lambda}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{cases}0&\operatorname{Re}\{H\}\ge0\\1&\operatorname{Re}\{H\}<0\end{cases},\quad l\ \text{integer},}$$
(2.4)

where $\arctan$ returns the principal value in $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$.

QuestionWhy does the phase formula need the extra term $\lambda\pi$?

$\arctan$ sees only the ratio $\operatorname{Im}/\operatorname{Re}$. The numbers $1+\mathrm{j}$ and $-1-\mathrm{j}$ have the same ratio 1, but lie in opposite quadrants. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\arctan1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{45^\circ}$ is correct for $1+\mathrm{j}$. For $-1-\mathrm{j}$ the true angle is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{45^\circ+180^\circ}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{225^\circ}$, equivalently $-135^\circ$ (with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{l}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$). Whenever the real part is negative, you have to add $\pi$.

Step input: the exponential curve. At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ the voltage $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ V is applied; the capacitor is empty. For $t\ge0$, Kirchhoff's voltage law with the current $i$ gives

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R\,i(t)+\frac1C\int_0^ti(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.}$$

Differentiating gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R\,\frac{\mathrm{d} i}{\mathrm{d} t}+\frac iC}$, hence $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\mathrm{d} i}{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{\mathrm{d} t}{RC}}$, and integrating gives

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\ln|i|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac{t}{RC}+K\quad\Longrightarrow\quad i(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{A\,\mathrm{e}^{-t/RC},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{i(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1R,}$$

because at the first instant the whole voltage is across $R$. Kirchhoff's voltage law, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-R\,i}$, then gives

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_2(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-t/RC}}$$
$$(t\ge0).$$
(2.5)

The mechanism: the fuller the capacitor, the less voltage is left for $R$, the smaller the charging current, and the more slowly the capacitor continues to fill. This feedback makes the curve exponential.

Exercise 2 ★★ Frequency response and phase

  1. Compute $|H|$ and $\varphi$ of the RC low-pass for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $1$ and $10$. Which frequency in Hz corresponds to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ in the reference model?

  2. A signal $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_1(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\cos(2\pi Ft)}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ is applied. Write down $u_2(t)$. By how many seconds does $u_2$ lag if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{RC}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s?

  3. Use (2.4) to determine the phases of $-1-\mathrm{j}$, $-\mathrm{j}$ and $-2$.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.707}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-45^\circ}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.0995}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-84.3^\circ}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ means $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10/(2\pi RC)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.59}$ Hz.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_2(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.707\cos(2\pi Ft-45^\circ)}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\pi F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/\mathrm s}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.159}$ Hz. It lags by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\varphi/(2\pi F)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(\pi/4)/(1/\mathrm s)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.785}$ s.

(c) $-1-\mathrm{j}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\arctan1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{45^\circ}$, real part negative, so add $180^\circ$: $225^\circ$, equivalently $-135^\circ$. $-\mathrm{j}$: real part zero, imaginary part negative; $\arctan(\operatorname{Im}/\operatorname{Re})$ tends to $-90^\circ$, so the phase is $-90^\circ$. $-2$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\arctan0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, real part negative, so $180^\circ$.
