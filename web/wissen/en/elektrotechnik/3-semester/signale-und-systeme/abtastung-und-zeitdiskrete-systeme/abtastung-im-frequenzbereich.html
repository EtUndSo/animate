Stage 4: Sampling in the frequency domain

What this stage is about:

The same principle holds with the roles swapped: if you sample the spectrum, the signal becomes periodic.

A spectrum that is known only at the frequencies $kF$ is a comb of weighted Dirac impulses. With the factor $F$, the weights are exactly the Fourier coefficients from Chapter 3:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{F\sum_kS(kF)\,\delta(f-kF)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{F\,S(f)\cdot\sum_k\delta(f-kF)\;\bullet\!\!-\!\!\circ\;s_p(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_ns\Bigl(t-\frac nF\Bigr).}$$
(4.1)

The corresponding signal is the original, repeated periodically with period $1/F$. If the signal is shorter than $1/F$, the repetitions do not overlap. Then it can be recovered with a time window, and the spectrum with si functions:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_p(t)\cdot\operatorname{rect}(Ft)}$$
$$\Longrightarrow$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_kS(kF)\operatorname{si}\Bigl(\pi\frac{f-kF}{F}\Bigr).}$$
(4.2)

⁠In short: discrete in time means periodic in the spectrum, and discrete in the spectrum means periodic in time. The second is the Fourier series from Chapter 3: its coefficients are samples of the spectrum of a single pulse, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_p(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{F\cdot S(kF)}$.
