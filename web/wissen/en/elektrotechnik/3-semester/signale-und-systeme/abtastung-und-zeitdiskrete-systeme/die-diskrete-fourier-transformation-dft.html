Stage 8: The discrete Fourier transform (DFT)

What this stage is about:

$S_\mathrm a(f)$ is a continuous function, but a computer needs finitely many numbers. For a sequence of finite length, finitely many samples of the spectrum are enough.

The idea. If $s(n)$ is limited to $M$ values, then by Stage 4 samples of the spectrum spaced $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/M}$ apart are enough. They correspond to the signal $s_d(n)$, repeated periodically with period $M$. Because the spectrum is also periodic (time discrete), $M$ spectral values suffice. Both sides are now discrete and periodic.

Theorem 8.1 (Discrete Fourier transform)
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_d(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}s_d(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi kn/M},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_d(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1M\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}S_d(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi kn/M},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{k,n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0,\dots,M-1.}$$
(8.1)

⁠How to read it: $S_d(k)$ is the value of the spectrum $S_\mathrm a(f)$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k/M}$. The index $k$ counts oscillations that fit exactly $k$ times into the $M$ values. The inverse transform is a Fourier series with $M$ terms. The theorems hold analogously, for example the shift $s_d(n-m)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S_d(k)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi mk/M}$ (circular: what is shifted out at the end comes back in at the start) and Parseval $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sum_n|s_d(n)|^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1M\sum_k|S_d(k)|^2}$.

Example

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_d}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1,\,2,\,0,\,-1)}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi/4}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\mathrm{j}}$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_d(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=0}^{3}s_d(n)(-\mathrm{j})^{kn}:}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_d(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2,\quad S_d(1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-3\mathrm{j},\quad S_d(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0,\quad S_d(3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+3\mathrm{j}.}$$

Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_d(3)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_d^*(1)}$, because the signal is real ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{k}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ corresponds to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{k}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$). Parseval: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1+4+0+1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac14(4+10+0+10)}$.

Exercise 8 ★★ DFT by hand

  1. Recompute $S_d(1)$ for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_d}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1,2,0,-1)}$.

  2. Use the shift theorem to determine the DFT of the circularly shifted sequence $(-1,1,2,0)$, and check $S_d(1)$ directly.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_d(1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+2(-\mathrm{j})+0\cdot(-1)+(-1)(-\mathrm{j})^3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-2\mathrm{j}-\mathrm{j}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-3\mathrm{j}}$, because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(-\mathrm{j})^3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{j}}$.

(b) Shift by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$: $S_d(k)\,(-\mathrm{j})^k$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(2,\;(1-3\mathrm{j})(-\mathrm{j}),\;0,\;(1+3\mathrm{j})\mathrm{j})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(2,\;-3-\mathrm{j},\;0,\;-3+\mathrm{j})}$. Directly: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_d(1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1+1\cdot(-\mathrm{j})+2\cdot(-1)+0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-3-\mathrm{j}}$.
