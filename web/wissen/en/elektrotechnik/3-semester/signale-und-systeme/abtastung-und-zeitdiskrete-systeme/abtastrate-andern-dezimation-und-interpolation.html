Stage 10: Changing the sampling rate, decimation and interpolation

What this stage is about:

A sequence can afterwards be represented at a lower or higher rate. The same rules apply to the spectrum as for the original sampling.

Downsampling by the factor $c$. Only every $c$th value is kept. Conceptually, first multiply by a comb of unit impulses, then drop the zeros:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_c(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(n)\sum_m\delta(n-mc),}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_u(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(nc).}$$

Multiplication by the comb produces $c$ copies of the spectrum spaced $1/c$ apart, and dropping the zeros stretches the frequency axis by $c$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_{c,\mathrm a}(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1c\sum_{k=0}^{c-1}S_\mathrm a\Bigl(f-\frac kc\Bigr),}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_{u,\mathrm a}(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_{c,\mathrm a}\Bigl(\frac fc\Bigr).}$$
(10.1)

If the spectrum extends up to $f_\mathrm g$ (normalized to the old rate), after downsampling it extends up to $cf_\mathrm g$. Aliasing is avoided only if $cf_\mathrm g\le\frac12$. Otherwise a digital low-pass filter must limit the band beforehand.

Upsampling by the factor $c$. Insert $c-1$ zeros between every two values: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_\uparrow(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(n/c)}$ for $n$ divisible by $c$, otherwise 0. The spectrum is compressed by $c$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_{\uparrow,\mathrm a}(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_\mathrm a(cf)}$, and now contains $c-1$ spectral images within the period. A low-pass filter with the cutoff frequency $\frac{1}{2c}$ and the gain $c$ removes them. In doing so it fills the zeros with interpolated values (the si interpolation from Stage 3 in discrete form), and the original values are preserved. Downsampling and upsampling in succession change the rate by a rational factor $c_1/c_2$.

Exercise 10 ★★ Downsampling

A sequence has a spectrum up to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_\mathrm g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.2}$.

  1. May it be downsampled by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$ without a prefilter? By $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$?

  2. What must happen before downsampling by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, and what is lost in the process?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$: afterwards the band extends up to $0.4\le0.5$, no aliasing. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{c}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$: up to $0.6>0.5$, aliasing.

(b) Limit the band beforehand to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{1}{2c}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac16}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.167}$ with a digital low-pass filter. The components between 0.167 and 0.2 are lost in the process. That is the price of the lower rate.
