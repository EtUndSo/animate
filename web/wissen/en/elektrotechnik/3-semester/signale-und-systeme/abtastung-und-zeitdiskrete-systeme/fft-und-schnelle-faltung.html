Stage 9: FFT and fast convolution

What this stage is about:

The DFT needs $M^2$ multiplications. The FFT gets by with roughly $M\,\mathrm{lb}\,M$. This also makes convolution via the frequency domain fast.

The idea of the FFT. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W_M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi/M}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_d(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_ns_d(n)W_M^{kn}}$. For even $M$, split into values with even and odd index:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_d(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\underbrace{\sum_{n=0}^{M/2-1}s_d(2n)\,W_{M/2}^{kn}}_{S_{d,1}(k)}+W_M^k\underbrace{\sum_{n=0}^{M/2-1}s_d(2n+1)\,W_{M/2}^{kn}}_{S_{d,2}(k)},}$$
(9.1)

because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W_M^{2kn}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{W_{M/2}^{kn}}$. Instead of one DFT of length $M$ you need two of length $M/2$ and $M$ multiplications by $W_M^k$, so $2(M/2)^2+M$ instead of $M^2$. Both partial DFTs are periodic with period $M/2$, so they also supply the values for $k\ge M/2$. If $M$ is a power of two, the splitting is repeated $\mathrm{lb}\,M$ times. The effort drops to about $M\,\mathrm{lb}\,M$. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1024}$ that is 10,240 instead of 1,048,576 multiplications, roughly a hundred times fewer. This is the fast Fourier transform (FFT).

Circular convolution. For periodic sequences with period $M$, the circular (periodic) convolution is

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g_d(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}s_d(m)\,h_d\bigl((n-m)\bmod M\bigr)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;G_d(k)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{S_d(k)\,H_d(k).}$$
(9.2)

It is the periodically repeated result of ordinary convolution. If $M$ is too small, the repetitions overlap. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1,1,1)}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1,2)}$ the ordinary convolution is $(1,3,3,2)$, with four values. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ the last value falls onto the first: $(3,3,3)$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ the result is correct: $(1,3,3,2)$. In general $M\ge M_1+M_2-1$ is required ($M_1$, $M_2$: lengths of the two sequences); to achieve this, the sequences are padded with zeros.

Fast convolution. Instead of evaluating the convolution sum directly, you transform both sequences with the FFT, multiply and transform back. A signal of arbitrary length is split into blocks; each block is convolved separately, and the results are added. This is allowed because convolution is distributive:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(n)*h(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Bigl[\sum_is_i(n)\Bigr]*h(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_i\bigl[s_i(n)*h(n)\bigr].}$$

Exercise 9 ★★ Circular or linear

  1. Compute the circular convolution of $(1,1,1)$ and $(1,2,0)$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ by hand. Which value of the linear convolution has moved where?

  2. A signal with 1000 values is to be convolved via the DFT with a filter of 25 values. What is the minimum length of the DFT?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_0h_0+s_1h_2+s_2h_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+0+2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_0h_1+s_1h_0+s_2h_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2+1+0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s_0h_2+s_1h_1+s_2h_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0+2+1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$. The fourth value of the linear convolution (2 at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$) has moved circularly to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1+2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$.

(b) At least $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1000+25-1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1024}$. Conveniently, this is a power of two.
