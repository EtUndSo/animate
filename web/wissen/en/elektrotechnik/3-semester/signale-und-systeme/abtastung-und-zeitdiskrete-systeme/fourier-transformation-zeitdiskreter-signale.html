Stage 7: The discrete-time Fourier transform

What this stage is about:

What spectrum does a sequence of numbers have? It is the spectrum of the ideally sampled signal, computed directly from the samples.

If you insert $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_\mathrm a(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_ns(nT)\delta(t-nT)}$ into the Fourier integral, the sifting property leaves a sum. Normalized to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$:

Theorem 7.1 (Discrete-time Fourier transform)
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_\mathrm a(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi nf},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}S_\mathrm a(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi nf}\,\mathrm{d} f.}$$
(7.1)

⁠How to read it: $S_\mathrm a(f)$ is periodic with period 1, which is the sampling rate. One period, $|f|\le\frac12$, contains everything. This is why the inverse transform integrates only over an interval of length 1. The sum is a Fourier series in $f$, with the samples as coefficients.

Example

For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon(n)\,b^n}$ with $|b|<1$ (geometric series):

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S_\mathrm a(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl(b\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f}\bigr)^n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{1-b\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f}},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|S_\mathrm a(f)|^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{1+b^2-2b\cos(2\pi f)}.}$$
(7.2)

This is also the frequency response $H_\mathrm a(f)$ of the recursive system from Stage 6. For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{b}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.6}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H_\mathrm a|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.5}$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $1.60$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.1}$, $0.86$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.25}$, $0.625$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$ (Fig. 6). The system is a low-pass filter, but with a periodic frequency response: at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ it amplifies as strongly as at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ again.

Convolution and multiplication. As with functions, $s(n)*h(n)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;S_\mathrm a(f)H_\mathrm a(f)$. The product of two sequences gives the periodic convolution of the spectra over one period:

$$s(n)\cdot h(n)\;\circ\!\!-\!\!\bullet\;\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}S_\mathrm a(\nu)\,H_\mathrm a(f-\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu.$$
(7.3)
Exercise 7 ★ Moving average

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(n)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac13\bigl[\delta(n)+\delta(n-1)+\delta(n-2)\bigr]}$ averages three consecutive values.

  1. Show that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_\mathrm a(f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac13\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f}\bigl[1+2\cos(2\pi f)\bigr]}$.

  2. At which frequency is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H_\mathrm a}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$? Which oscillation does the filter therefore cancel completely, and why (hint: the sum of three values of an oscillation with period 3)?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac13\bigl(1+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}4\pi f}\bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac13\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f}\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}2\pi f}+1+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f}\bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac13\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f}\bigl[1+2\cos(2\pi f)\bigr]}$.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\cos(2\pi f)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac12}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac13}$ (at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_\mathrm A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ kHz: 333 Hz). An oscillation with three values per period consists of three phasors spaced $120^\circ$ apart. Their sum is zero; in every window the filter sees exactly one whole period.
