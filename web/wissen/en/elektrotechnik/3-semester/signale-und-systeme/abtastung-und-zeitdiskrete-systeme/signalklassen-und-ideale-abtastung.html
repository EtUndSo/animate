Stage 1: Signal classes and ideal sampling

What this stage is about:

What kinds of signals are there, and how is sampling described mathematically?

Four classes. Time and amplitude can each be continuous or discrete. An analog signal is continuous in both. A discrete-time signal has values only at the times $nT$, but these with arbitrary precision. A digital signal is in addition stepped in amplitude (quantized), because a computer can represent only finitely many numbers. This chapter deals with the discretization of time. The quantization of the amplitude is a separate topic.

From the real to the ideal sampler. In every clock cycle, a real sampler closes a switch for the short time $T_0$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_0(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{rect}\Bigl(\frac{t-nT}{T_0}\Bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)\Bigl[\operatorname{rect}\Bigl(\frac{t}{T_0}\Bigr)*\sum_n\delta(t-nT)\Bigr].}$$
(1.1)

If $T_0$ is made shorter and shorter, only the values $s(nT)$ remain. Mathematically, the rectangle is replaced by a Dirac impulse. This is the ideal sampler:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s_\mathrm a(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t-nT)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(nT)\,\delta(t-nT).}$$
(1.2)

The sampled signal is a train of Dirac impulses, each with a sample as its weight. The second step is the sifting property $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)\delta(t-nT)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(nT)\delta(t-nT)}$. In this form, sampled signals can be treated with all the tools for continuous signals.

Summary

Terms
  • analog – An analog signal is continuous in both.
  • discrete-time – A discrete-time signal has values only at the times $nT$, but these with arbitrary precision.
  • digital – A digital signal is in addition stepped in amplitude (quantized), because a computer can represent only finitely many numbers.
  • ideal sampler – Mathematically, the rectangle is replaced by a Dirac impulse. This is the ideal sampler.
