The answer in three sentences

  1. A random signal is described by averages: mean value, power, variance, autocorrelation function and probability density function. For ergodic processes, they may be determined from a single, long signal.

  2. The Fourier transform of the autocorrelation function is the power spectral density. An LTI system multiplies it by $|H(f)|^2$, exactly as for energy signals (Chapter 6).

  3. Against white noise, the matched filter $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(T-t)}$ is optimal. It achieves the signal-to-noise ratio $E_s/N_0$, regardless of the signal shape.

Key points

  • Ensemble average $\mathrm E\{\cdot\}$; stationary: independent of shifts; ergodic: time average $=$ ensemble average.

  • $m_s$ (DC component), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm E\{s^2\}}$ (power), $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma_s^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L_s-m_s^2}$ (AC power).

  • ACF: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{ss}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L_s}$, even, maximum at 0; covariance $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mu}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi-m^2}$; sum: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma_s^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sigma_u^2+\sigma_v^2+2\mu_{uv}(0)}$.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_s(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{Prob}[s\le x]}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P_s'}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{m_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int xp_s}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int x^2p_s}$; uniform distribution $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a^2/12}$.

  • Characteristic curve: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p_g(y)|\mathrm{d} y|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{p_s(x)|\mathrm{d} x|}$; quantization error $\Delta^2/12$, 6 dB per bit.

  • Independent $\Rightarrow$ uncorrelated; sum of independent variables: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{p_f*p_g}$; Gaussian: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\mathrm{e}^{-(x-m)^2/2\sigma^2}$.

  • LTI: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{m_g}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{m_sH(0)}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{gg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{ss}*\varphi^E_{hh}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Phi_{gg}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Phi_{ss}|H|^2}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int\Phi\,\mathrm{d} f}$.

  • White: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Phi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{N_0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{N_0\delta}$; thermal $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm E\{u^2\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4kTRf_\mathrm g}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{kT}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ pW/GHz.

  • Matched filter: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\,s(T-t)}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(S_a/N)_{\max}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{E_s/N_0}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_{e,\min}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12\operatorname{erfc}\sqrt{E_s/(8N_0)}}$.

One principle behind all the results. You replace the unknown signal by its averages, and averages are linear:

ResultWhere it comes from
Mean value, power, varianceensemble average and time average (Stages 1–3)
ACF, covariance, CCFaverage over products of values at two instants (Stage 4)
Probability density functionfraction of time or of the ensemble below a threshold (Stage 5)
Sum of independent variablesconvolution of the densities, hence Gaussian (Stages 6, 7)
Power spectral density, Wiener–Leelinearity of the expected value in the convolution integral (Stage 8)
White noiseconstant power spectral density, ACF $=N_0\delta$ (Stage 9)
Matched filterCauchy–Schwarz: the correlation is greatest with the signal itself (Stage 10)
Error probabilityarea under a Gaussian curve beyond the threshold (Stage 11)
