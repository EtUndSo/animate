Stage 4: Autocorrelation, covariance and cross-correlation

What this stage is about:

How fast does a random signal change? The ACF tells you: how similar the signal still is to itself after the time $\tau$.

Theorem 4.1 (Properties of the ACF of stationary processes)
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{ss}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L_s,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{ss}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{ss}(-\tau),}$$
$$|\varphi_{ss}(\tau)|\le\varphi_{ss}(0).$$
(4.1)

For ergodic processes, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{ss}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\overline{s(t)s(t+\tau)}}$, that is, the ACF of a power signal from Chapter 6.

Derivation: Maximum at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$

$\mathrm E\{[s(t)\pm s(t+\tau)]^2\}\ge0$, because the expected value of a square is never negative. Multiplying out and using linearity: $\mathrm E\{s^2(t)\}+\mathrm E\{s^2(t+\tau)\}\pm2\mathrm E\{s(t)s(t+\tau)\}\ge0$, so $2\varphi_{ss}(0)\pm2\varphi_{ss}(\tau)\ge0$. Evenness: substitute $t\to t-\tau$ in the definition.

Covariance. If you subtract the mean value first, you obtain the autocovariance function

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mu_{ss}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm E\{[s(t)-m_s][s(t+\tau)-m_s]\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{ss}(\tau)-m_s^2,}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mu_{ss}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sigma_s^2.}$$
(4.2)

If the process contains no periodic components, values that lie far apart become independent: $\varphi_{ss}(\tau)\to m_s^2$ and $\mu_{ss}(\tau)\to0$ for $\tau\to\infty$.

Cross-correlation and sums. For two processes, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{uv}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm E\{u(t)v(t+\tau)\}}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{vu}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{uv}(-\tau)}$, and the cross-covariance is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mu_{uv}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{uv}(\tau)-m_um_v}$. For the sum $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u+v}$, multiplying out gives

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{ss}(\tau)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varphi_{uu}+\varphi_{uv}+\varphi_{vu}+\varphi_{vv},}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L_s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L_u+L_v+2\varphi_{uv}(0),}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma_s^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sigma_u^2+\sigma_v^2+2\mu_{uv}(0).}$$
(4.3)

⁠In short: Powers add only if the signals are uncorrelated. Otherwise the cross term is added. Two identical signals give four times the power, two opposite ones give zero power.

Exercise 2 ★★ Two noise sources

Two zero-mean noise voltages with the RMS values 3 mV and 4 mV are added. What is the RMS value of the sum if they are (a) uncorrelated, (b) identical in shape ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{uv}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sigma_u\sigma_v}$), (c) identical in shape but of opposite sign ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi_{uv}(0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\sigma_u\sigma_v}$)?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{9+16}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ mV. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9+16+2\cdot12}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{49}$, so 7 mV: the amplitudes add. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sigma^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9+16-24}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, so 1 mV: the signals almost cancel each other.
