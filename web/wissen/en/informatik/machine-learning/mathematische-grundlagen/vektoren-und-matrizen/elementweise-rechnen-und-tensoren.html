What this stage is about:

Not every calculation with vectors and matrices is a matrix product. This section presents the calculations that work entry by entry, and it extends vectors and matrices to arrays of numbers with more than two indices, as they are needed for images.

The Hadamard product#

A shopkeeper has the list of quantities sold and the list of unit prices. For each item he wants the revenue, that is, quantity times price, but not yet the sum. This is a product entry by entry.

Definition 15.1 (Hadamard product)

For two vectors or two matrices of the same size, the Hadamard product $\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{B}$ is the product entry by entry:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{B})_{ij}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a_{ij}\,b_{ij} .}$$
(15.1)

The Hadamard product is not the matrix product. The matrix product forms dot products of rows and columns; the Hadamard product only multiplies entries in the same position. Two connections with things you already know: if you add up the Hadamard product of two vectors, you get their dot product, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf 1^{\top}(\mathbf{u}\odot\mathbf{v})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{v}}$. And the Hadamard product with a fixed vector is multiplication by a diagonal matrix: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{v}\odot\mathbf{x}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{v})\,\mathbf{x}}$.

Worked example

$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(1,\,2,\,3)^{\top}\odot(4,\,5,\,6)^{\top}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(4,\,10,\,18)^{\top}}$, and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{4+10+18}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{32}$ is the dot product. For matrices,

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}\odot\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\3&0\end{pmatrix},}$$
$$\text{but}$$
$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\4&3\end{pmatrix}.}$$

With a vector of zeros and ones you can mask out features: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(1,\,0,\,1)^{\top}\odot\mathbf{x}_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(60,\,0,\,3)^{\top}}$ drops the number of rooms of apartment 1.

⁠In short: The Hadamard product $\odot$ multiplies entry by entry; it is not the matrix product.

Elementwise functions#

Often you apply the same function to every entry of a vector: you square all errors, take the absolute value of all of them or round all values.

Definition 15.2 (Elementwise function)

For a function $f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ and a vector $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^d$, $f$ applied elementwise is the vector $\bigl(f(x_1),\dots,f(x_d)\bigr)^{\top}$. For matrices, the same is done entry by entry.

Many norms can be written this way: the 1-norm is the sum of the elementwise absolute values, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf 1^{\top}|\mathbf{x}|}$, and the square of the Euclidean norm is the sum of the elementwise squares. An elementwise function is in general not linear: elementwise squaring turns $2\mathbf{x}$ into the vector with the entries $4x_j^2$, not $2x_j^2$.

Worked example

The real estate agent's error vector was $(-12,\,-4,\,-4,\,-8)^{\top}$. The elementwise absolute values give $(12,\,4,\,4,\,8)^{\top}$ with the sum $28$, the 1-norm. For appraiser A from Subsection 3.5, the elementwise squares give $(100,\,100,\,100,\,100)^{\top}$ with the sum $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{400}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20^2}$.

⁠In short: An elementwise function acts on each entry separately; in general it is not linear.

Adding a vector to every row#

To center a whole dataset, you subtract the same vector, the mean of the examples, from every row of the data matrix. In the same way, the affine prediction adds the same intercept to every component. Such calculations need a notation.

The mean of the examples is given by a matrix–vector product: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1N\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf 1}$, because its component $j$ is $\frac1N\sum_ix_{ij}$, the mean of column $j$. The matrix whose rows all equal $\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}$ is the outer product $\mathbf 1\,\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}$. So the centered data matrix is

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf{X}-\mathbf 1\,\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\top} ,}$$
(15.2)

and its row $i$ is the vector $(\mathbf{x}_i-\bar{\mathbf{x}})^{\top}$. Many texts write $\mathbf{X}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}$ for short and mean by it: subtract the row vector from every row. Strictly speaking, the difference of an $N\times d$ matrix and a $1\times d$ matrix is not defined; the shorthand always stands for (15.2).

Worked example

For the apartments, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac14\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf 1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(65,\ 2.5,\ 3)^{\top}}$ and

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}60&2&3\\40&1&1\\90&4&6\\70&3&2\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}65&2.5&3\\65&2.5&3\\65&2.5&3\\65&2.5&3\end{pmatrix}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}-5&-0.5&0\\-25&-1.5&-2\\25&1.5&3\\5&0.5&-1\end{pmatrix}.}$$

Each column is the centered feature column from Subsection 4.3 and sums to zero.

⁠In short: Adding a vector $\mathbf{q}$ to every row of a matrix $\mathbf{A}$ means forming $\mathbf{A}+\mathbf 1\,\mathbf{q}^{\top}$; the common shorthand $\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{q}^{\top}$ means exactly this.

Tensors#

A scalar is a single number, a vector a list with one index, a matrix a table with two indices. A color image needs three: row, column and color channel. A stack of color images needs four. All these arrays of numbers are gathered under one name.

Definition 15.3 (Tensor, shape)

In machine learning, a tensor is an array of numbers whose entries are numbered by $k$ indices. Each index runs along an axis. The list of the axis lengths is called the shape of the tensor.

A scalar is a tensor with $0$ axes, a vector in $\mathbb{R}^d$ one with a single axis of length $d$, a matrix in $\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ one with the shape $m\times n$. A tensor of shape $n_1\times n_2\times\dots\times n_k$ has $n_1\cdot n_2\cdots n_k$ entries. Figure 42 shows the first few levels.

Worked example

A color image with $4\times4$ pixels and three color channels is a tensor of shape $3\times4\times4$ with $48$ entries. The entry with the indices $(1,2,3)$ is the brightness of the first channel, that is, red, at the pixel in row $2$ and column $3$.

⁠In short: A tensor is an array of numbers with any number of axes; scalar, vector and matrix are the cases with $0$, $1$ and $2$ axes.

BackgroundThe tensor of physics is something different

In physics, a tensor is more than an array of numbers, for example the inertia tensor, which states how hard it is to rotate a body about each axis, or the tensor that describes how strongly a loaded beam is pulled or pushed in each direction. There, a tensor comes with a rule for how its entries change when you rotate the coordinate system; the entries alone do not make it a tensor. In machine learning this rule is absent: there, a tensor is just a multidimensional array of numbers. The word is the same, the concept is a different one.

Images as tensors#

Image data are the most important case of tensors with more than two axes. Their shape shows at once how many numbers a dataset contains.

A grayscale image with $28\times28$ pixels is a matrix, that is, a tensor of shape $28\times28$ with $784$ entries. A color image with $32\times32$ pixels has three channels and the shape $3\times32\times32$, that is, $3072$ entries. A stack of $100$ such color images has the shape $100\times3\times32\times32$ and $307\,200$ entries. The first axis counts the examples, just like the rows of the data matrix: the data matrix is the special case of a stack of vectors, with the shape $N\times d$.

The order of the axes is a convention. Some programs put the color channel first ($3\times32\times32$), others last ($32\times32\times3$). The numbers are the same, only their arrangement differs, and before any calculation it must be clear which axis means what.

Worked example

A dataset of $60\,000$ grayscale images with $28\times28$ pixels is a tensor of shape $60\,000\times28\times28$ with $47\,040\,000$ entries. If you read out each image into a vector in $\mathbb{R}^{784}$, this becomes a data matrix in $\mathbb{R}^{60\,000\times784}$, with the same entries.

⁠In short: An image is a tensor with two or three axes, a stack of images one with one more axis; the first axis counts the examples.

Exercise 36 ★ Elementwise or not?

  1. Compute $\mathbf{u}\odot\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{v}$ for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{u}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(2,\,-1,\,3)^{\top}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{v}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1,\,4,\,2)^{\top}}$.

  2. Compute $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\odot\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\1&3\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and the matrix product of the same matrices.

  3. What shape does a stack of $64$ color images with $224\times224$ pixels have, with the color channel first, and how many entries does it have?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{u}\odot\mathbf{v}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(2,\,-4,\,6)^{\top}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{v}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2-4+6}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$, the sum of the entries of the Hadamard product.

(b) Hadamard: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1\cdot2&2\cdot0\\3\cdot1&4\cdot3\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\3&12\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)}$. Matrix product: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2+2&0+6\\6+4&0+12\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}4&6\\10&12\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)}$. Only the bottom right entry happens to agree.

(c) The shape is $64\times3\times224\times224$, with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{64\cdot3\cdot224\cdot224}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9\,633\,792}$ entries.

Exercise 37 ★★ Centering a dataset

  1. Show: the columns of the centered data matrix (15.2) sum to zero, that is, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf{0}^{\top}}$. Use $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf 1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{N}$.

  2. Center the data matrix of the three texts from Subsection 5.4.

Solution

(a) We have $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1N\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf{X}}$, the transpose of $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1N\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf 1}$. Hence

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf{X}-(\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf 1)\,\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf{X}-N\cdot\tfrac1N\,\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf{X}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf{0}^{\top} .}$$

Each column is a centered vector, and its components add up to zero, as in Subsection 4.3.

(b) The mean of the texts is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac13\bigl((3,2,0)^{\top}+(6,4,0)^{\top}+(0,1,3)^{\top}\bigr)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(3,\,\tfrac73,\,1)^{\top}}$. Centered:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac13&-1\\3&\frac53&-1\\-3&-\frac43&2\end{pmatrix}.}$$

Each column sums to zero: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0+3-3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-\tfrac13+\tfrac53-\tfrac43}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-1-1+2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.
