Key points so far

  • An example with $d$ features is a column vector $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^d$; a dataset of $N$ examples is the data matrix $\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}$ with one example per row.

  • The dot product $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{v}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_ju_jv_j}$ is a weighted sum and a comparison with a pattern; the weighted sum $\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}$ is the most general linear rule with one output.

  • Norms measure size: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_j|x_j|}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_\infty}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\max_j|x_j|}$; the RMS value is $\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert/\sqrt n$. Distances $\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\rVert$ depend on the units; standardizing makes them independent of the units.

  • $|\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{v}|\le\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert$; the angle is given by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\cos\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{v}/(\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert)}$; orthogonal means $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{v}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

  • $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}$ is a list of dot products (row view) and a linear combination of the columns (column view); $\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}$ gives all predictions at once.

  • Every linear map is a matrix whose columns are the images of the unit vectors; affine means $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{b}$.

  • $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}$ is “first $\mathbf{B}$, then $\mathbf{A}$”; associative, not commutative; linear stages in series are a single matrix.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})^{\top}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}}$; $\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{\top}$ contains all dot products of the examples, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sum_i\mathbf{x}_i\mathbf{x}_i^{\top}}$ those of the features.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf{B}^{-1}\mathbf{A}^{-1}}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf{y}}$ has no solution, one or infinitely many, and it is solvable exactly when $\mathbf{y}$ lies in the column space.

  • $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\dim\ker\mathbf{A}+\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{n}$; $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathbf{y}}$ is solvable exactly when $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A}\,|\,\mathbf{y})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{A}}$, and the solution is then unique exactly when $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{A}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{n}$; dependent features lower the rank and make the weights ambiguous.

  • $\det\mathbf{A}$ is the signed volume factor, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\det(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\det\mathbf{A}\det\mathbf{B}}$, and $\mathbf{A}$ is invertible exactly when $\det\mathbf{A}\ne0$.

  • $\odot$ multiplies entry by entry; tensors are arrays of numbers with any number of axes.

The answer in three sentences

  1. A dataset is a matrix whose rows contain the examples as vectors, and similarity, distance and size are dot products and norms of these vectors, which depend on the chosen units.

  2. A linear rule is determined by the images of the unit vectors and is therefore a matrix, and the matrix–vector product applies it to all examples at once.

  3. Composition is multiplication of the matrices, and whether a map can be reversed is decided by rank, null space and determinant: they show whether directions are lost along the way.

One principle behind all the results. Almost everything in this chapter follows from two operations: vectors are added and multiplied by numbers, and linear maps preserve exactly these two operations.
