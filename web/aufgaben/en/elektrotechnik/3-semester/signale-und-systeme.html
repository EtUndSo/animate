Stage 1: Describing signals

Exercise 1 ★ Shifting, stretching, phase

  1. Where does $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\operatorname{rect}\bigl(\frac{t-3}{4}\bigr)}$ begin and end, and how high is the pulse?

  2. Write down a rectangular pulse that has the height 0.5 V from $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ s.

  3. Sketch $s(-t)$ for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon(t-1)}$. Where is the edge?

  4. A cosine with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50}$ Hz is delayed by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ ms. What is its phase $\varphi$ afterwards?

Solution

(a) Center $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, duration $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$: from $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$, height 2.

(b) Center 2.5 s, duration 3 s: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5\operatorname{rect}\bigl(\frac{t-2.5}{3}\bigr)}$.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s(-t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon(-t-1)}$. The edge lies where the argument becomes zero: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-t-1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$. The value is 1 where the argument is $\ge0$, that is, for $t\le-1$. A flipped step: 1 to the left of $-1$, 0 to the right of it.

(d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2\pi Ft_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2\pi\cdot50\,\mathrm{Hz}\cdot5\,\mathrm{ms}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\pi/2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-90^\circ}$. A delay of a quarter period is a quarter turn.

Stage 2: A first system, the RC circuit

Exercise 2 ★★ Frequency response and phase

  1. Compute $|H|$ and $\varphi$ of the RC low-pass for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $1$ and $10$. Which frequency in Hz corresponds to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ in the reference model?

  2. A signal $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_1(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\cos(2\pi Ft)}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ is applied. Write down $u_2(t)$. By how many seconds does $u_2$ lag if $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{RC}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s?

  3. Use (2.4) to determine the phases of $-1-\mathrm{j}$, $-\mathrm{j}$ and $-2$.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.707}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-45^\circ}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|H|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.0995}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-84.3^\circ}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ means $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10/(2\pi RC)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.59}$ Hz.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_2(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.707\cos(2\pi Ft-45^\circ)}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\pi F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/\mathrm s}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.159}$ Hz. It lags by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\varphi/(2\pi F)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(\pi/4)/(1/\mathrm s)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.785}$ s.

(c) $-1-\mathrm{j}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\arctan1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{45^\circ}$, real part negative, so add $180^\circ$: $225^\circ$, equivalently $-135^\circ$. $-\mathrm{j}$: real part zero, imaginary part negative; $\arctan(\operatorname{Im}/\operatorname{Re})$ tends to $-90^\circ$, so the phase is $-90^\circ$. $-2$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\arctan0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, real part negative, so $180^\circ$.

Stage 3: What an LTI system is

Exercise 3 ★ LTI or not?

Check each system for linearity and time invariance: (a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3\,s(t-2)}$, (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)^2}$, (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t)+1}$, (d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,s(t)}$, (e) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(2t)}$, (f) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^ts(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau}$.

Solution

(a) Linear and time-invariant (gain 3, delay 2): LTI. (b) Not linear: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{2s\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4s^2\neq2s^2}$. Time-invariant. (c) Not linear (affine, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{0\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$). Time-invariant. (d) Linear. Not time-invariant: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{s(t-t_0)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,s(t-t_0)}$, but $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t-t_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(t-t_0)\,s(t-t_0)}$. The factor $t$ is a component that changes with time. (e) Linear. Not time-invariant: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{Tr}\{s(t-t_0)\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(2t-t_0)}$, but $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t-t_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(2t-2t_0)}$. Compressing time also compresses every shift. (f) LTI. Linear, because the integral is linear. Time-invariant: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_{-\infty}^ts(\tau-t_0)\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_{-\infty}^{t-t_0}s(\vartheta)\mathrm{d}\vartheta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{g(t-t_0)}$. Its impulse response is $\varepsilon(t)$ (Stage 8).

Exercise 4 ★★ A closer look at the staircase

  1. Compute the response $g_0(t)$ of the RC circuit ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{RC}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s) to the rectangle of height $1/T_0$ from $0$ to $T_0$. To do this, use (2.5) and write the rectangle as the difference of two steps.

  2. Show that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g_0(T_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1-\mathrm{e}^{-T_0})/T_0}$ and that it tends to 1 as $T_0\to0$.

  3. Why does the approximation $g_a$ in Fig. 5 come too late? (Hint: where does the stair step lie relative to the triangle on the rising edge, and where on the falling edge?)

Solution

(a) The rectangle is $\frac{1}{T_0}[\varepsilon(t)-\varepsilon(t-T_0)]$. Linearity and time invariance together with the step response (2.5) give

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g_0(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{T_0}\Bigl[\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-t}\bigr)\varepsilon(t)-\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-(t-T_0)}\bigr)\varepsilon(t-T_0)\Bigr].}$$

(b) At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T_0}$ the second term is zero: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g_0(T_0)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1-\mathrm{e}^{-T_0})/T_0}$. With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{-T_0}}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-T_0+T_0^2/2}$ for small $T_0$, this is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-T_0/2\to1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{h(0^+)}$. Numbers: 0.632, 0.787 and 0.952 for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$, 0.5 and 0.1 s.

(c) Each stair step holds the value $s(nT_0)$ from the beginning of its interval to the end. On the rising edge the staircase therefore lies below the triangle, and on the falling edge above it: on average it is delayed by $T_0/2$. The output inherits this delay; on the falling side, $g_a$ lies above $g$ ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g_a(2)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.508}$ instead of $0.400$ for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{T_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.5}$ s). As $T_0$ gets smaller, the delay shrinks, and with it the error.

Stage 5: Evaluating the convolution

Exercise 5 ★★ Rectangle with rectangle

Compute $\operatorname{rect}(t)*\operatorname{rect}(t)$ with the recipe. Distinguish $|t|>1$, $-1\le t\le0$ and $0\le t\le1$. Which elementary signal comes out?

Solution

The flipped rectangle is again $\operatorname{rect}$ (it is symmetric); after shifting, it occupies $[t-\frac12,t+\frac12]$. The integral is the length of the overlap with $[-\frac12,\frac12]$:

  • $|t|>1$: no overlap, 0.

  • $-1\le t\le0$: overlap from $-\frac12$ to $t+\frac12$, length $1+t$.

  • $0\le t\le1$: overlap from $t-\frac12$ to $\frac12$, length $1-t$.

Together, $1-|t|$ for $|t|\le1$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\operatorname{rect}*\operatorname{rect}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\Lambda}$. Numerically: 1.000, 0.500 and 0.200 at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, 0.5 and 0.8.

Exercise 6 ★★ Checking the rectangle response

  1. Show that (5.2) is continuous at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T_0}$. Why must it be continuous?

  2. Where does $g(t)$ have its maximum, and how large is it in the reference model?

  3. Show that the area under $g(t)$ equals $aT_0$, and justify this without calculation using $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int h\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$.

Solution

(a) At $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T_0}$: $a(1-\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T})$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{a(\mathrm{e}^{T_0/T}-1)\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a(1-\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T})}$, which are equal. Physically: the capacitor voltage cannot jump, because that would require an infinite current. Mathematically: the convolution of a bounded signal with an integrable $h$ is continuous.

(b) During the pulse $g$ rises, and afterwards it falls. The maximum is at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{T_0}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(1\,\mathrm{s})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.632}$.

(c) By calculation:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int g\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a\Bigl[T_0-T\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T}\bigr)\Bigr]+a\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{T_0/T}-1\bigr)T\mathrm{e}^{-T_0/T}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{aT_0.}$$

Without calculation: the area of a convolution is the product of the areas, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int g\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int\!\!\int s(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int s\,\mathrm{d}\tau\cdot\int h\,\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{aT_0\cdot1}$. Numerically: 1.000.

Stage 6: Rules of convolution

Exercise 7 ★★ Two RC circuits in cascade

Two RC circuits with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{RC}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s are connected in cascade. Assume that the second does not load the first (for example, with a voltage follower in between).

  1. Compute the impulse response of the cascade, $h*h$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t)}$.

  2. When is it at its maximum, and what is its area?

  3. Why is the condition “does not load” needed?

Solution

(a) For $t\ge0$:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(h*h)(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_0^t\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-(t-\tau)}\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-t}\int_0^t\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,\mathrm{e}^{-t}.}$$

So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h_{\text{cascade}}(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t)}$.

(b) Derivative: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(1-t)\mathrm{e}^{-t}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s, maximum $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{e}^{-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.368}$. Area: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_0^\infty t\mathrm{e}^{-t}\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\cdot1}$ (product of the areas).

(c) Without decoupling, the second RC circuit draws current from the first capacitor. The voltage across the first capacitor is then no longer that of an unloaded RC circuit, and the cascade does not have $h*h$ as its impulse response. It remains an LTI system, but with a different impulse response. Convolving the individual responses assumes that no system acts back on its input.

Stage 8: Integrating and differentiating

Exercise 9 ★★ High-pass: a Dirac impulse in the impulse response

If $R$ and $C$ are swapped, the output voltage is taken across $R$ (CR high-pass, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{RC}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ s). Its step response is $\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t)$.

  1. Explain the step response through the mechanism: what is across $R$ at the first instant, and what after a long time?

  2. Find the impulse response by differentiation. What does the Dirac component mean physically?

  3. Check: integrating the impulse response must give back the step response.

Solution

(a) At the first instant the capacitor is empty, so there is no voltage across it. The full 1 V is across $R$. The current then charges the capacitor; its voltage rises as $1-\mathrm{e}^{-t}$, and $\mathrm{e}^{-t}$ remains across $R$. After a long time the capacitor is full, no current flows, and no voltage is left across $R$.

(b) Product rule:

$$\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t}\bigl[\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t)\bigr]}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t)+\mathrm{e}^{0}\,\delta(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\delta(t)-\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t).}$$

Here the step response jumps from 0 to 1 at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, so the Dirac component remains. Physically: an infinitely short impulse passes straight through, because the capacitor cannot build up a voltage in zero time. The charge that the impulse has put on the capacitor then flows off again through $R$, in the opposite direction: $-\mathrm{e}^{-t}$.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_{-\infty}^t\bigl[\delta(\tau)-\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\varepsilon(\tau)\bigr]\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon(t)-\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-t}\bigr)\varepsilon(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-t}\varepsilon(t)}$.

Stage 9: Causal and stable

Exercise 10 ★★ Causal? Stable?

Examine (a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-2t}\varepsilon(t)}$, (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{t}\varepsilon(t)}$, (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\varepsilon(t)-\varepsilon(t-1)}$, (d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^{-|t|}}$, (e) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{h(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\delta(t+1)}$. For each unstable system, give a bounded input that produces an unbounded output.

Solution

(a) Causal, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int|h|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac12}$: stable.

(b) Causal, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_0^\infty\mathrm{e}^t\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\infty}$: unstable. The step $\varepsilon(t)$ produces $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_0^t\mathrm{e}^\tau\mathrm{d}\tau}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{e}^t-1}$, which is unbounded.

(c) Causal, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int|h|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$: stable. The system forms the integral of the input over the last second (sliding window).

(d) Not causal, because $h(t)\neq0$ for $t<0$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int\mathrm{e}^{-|t|}\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$: stable.

(e) $\delta(t+1)$ acts at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1<0}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{g(t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s(t+1)}$; the system “sees” the input one second in advance. Not causal, but stable ($\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int|h|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$).

Stage 10: Convolution without any system

Exercise 11 ★ Convolution with dice and polynomials

  1. Compute $P(7)$ and $P(4)$ for two dice from the convolution sum. Which splittings contribute in each case?

  2. Compute $P(10)$ for three dice by convolving the distribution for two dice with the distribution for one die.

  3. Multiply out $(1+2x)(3+x)$. Show that the coefficient sequence of the result is the convolution of the sequences $(1,2)$ and $(3,1)$.

Solution

(a) $P(7)$: splittings $1+6,\,2+5,\dots,6+1$, six in all, each with $\frac16\cdot\frac16$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P(7)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{6}{36}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac16}$. $P(4)$: $1+3,\,2+2,\,3+1$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{3}{36}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{12}}$.

Solution

(a) The input consists of building blocks $\mathrm{e}^{pt}$ with weights $S(p)$. The first system multiplies each building block by $H_1(p)$, so the weights are then $H_1S$. The second multiplies by $H_2(p)$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H_2H_1S}$. So the cascade has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{H}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{H_1H_2}$. Approximation: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac13+\frac{\sqrt3}{6}\cdot\sqrt3(2t-1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac13+t-\frac12}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t-\frac16}$.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\int_0^1\bigl(t^2-t+\tfrac16\bigr)^2\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\int_0^1\bigl(t^4-2t^3+\tfrac43t^2-\tfrac13t+\tfrac1{36}\bigr)\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac15-\tfrac12+\tfrac49-\tfrac16+\tfrac1{36}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\tfrac{1}{180}}$.

(c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{b_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt2\int_0^1\cos(2\pi t)\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{b_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt6\int_0^1(2t-1)\cos(2\pi t)\mathrm{d} t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ (the cosine is symmetric about $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12}$, and $2t-1$ is antisymmetric). Both coefficients are zero: the function is orthogonal to both basis functions, the approximation is zero, and the entire signal content remains in the residual. A basis is suitable only for signals that resemble its functions.

Exercise 3 ★★ Checking d'Alembert

Show by substitution that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u(s,t)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u_\mathrm{fw}(s-vt)}$ solves the wave equation (4.1) with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/\sqrt{L'C'}}$. How is the associated current related to the voltage? Hint: first transmission-line equation with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

Solution

With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{s-vt}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial s^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u_\mathrm{fw}''(x)}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial t^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{v^2u_\mathrm{fw}''(x)}$. The wave equation requires $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u''}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L'C'v^2u''}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{L'C'}}$. Current: with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-L'\frac{\partial i}{\partial t}}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{u_\mathrm{fw}'(x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-L'\frac{\partial i}{\partial t}}$. The ansatz $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{i}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{u_\mathrm{fw}(x)/Z}$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\partial i}{\partial t}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\frac vZu'_\mathrm{fw}}$, and this is consistent because $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L'v/Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{L'\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{L'C'}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{C'}{L'}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. In the forward wave, voltage and current are everywhere in the ratio $Z$.

Exercise 4 ★★ Cable and termination

A lossless cable has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{L'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{300}$ nH/m and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{C'}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{120}$ pF/m.

  1. Compute $Z$, $v$ and the propagation delay for 5 m.

  2. With what resistance must it be terminated so that nothing is reflected? What reflection coefficient results with 100 $\Omega$?

  3. Up to what frequency can the 5 m still be treated as “short” if the line is to be at most one tenth of the wavelength long?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{300/120}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\text{nH}}{\text{pF}}}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{2.5\cdot10^3}\,\Omega}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\Omega}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{300\cdot10^{-9}\cdot120\cdot10^{-12}}}\,\mathrm{m/s}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{3.6\cdot10^{-17}}}\,\mathrm{m/s}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.67\cdot10^8}$ m/s. (b) With $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{50\,\Omega}$. With 100 $\Omega$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{r}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{100-50}{150}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac13}$.
