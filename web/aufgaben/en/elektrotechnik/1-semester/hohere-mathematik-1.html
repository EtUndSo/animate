Chapter 1: Sets, Logic and Real Numbers

Stage 1: Sets and statements

Exercise 1 ★ Calculating with sets

  1. In the reference model, calculate $(A\cup B)^c$ and $A\setminus(A\cap B)$.

  2. Show with an element table: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(A\cup B)\setminus(A\cap B)}$.

  3. How many elements does $\mathcal P(A\cap B)$ have? Predict how many $\mathcal P(M)$ has if $M$ has exactly $n$ elements, and justify it.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A\cup B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,11,12\}}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(A\cup B)^c}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{8,9,10\}}$. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A\setminus(A\cap B)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{A\setminus\{2,3\}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{1,4,6,12\}}$, which is $A\setminus B$. (b) For the cases $(\in,\in)$, $(\in,\notin)$, $(\notin,\in)$, $(\notin,\notin)$ of $x$ in $A$, $B$, both sides give $\notin,\in,\in,\notin$: $x$ lies in it if it lies in exactly one of the sets. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{A\cap B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{2,3\}}$ has 2 elements, $\mathcal P(A\cap B)$ has $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$. With $n$ elements you decide for each one individually: in or out. That makes $2^n$ subsets.

Exercise 2 ★★ Reasoning with tables

  1. Show the transitivity of implication with a table of eight rows.

  2. If the fuse is intact ($\mathcal A$), the lamp lights up ($\mathcal B$). This implication is true, and the lamp does not light up. What follows for the fuse? And if the lamp does light up?

  3. Is $(\mathcal A\Rightarrow\mathcal B)\Leftrightarrow(\mathcal B\Rightarrow\mathcal A)$ a tautology?

Solution

(a) The hypothesis $(\mathcal A\Rightarrow\mathcal B)\wedge(\mathcal B\Rightarrow\mathcal C)$ is true only for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(\mathcal A,\mathcal B,\mathcal C)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(t,t,t)}$, $(f,t,t)$, $(f,f,t)$, $(f,f,f)$. In all four, $\mathcal A\Rightarrow\mathcal C$ is true. In the remaining four rows it is false, so the overall statement is true. (b) Contraposition: $\neg\mathcal B\Rightarrow\neg\mathcal A$, the fuse is not intact. If the lamp lights up, nothing follows: The row $f\Rightarrow t$ is allowed. (c) No: For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathcal B}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{f}$ the left side is $f\Leftrightarrow t$, hence false.

Stage 2: Quantifiers and indirect proof

Exercise 3 ★★ Negating and proving indirectly

  1. Negate: “Every circuit contains a component that gets warm.”

  2. Negate $\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists n\in\mathbb{N}:\frac1n<\varepsilon$. Does the statement become true or false if the quantifiers are swapped?

  3. Show indirectly: If $n^2$ is odd, then $n$ is odd.

Solution

(a) “There is a circuit in which no component gets warm.” (b) Negation: $\exists\varepsilon>0\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}:\frac1n\ge\varepsilon$. Swapped: $\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\ \forall\varepsilon>0:\frac1n<\varepsilon$ is false, because for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varepsilon}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac1n}$ we get $\frac1n<\frac1n$. The original statement, by contrast, is true, as Stage 6 shows. (c) Assumption: $n^2$ odd and $n$ even, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2k}$. Then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\cdot2k^2}$ is even, a contradiction.

Stage 3: Field axioms

Exercise 4 ★★ Proving in a field

  1. Show: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(x+y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0\wedge x+z}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0)\Rightarrow y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{z}$. Give the axiom for each step.

  2. Show $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(x+y)(x-y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2-y^2}$ and state where (AM) and (M1) are needed.

  3. In the field $\{0,1\}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1+1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1\cdot1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$: What is $-1$? Check $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(-1)(-1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$.

Solution

(a) From $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x+y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x+z}$ it follows that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(-x)+(x+y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(-x)+(x+z)}$. With (A2), (A1), (A4) this becomes $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0+y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0+z}$, with (A1), (A3) it becomes $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{z}$. (b) (AM) and (M1): $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(x+y)(x-y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(x+y)x+(x+y)(-y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2+yx-xy-y^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{x^2-y^2}$, where $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{yx}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{xy}$ by (M1) and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(x+y)(-y)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-(x+y)y}$ by $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(-1)x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-x}$. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1+1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ means $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{-1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. Then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(-1)(-1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\cdot1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$. That is the rule from Theorem 3.2; it holds in every field.

Stage 4: Order axioms

Exercise 5 ★★ Inequalities

  1. Show $x<y\wedge z<0\Rightarrow xz>yz$ from (O4) and (a).

  2. For which real $x\ne1$ does $\frac3{x-1}>1$ hold? Distinguish two cases.

Solution

(a) From $z<0$ it follows that $-z>0$, by (O4) $-xz<-yz$, by (a) $yz<xz$. (b) Case $x>1$: Multiplying by $x-1>0$ gives $3>x-1$, so $1<x<4$. Case $x<1$: The left side is negative; no solution. Result: $1<x<4$.

Stage 5: Mathematical induction, sum and product

Exercise 6 ★★ Practicing induction

  1. Prove $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sum_{i=1}^ni^3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{n^2(n+1)^2}4}$ and check $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$.

  2. Calculate $\prod_{i=1}^5(1+\frac1i)$. Predict the formula for $\prod_{i=1}^n(1+\frac1i)$ and prove it.

  3. Find the error: “All cables have the same color. Base case: One cable has one color. Step: From $n+1$ cables take one away; the remaining $n$ have the same color. Take a different one away, likewise. So all have the same color.”

Solution

(a) Base case: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{1\cdot4}4}$. Step: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{n^2(n+1)^2}4+(n+1)^3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{(n+1)^2(n^2+4n+4)}4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{(n+1)^2(n+2)^2}4}$. Check: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1+8+27}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{36}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{9\cdot16}4}$. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\cdot\frac32\cdot\frac43\cdot\frac54\cdot\frac65}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$. Conjecture: $n+1$. Base case: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1+\frac11}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$. Step: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(n+1)(1+\frac1{n+1})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{n+2}$. (c) The step fails at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$: Of two cables, taking one away leaves a different one each time, and the remainders have no cable in common. The step must hold for every $n$.

Stage 6: The completeness axiom

Exercise 7 ★★ Determining bounds

Determine supremum, infimum, maximum and minimum in $\mathbb{R}$, where they exist, and justify: (a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{x\in\mathbb{Q}:x^2\le4\}}$, (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{\frac{(-1)^n}n:n\in\mathbb{N}\}}$, (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{x\in\mathbb{Q}:x^2<3\}}$. Which of these quantities are missing in (c) if one searches only in $\mathbb{Q}$?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{x\in\mathbb{Q}:-2\le x\le2\}}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sup}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\max}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\inf}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\min}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2}$. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{M_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\{-1,\frac12,-\frac13,\frac14,\dots\}}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sup}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\max}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\inf}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\min}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sup M_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt3}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\inf M_3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\sqrt3}$, no maximum and no minimum, because $\sqrt3\notin\mathbb{Q}$ (Exercise 8). In $\mathbb{Q}$, supremum and infimum are missing too: For every rational upper bound there is a smaller one.

Stage 7: Nested intervals and roots

Exercise 8 ★★★ The square root of 3

  1. Carry out three halving steps for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, starting with $[1,2]$. How wide is the result?

  2. Prove $\sqrt3\notin\mathbb{Q}$. Hint: A number that is not divisible by 3 has the form $3k\pm1$.

  3. At which point does the same proof fail for $\sqrt4$?

Solution

(a) Midpoint $1.5$: $2.25<3$, so $[1.5, 2]$. Midpoint $1.75$: $3.0625>3$, so $[1.5, 1.75]$. Midpoint $1.625$: $2.640625<3$, so $[1.625, 1.75]$. Width $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac18}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.125}$. (b) Assumption: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sqrt3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac pq}$, $p,q\in\mathbb{N}$, not both divisible by 3. Then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3q^2}$. If $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3k\pm1}$, then $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3(3k^2\pm2k)+1}$, not divisible by 3. So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3k}$, hence $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{q^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3k^2}$, and likewise $q$ is divisible by 3. Contradiction. (c) From $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4q^2}$ it follows that $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{p}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2k}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{k^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{q^2}$. That says nothing about the divisibility of $q$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sqrt4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac21}$.

Stage 8: Distance and absolute value

Exercise 9 ★★ Inequalities with absolute values

  1. Describe $\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|x-2|\le3\}$ as an interval.

  2. Solve $|x-2|+|x+1|\le5$ by case analysis at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2}$.

  3. Two voltages satisfy $|U_1-5\,\mathrm V|\le0.1\,\mathrm V$, $|U_2-3\,\mathrm V|\le0.2\,\mathrm V$. How far can $U_1+U_2$ deviate from 8 V at most? Give a pair that reaches this value.

Solution

(a) $-3\le x-2\le3$, so $[-1,5]$. (b) For $x\ge2$: $2x-1\le5$, so $2\le x\le3$. For $-1\le x<2$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(2-x)+(x+1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3\le5}$, always true. For $x<-1$: $1-2x\le5$, so $-2\le x<-1$. Together: $[-2,3]$. (c) $|(U_1+U_2)-8\,\mathrm V|\le|U_1-5\,\mathrm V|+|U_2-3\,\mathrm V|\le0.3\,\mathrm V$. Reached at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.1\,\mathrm V}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.2\,\mathrm V}$.

Stage 9: Elementary inequalities

Exercise 10 ★★★ Estimating

  1. Show $2^{n-1}\ge n$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$. (Stage 7 needs this inequality.)

  2. Estimate $0.995^{20}$ from below and compare with the exact value.

  3. A rectangle has area 16. Use the means to show that the perimeter is at least 16. For which sides does equality hold?

  4. Check Cauchy-Schwarz and Minkowski for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1,2,2)}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(2,-1,2)}$.

  5. Show: For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{t\,\vec x}$ with $t\in\mathbb{R}$, equality holds in (9.1).

Solution

(a) For $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1,2}$ we get $1\ge1$ and $2\ge2$. For $n\ge3$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2^{n-1}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(1+1)^{n-1}>1+(n-1)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{n}$ by Bernoulli. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-0.005}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{n}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{20}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{0.995^{20}>1-0.1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.9}$. Exactly $0.9046$; the bound lies 0.5 % below it. (c) Sides $a,b>0$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{ab}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{16}$: Perimeter $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2(a+b)\ge2\cdot2\sqrt{ab}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{16}$. Equality for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{a}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{b}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$, the square. (d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\|\vec x\|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{1+4+4}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\|\vec y\|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{4+1+4}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$. Cauchy-Schwarz: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2+2+4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8\le9}$. Minkowski: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\vec x+\vec y}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(3,1,4)}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\|\vec x+\vec y\|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{26}}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.10\le6}$. (e) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\sum_i|x_i\cdot tx_i|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|t|\sum_ix_i^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|t|\,\|\vec x\|^2}$. On the right: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\|t\vec x\|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{t^2\sum_ix_i^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|t|\,\|\vec x\|}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\|\vec x\|\,\|\vec y\|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{|t|\,\|\vec x\|^2}$.

Exercise 1 ★ Calculating in standard form

Bring to the form $x+\mathrm{i}y$: (a) $(4-\mathrm{i})(2+3\mathrm{i})$, (b) $(1+\mathrm{i})^2$ and from it $(1+\mathrm{i})^4$, (c) $\mathrm{i}^{27}+\mathrm{i}^{42}$.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{8+12\mathrm{i}-2\mathrm{i}-3\mathrm{i}^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{11+10\mathrm{i}}$. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(1+\mathrm{i})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+2\mathrm{i}+\mathrm{i}^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\mathrm{i}}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(1+\mathrm{i})^4}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(2\mathrm{i})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-4}$. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{27}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\cdot6+3}$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{i}^{27}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{i}^3}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-\mathrm{i}}$, and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{42}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\cdot10+2}$ gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{i}^{42}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm{i}^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$. The sum is $-1-\mathrm{i}$.

Stage 2: The complex plane

Exercise 2 ★★ Geometry with numbers

Given are $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+\mathrm{i}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4+2\mathrm{i}}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2+4\mathrm{i}}$. (a) Compute the fourth corner $S$ of the parallelogram with the sides $PQ$ and $PR$. Show that both diagonals have the same midpoint. (b) Rotate $Q$ about the point $P$ by $90^\circ$ counterclockwise. Hint: The arrow from $P$ to $Q$ is $Q-P$. (c) Limiting case: What does your formula from (b) give for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P}$?

Solution

(a) From $P$ start the arrows $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q-P}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3+\mathrm{i}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R-P}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+3\mathrm{i}}$. The fourth corner is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{S}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P+(Q-P)+(R-P)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q+R-P}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5+5\mathrm{i}}$. The midpoints of the diagonals are $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(P+S)/2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(6+6\mathrm{i})/2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3+3\mathrm{i}}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{(Q+R)/2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(6+6\mathrm{i})/2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3+3\mathrm{i}}$.

(b) You rotate the arrow $Q-P$ and attach it at $P$ again: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P+\mathrm{i}(Q-P)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+\mathrm{i}+\mathrm{i}(3+\mathrm{i})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1+\mathrm{i}-1+3\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4\mathrm{i}}$. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P+\mathrm{i}\cdot0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{P}$: The center of rotation stays where it is.
