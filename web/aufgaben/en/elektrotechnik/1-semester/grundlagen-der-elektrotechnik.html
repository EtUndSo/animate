Stage 1: Quantities, force and work

Exercise 1 ★ Units and prefixes

Calculate and give the result in a suitable unit: (a) $3\,\mathrm{kN}+450\,\mathrm{N}$, (b) $2\,\mathrm{mA}\cdot4\,\mathrm{ks}$, (c) $6\,\mu\text{C}/3\,\mathrm{ms}$, (d) 1 kWh in joules, (e) 1.5 mm$^2$ in m$^2$, (f) the unit V/m in base units.

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{3000\,\mathrm{N}+450\,\mathrm{N}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.45\,\mathrm{kN}}$. (b) Milli and kilo cancel, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{A\cdot s}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm C}$: 8 C. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mu\text{}/\mathrm m}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm m}$ (milli) and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\mathrm{C/s}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\mathrm A}$: 2 mA. (d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1000\,\mathrm{W}\cdot3600\,\mathrm{s}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.6\,\mathrm{MJ}}$. (e) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1\,\mathrm{mm^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}}$, so $1.5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. (f) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\frac{\mathrm V}{\mathrm m}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\mathrm{kg\,m^2}}{\mathrm{A\,s^3}\cdot\mathrm m}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac{\mathrm{kg\,m}}{\mathrm{A\,s^3}}}$.

Stage 2: Charge

Exercise 2 ★ Counting charges

  1. After rubbing, a glass rod carries $+8$ nC. How many electrons has it given up? What charge does the cloth carry if both were neutral beforehand?

  2. Two identical metal spheres carry $+8$ nC and $-2$ nC. They touch and are separated. What charge does each carry? How many electrons have moved across, and in which direction?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{N}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{8\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}/(1.602\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C})}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.99\cdot10^{10}}$ electrons. The cloth has taken them up and carries $-8$ nC. (b) The total charge $+6$ nC is conserved and divides evenly between identical spheres: $+3$ nC each. The negative sphere therefore gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{5\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}/e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.12\cdot10^{10}}$ electrons to the positive one.

Stage 3: The force between charges

Exercise 3 ★★ Coulomb force

Two small metal spheres carry $+2$ nC and $+5$ nC at a distance of 2 cm.

  1. How large is the force, and where does it point on each sphere?

  2. What results at three times the distance? Answer first without a calculator.

  3. The equal-sized spheres briefly touch and are brought back to 2 cm. What force acts now? Why has it not stayed the same, even though the total charge has?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k\cdot2\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot5\,\mathrm{nC}/(2\,\mathrm{cm})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{225}$ µ N. Like charges: each sphere is pushed away from the other. (b) Three times the distance gives one ninth: 25 µ N.

(c) Each sphere now carries $+3.5$ nC, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k\,(3.5\,\mathrm{nC})^2/(2\,\mathrm{cm})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{275}$ µ N. The force depends on the product of the charges, not on their sum: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{3.5\cdot3.5}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12.25}$ versus $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\cdot5}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$.

Stage 4: The field strength

Exercise 4 ★★ Two charges, one field-free point

On the $x$-axis, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{+4}$ nC is at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{+1}$ nC at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ cm.

  1. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the field at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ cm. What force does an electron experience there?

  2. Where between the charges is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$?

  3. Limiting case: Where does this point move for $Q_2\to0$? Does this fit the field of a single charge?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k\cdot4\,\mathrm{nC}/(3\,\mathrm{cm})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{40.0}$ kV/m to the right, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k\cdot1\,\mathrm{nC}/(3\,\mathrm{cm})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9.99}$ kV/m to the left, together $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{30.0}$ kV/m to the right. Electron: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{F}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{e\,E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.8\cdot10^{-15}}$ N to the left.

(b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ requires $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_1/x^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{Q_2/(a-x)^2}$ with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{a}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ cm. From this $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2/x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/(a-x)}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac23a}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ cm. (c) In general, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{a/(1+\sqrt{Q_2/Q_1})}$. For $Q_2\to0$, $x\to a$: the point moves to the location of $Q_2$ and vanishes: a single charge has no field-free point.

Stage 5: Field lines and conductors

Exercise 5 ★ Predicting shielding

First answer with field lines alone, without calculating.

  1. A metal box with no charge of its own stands in the field of a charged rod. How large is the field in the box? Where are the induced charges?

  2. A charge $+Q$ is placed at the center of an isolated, neutral hollow metal sphere. Is there a field in the cavity? Is there a field outside?

Solution

(a) In the box, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$. The induced charges sit on the outside: facing the rod with the opposite sign, on the far side with the same sign. (b) There is a field in the cavity, because lines start from the charge. They end on the inner wall, which acquires $-Q$ by electrostatic induction. The sphere is neutral, so $+Q$ sits on the outside, and outside there is the field of a charge $+Q$. Shielding protects only the interior from external fields.

Exercise 6 ★★ Paths in the field

In the uniform field $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ kV/m in the $x$-direction, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{+2}$ nC moves from $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(0;\,0)}$ to $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{P_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{(2\,\mathrm{mm};\,1.5\,\mathrm{mm})}$.

  1. Calculate $W_{12}$ on the straight path.

  2. Calculate $W_{12}$ on the detour first in the $y$-direction, then in the $x$-direction.

  3. What changes for $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-2}$ nC?

Solution

(a) Length $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{s}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\sqrt{2^2+1.5^2}\,\mathrm{mm}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.5}$ mm, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\cos\alpha}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.8}$: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot3000\,\mathrm{V/m}\cdot2.5\,\mathrm{mm}\cdot0.8}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ nJ. (b) In the $y$-direction the path is perpendicular to $\vec E$: 0. In the $x$-direction: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot3000\,\mathrm{V/m}\cdot2\,\mathrm{mm}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{12}$ nJ. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-12}$ nJ; 12 nJ must be supplied from outside.

Stage 7: The potential

Exercise 7 ★★ Separating potential and field

On the $x$-axis, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{+4}$ nC is at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{Q_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{-1}$ nC at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{6}$ cm; the reference point is at infinity.

  1. Calculate the potential at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ cm. Why do you add here without directions?

  2. Where between the charges is $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$? How large is $E$ there?

Solution

(a) Potentials are numbers; they add with their signs: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k\,(4\,\mathrm{nC}-1\,\mathrm{nC})/3\,\mathrm{cm}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{899}$ V. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{4/x-1/(6-x)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ with $x$ in cm gives $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.8}$ cm. There, both fields point to the right: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{k\cdot4\,\mathrm{nC}/(4.8\,\mathrm{cm})^2+k\cdot1\,\mathrm{nC}/(1.2\,\mathrm{cm})^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{15.6\,\mathrm{kV/m}+62.4\,\mathrm{kV/m}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{78.0}$ kV/m. So $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$ does not mean $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{E}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$.

A 12 V source feeds three resistors in series. The connection points (nodes) are called 1 (plus terminal), 2, 3 and 4 (minus terminal). $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_{12}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_{23}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4}$ V, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_{34}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3}$ V.

Stage 9: What voltage does to a charge

Exercise 10 ★★★ Electron against the field

An electron leaves plate A of the reference model with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.0\cdot10^6}$ m/s toward plate B.

  1. Does it reach plate B? Compare its kinetic energy with the energy needed.

  2. At which location $x$ does it turn around? Calculate once with the energy theorem and once with (1.4).

  3. When is it back at plate A, and with what speed?

  4. What initial speed would it need to just reach B?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{W_\mathrm{kin}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{\frac12m_\mathrm{e}v_0^2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.55\cdot10^{-19}}$ J. Reaching B would take $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{e\cdot9\,\mathrm{V}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.44\cdot10^{-18}}$ J. It does not reach B. (b) Energy theorem: it travels until $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\Delta\varphi}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{W_\mathrm{kin}/e}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2.84}$ V are used up. At 3 V/mm, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.948}$ mm. With (1.4): deceleration $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{a}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5.28\cdot10^{14}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}$, turnaround at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{v_0/a}$, so $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{x}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{v_0^2/(2a)}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.948}$ mm.

(c) There and back: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{t}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{2v_0/a}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{3.79}$ ns. The potential is the same again, so by (9.1) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{v_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.0\cdot10^6}$ m/s, in the opposite direction. (d) Exactly the energy from the example: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{v_0}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.78\cdot10^6}$ m/s.

The cable from Exercise 1 is made of copper and is 30 m long.

Stage 3: Ohm's law from field and collisions

Exercise 3 ★★ Resistance from geometry

A supply line consists of an outgoing and a return conductor, each 20 m of copper with a cross section of $1.5\,\mathrm{mm^2}$.

  1. Calculate its resistance and the voltage that drops across it at 16 A.

  2. What cross section would aluminum need for the same resistance?

  3. Prediction: A wire is drawn to twice its length while its volume stays the same. By what factor does $R$ change?

Solution

Exercise 9 ★★★ Compensating a current source

A NIC with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ k$\Omega$ and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.7}$ k$\Omega$ is in parallel with a source with 2 mA and $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{q}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{4.7}$ k$\Omega$. The load is 1 k$\Omega$. (a) What current does the load get without the NIC, and what with it? (b) Where is the op amp output? (c) Up to what load resistance does the output's 11 V suffice? (d) Why is the circuit stable with a load but not without one?

Solution

(a) Without the NIC $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{2\,\mathrm{mA}\cdot4.7/5.7}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1.65}$ mA, with the NIC 2 mA. Without a load, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_\mathrm{i}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{R_2}$, and the two feedback paths cancel each other.

Exercise 11 ★★★ Measuring amplifier

A sensor with 10 mV is to be amplified to about 1 V using V: $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_1}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1}$ k$\Omega$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{100}$ k$\Omega$, non-inverting. (a) How large are $\beta$, $G$ and the deviation due to $A$? (b) Up to what frequency does the circuit reach? How large is $|G|$ at 5 kHz? (c) How large is the offset at the output, and what share of the signal is it? How much does $I_\mathrm{B}$ add? (d) Up to what input does the output reach? (e) An integrator with $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{R\,C}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{10}$ ms has the same offset. How fast does its output run at $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{U_\mathrm{E}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0}$, and when does it hit its limit?

Solution

(a) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{\beta}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1/101}$, $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{G}\textcolor{#15633a}{\approx}\textcolor{#c2410c}{101}$, deviation $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{101/A}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.10}$ %. (b) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{f_G}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{1\,\mathrm{MHz}/101}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{9.9}$ kHz. $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{|G(5\,\mathrm{kHz})|}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{101/\sqrt{1+(5/9.9)^2}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{90.2}$. Such a circuit already loses a tenth at 5 kHz. (c) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{101\cdot1\,\mathrm{mV}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{101}$ mV, that is, 10 % of the signal of 1.01 V. In addition $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{I_\mathrm{B}R_2}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{5}$ mV. (d) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{11\,\mathrm{V}/101}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{109}$ mV. (e) $\textcolor{#1b7f4a}{1\,\mathrm{mV}/10\,\mathrm{ms}}\textcolor{#15633a}{=}\textcolor{#c2410c}{0.1}$ V/s, at the limit after 110 s. A real integrator therefore needs a reset switch or a large resistor in parallel with $C$.
